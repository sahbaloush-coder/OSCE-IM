# Miscellaneous

## How to use this

The miscellaneous set is mostly communication stations (≈ 50 marks for the framework + 50 for examiner questions). Six of its stations are the same as the General & Communication file, so only the new ones are written out in full here; the frameworks are repeated in short form so this tab stands alone.

- **Stations covered:** angry patient — missed DVT prophylaxis ×2; angry patient — warfarin given by mistake; pre-op cardiac assessment for cholecystectomy after DES; plus the shared stations (breaking bad news for lung and colon cancer, anaphylaxis after amoxicillin and ibuprofen, biologic counseling in RA, cyclophosphamide counseling in lupus nephritis).

## 1. Communication frameworks (short form)

- **Angry patient:** ABC first if unwell → introduce, confirm identity, purpose, consent → safety (door open, sit near the door, not too close) → find the reason, let him vent, acknowledge empathically, listen without interrupting → apologize, accept the grievance, never blame a colleague or department, not defensive → plan for the current problem, transparent investigation to prevent recurrence, explain the formal complaint route, reassure care won't be affected → follow-up, contact route, resources → check understanding, questions, thank.
- **Breaking bad news (SPIKES):** private setting, phone off, relative? → perception (ideas, concerns, expectations) → invitation (how much to know) → knowledge (warning shot, why the test, result in plain words) → emotions (silence, acknowledge) → strategy (MDT plan, psychosocial support, follow-up) → safety net (help getting home, contact number, leaflets) → check understanding, summarize, thank.
- **Medication counseling:** introduce, consent, private, relative? → explore knowledge, ICE, disease course, adherence, comorbidities → explain diagnosis, drug name, route, regimen, alternatives → side effects and how they are reduced → when to stop and call → contacts, leaflets → check understanding, consent, thank.

## 2. Case scenarios

### Angry patient after a medical error

#### 1. Missed DVT prophylaxis (70M bedbound with pneumonia, new right leg swelling day 3)

- **Task:** angry patient framework.
- **Leg swelling DDx (8):** infection (cellulitis, abscess); lymphedema; vascular (DVT, superficial thrombophlebitis, hematoma, venous or arterial insufficiency, post-thrombotic syndrome); inflammatory (myositis, myonecrosis, rhabdomyolysis); trauma (compartment syndrome, sport injury); joint (arthritis, ruptured Baker cyst); neurogenic (neuropathy); tumor (lipoma, hemangioma, nerve sheath tumor).
- **Result:** Doppler shows right leg DVT.
- **Thrombophilia workup indications:** recurrent VTE; VTE at a young age (< 45–50); strong family history of VTE; thrombosis at unusual sites (cerebral, splanchnic, hepatic veins); also recurrent pregnancy loss, warfarin skin necrosis.
- **Anticoagulate superficial vein thrombosis when:** ≥ 5 cm long (extensive); within 3–5 cm of the deep system (saphenofemoral / saphenopopliteal junction); plus positive D-dimer, active cancer, prior VTE, above the knee, symptoms progressing (fondaparinux 2.5 mg for 45 days).
- **DVT + brain metastasis:** anticoagulation is generally safe (except hemorrhagic metastases — melanoma, RCC, choriocarcinoma, thyroid); choice LMWH (enoxaparin) — DOACs acceptable in many cancers; duration indefinite while the cancer is active / until cured (at least 3–6 months).

#### 2. Missed DVT prophylaxis — repeat station

- **Same stem and questions as case 1.**
- **Extra question:** is it safe to anticoagulate with brain metastasis? → yes; enoxaparin; indefinite.

#### 3. Warfarin given by mistake for 2 days (55M admitted with pneumonia, gum bleeding)

- **Task:** angry patient framework.
- **Bleeding gum DDx (8):** gingivitis / periodontitis; hard brushing / trauma; dental infection; leukemia; coagulation defects (hemophilia, liver disease, DIC); platelet disorders (ITP, TTP, HUS); vitamin C (scurvy) or vitamin K deficiency; drugs (anticoagulants, antiplatelets); pregnancy.
- **Vitamin K or 4-factor PCC?** No — no significant bleeding (minor gum bleeding); stop warfarin and monitor. Vitamin K (oral) if INR > 10 without bleeding; IV vitamin K + 4F-PCC for major bleeding.
- **Recheck INR:** in 24–48 hours.
- **PCC complications:** thrombosis (≈ 1.4%), heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (contains heparin), allergic reactions, infection risk (theoretical).
- **PCC over FFP:** faster INR reversal, small volume (less overload), no need for ABO / thawing, lower TRALI and transfusion-reaction risk.
- **Drugs increasing warfarin effect:** amiodarone, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, cotrimoxazole (also metronidazole, fluconazole).
- **Drugs decreasing warfarin effect:** rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, OCPs (also vitamin K-rich food).

### Pre-operative assessment

#### 4. Cholecystectomy after recent STEMI with DES (64M, DM, HTN)

- **History:** timing of MI; PCI with stent number, site, type (BMS vs DES); current cardiac symptoms (chest pain, dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, palpitations, syncope); comorbidity (DM, HTN, smoking, lipids, PVD, stroke, CKD); drugs (ASA, clopidogrel, beta blocker, CCB, insulin); functional capacity.
- **Exam:** vitals; JVP and leg edema; precordium for gallop and murmurs; chest for crackles.
- **Findings:** STEMI 5 months ago with one DES in the LAD; asymptomatic; DM on insulin; ex-smoker; on ASA, clopidogrel, bisoprolol, lisinopril, atorvastatin, insulin; climbs 2 flights and jogs 30 min (≥ 4 METs); exam normal.
- **Risk tool:** Revised Cardiac Risk Index (Lee).
- **RCRI items:** high-risk surgery (intraperitoneal, intrathoracic, suprainguinal vascular); IHD; HF; cerebrovascular disease; DM on insulin; creatinine > 2 mg/dL (177 µmol/L).
- **His risk:** high (IHD + insulin-treated DM + intraperitoneal surgery).
- **Pre-op tests:** cardiac (ECG; echo if none within a year); imaging (CXR); CBC; coagulation (PT / INR, aPTT); renal profile (creatinine — RCRI cut-off > 2 mg/dL = 177 µmol/L); glucose / HbA1c.
- **Results:** ECG Q waves V1–V2; echo anteroseptal hypokinesia, EF 55%, normal PA pressure.
- **Anticipated complications:** stent thrombosis, ACS, death, stroke, pulmonary edema, arrhythmia.
- **Recommendation:** elective surgery → postpone until 12 months after DES (minimum 6 months per newer guidance) on DAPT.
- **When surgery goes ahead:** monitored post-op bed; cardiology consult; stop clopidogrel 5 days before; continue aspirin; continue beta blocker and statin (hold ACEi the morning of surgery per most guidance); avoid volume overload; restart P2Y12 as soon as bleeding risk allows.
- **Post-op monitoring:** daily ECG; troponin (± NT-proBNP / BNP) on days 1–3.

### Shared with General & Communication (see that tab for detail)

#### 5. Breaking bad news — lung cancer (73M heavy smoker)

- **Task:** SPIKES.
- **Examiner Qs:** lung nodule DDx (malignant, benign tumor, infection, inflammatory, congenital, vascular); 8 complications of lung cancer (SVC syndrome, Pancoast / Horner, hoarseness, effusion, post-obstructive pneumonia, metastases, thrombosis, paraneoplastic, death); solitary nodule features (single, < 3 cm, surrounded by lung, no nodes, no effusion).

#### 6. Breaking bad news — colon cancer (50M, rectal bleeding, weight loss)

- **Task:** SPIKES.
- **Examiner Qs:** risk factors (age > 45, IBD, family / personal CRC or polyps, low fiber / red meat, inactivity, DM, obesity, male, alcohol, smoking); Saudi high-risk group (polyps, ≥ 1 first-degree or ≥ 2 second-degree relatives, IBD, prior abdominal / pelvic radiation); prognostic factors (stage, nodes, grade, metastases, KRAS / BRAF vs MSI-high, performance status, treatment response).

#### 7. Anaphylaxis — amoxicillin despite penicillin allergy (62M, leg cellulitis)

- **Task:** ABC first, then angry patient framework.
- **Examiner Qs:** 5 leg-swelling DDx; diagnosis = anaphylaxis.
- **Management:** ABC; IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (1:1000), repeat every 5 min; O2, salbutamol, intubation / cricothyrotomy for laryngeal edema; large-volume IV fluids; IV antihistamine; steroid against biphasic reaction; monitored bed / ICU ± vasopressors; glucagon if on beta blockers; allergy referral; education and adrenaline auto-injector.

#### 8. Anaphylaxis — ibuprofen despite aspirin allergy (60M, knee OA)

- **Task:** ABC first, then angry patient framework.
- **Flushing DDx:** emotional / menopause / autonomic, carcinoid, pheochromocytoma, mastocytosis, anaphylaxis, medullary thyroid cancer, serotonin syndrome, VIPoma, rosacea, drugs (CCB, vancomycin, steroids, niacin), alcohol, spicy food.
- **Diagnosis:** anaphylactic (distributive) shock; same management as case 7.

#### 9. Biologic therapy counseling — RA on methotrexate (26F)

- **Explore:** disease duration and activity, extra-articular features; doses, adherence, reasons MTX failed (smoking, interactions, fibromyalgia, OSA); comorbidities; infection review (recent infection, TB, shingles, HIV / hepatitis risk factors); HF, lung disease, demyelination, thrombosis, malignancy; vaccines (flu, pneumococcal, zoster before starting); pregnancy / breastfeeding.
- **Educate:** side effects (allergy, infection, TB reactivation) with leaflet; dose, duration, technique; questions and consent.
- **Pre-biologic tests:** CBC (with differential); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); liver profile; lipid profile (tocilizumab, JAKi); urinalysis; infection screen (HBV — HBsAg, anti-HBc; HCV; HIV; latent TB — TST or QuantiFERON); imaging (CXR).
- **Approved:** TNFi (infliximab, etanercept, adalimumab, certolizumab, golimumab); IL-6 (tocilizumab, sarilumab); rituximab; abatacept; JAKi (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib).

#### 10. Cyclophosphamide counseling — lupus nephritis class III with crescents (30F)

- **Regimens:** Euro-Lupus 500 mg IV every 2 weeks × 6; NIH 0.5–1 g/m² IV monthly × 6.
- **Side effects:** infertility / premature ovarian failure (higher > 30 years), marrow suppression and infection, hemorrhagic cystitis and bladder cancer, teratogenic, SIADH, hypotension, nausea, alopecia.
- **Risk reduction:** GnRH agonist (leuprolide 3.75 mg IM 10–14 days before), IV hydration, MESNA (25% of the dose before and after), ondansetron + dexamethasone, TMP-SMX PCP prophylaxis 3× / week, contraception.
- **Stop and call:** rash / allergy, infection, severe vomiting, jaundice, hematuria, planning pregnancy.
- **High dose preferred:** nephritic sediment with GFR 25–80, or crescents / necrosis in > 25% of glomeruli.
- **Induction:** CYC or MMF; **maintenance:** MMF or azathioprine; **activity / relapse antibody:** anti-C1q.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Error disclosure essentials:** acknowledge, apologize, explain what happened and what you are doing about it, incident report, transparent review, complaint route, continuity of care.
- **Warfarin reversal by INR:** INR above range < 10, no bleeding → hold doses; INR > 10, no bleeding → hold + oral vitamin K 2.5–5 mg; major bleeding at any INR → IV vitamin K 5–10 mg + 4F-PCC (FFP if PCC unavailable).
- **Cancer-associated VTE:** LMWH or DOAC (avoid DOAC in GI / GU luminal cancers with bleeding risk); continue while cancer is active.
