# Data Interpretation

## How to use this

Short data-interpretation stations from the KFSHRC residency sets (PFT, peripheral smear, urinalysis). Each specialty tab also has its own "Data interpretation" section at the end with the images from its stations (ECGs, X-rays, CTs, smears, skin photos).

- **Approach to any data station:** describe what you see first (pattern, key abnormal values with units); then give the diagnosis; then 2–3 differentials; then the next test and management.
- **Sets covered:** PFT (obstructive, restrictive with normal DLCO); peripheral smear (schistocytes, teardrop cells); urinalysis (WBC cast, calcium oxalate, WBCs, squamous cells, uric acid, eosinophiluria).

## 1. Pulmonary function tests

### How to read a PFT

- **Step 1 — spirometry:** FEV1/FVC < 0.70 (or < LLN; rough LLN = predicted − 8) = obstruction; FEV1/FVC normal or high with low FVC = possible restriction.
- **Step 2 — reversibility:** post-bronchodilator FEV1 or FVC rise ≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL = significant (asthma).
- **Step 3 — lung volumes:** TLC < 80% predicted confirms restriction; RV / TLC high = air trapping.
- **Step 4 — DLCO:** low with restriction = parenchymal (ILD); normal with restriction = extraparenchymal (chest wall, pleura, neuromuscular, obesity); low with obstruction = emphysema; isolated low DLCO = pulmonary vascular disease or anemia; high DLCO = hemorrhage, polycythemia, asthma.
- **Severity (FEV1 % predicted):** mild ≥ 70%; moderate 60–69%; moderately severe 50–59%; severe 35–49%; very severe < 35%.

### Case 1 — obstruction with marked reversibility (54M non-smoker, dyspnea and cough)

- **Data:** FVC 3.19 L (76%) → 4.00 L after BD (+25%); FEV1 2.18 L (64%) → 2.83 L after BD (+30%, +650 mL); FEV1/FVC 68% (LLN ≈ 72) → 71%.

![Flow-volume loop, pre- and post-bronchodilator](DI_pft1)

- **Flow-volume loop:** scooped (concave) expiratory limb below the predicted points; the post-bronchodilator loop is larger.
- **Pattern:** FVC and FEV1 below 80% with FEV1/FVC 0.68 = airflow obstruction.
- **Severity:** FEV1 64% = moderate obstruction.
- **Reversibility:** +30% / +650 mL = significant (≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL) → favors asthma.
- **Differential:** asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis.

### Case 2 — restriction with normal DLCO (36F non-smoker, exertional dyspnea)

- **Data:** FVC 0.88 L (26%); FEV1 0.87 L (30%); FEV1/FVC 99% (predicted 86); RV 1.61 L (115%); TLC 1.61 L (53%); RV / TLC 65% (predicted 29); DLCO 20 (75% — normal).
- **Pattern:** low FEV1 and FVC with preserved ratio, TLC 53% = moderate restrictive defect.
- **DLCO:** normal → alveolar-capillary interface intact → extraparenchymal cause; high RV / TLC suggests poor expiratory muscle effort.
- **Differential:** chest wall disorders (kyphoscoliosis, obesity); pleural disease; neuromuscular disease (myasthenia, ALS, diaphragm palsy, myopathy).
- **Next:** MIP / MEP, supine vs upright FVC (diaphragm), CK, AChR antibodies, chest imaging.

## 2. Peripheral blood smear

### Case 1 — schistocytes (33F, fever and bruising)

![Peripheral smear with fragmented red cells](DI_sm1)

- **Findings:** schistocytes (helmet and triangular fragmented red cells), few platelets.
- **Diagnosis:** microangiopathic hemolytic anemia → TTP / HUS.
- **Investigations:** ADAMTS13 activity; hemolysis panel (LDH, haptoglobin, indirect bilirubin, retics, DAT); CBC with differential; creatinine; liver profile; coagulation (normal in TTP, abnormal in DIC).
- **Management:** urgent plasma exchange; glucocorticoids; rituximab; caplacizumab.

### Case 2 — teardrop cells (44M, fatigue, massive splenomegaly)

- **Data:** Hb 9 g/dL (90 g/L), normocytic normochromic; WBC 4 ×10⁹/L; platelets 100 ×10⁹/L; early satiety.

![Peripheral smear with teardrop cells](DI_sm2)

- **Findings:** teardrop cells (dacrocytes); may come with a leukoerythroblastic picture (nucleated red cells + immature myeloid cells).
- **Diagnosis:** primary myelofibrosis (marrow fibrosis → extramedullary hematopoiesis).
- **Next:** JAK2 / CALR / MPL mutations, bone marrow biopsy (dry tap, reticulin fibrosis).
- **Complications:** worsening anemia and thrombocytopenia; massive splenomegaly, splenic infarction; portal hypertension; pulmonary hypertension; pleural effusion, ascites (extramedullary hematopoiesis); liver failure; bone pain; marrow failure; transformation to acute leukemia.

## 3. Urinalysis — urine microscopy

### Image 1 — WBC cast

![WBC cast](DI_ua1)

- **Findings:** cylindrical cast packed with white cells (lobulated nuclei).
- **Meaning:** renal parenchymal inflammation → pyelonephritis, acute interstitial nephritis; also proliferative GN.

### Image 2 — calcium oxalate crystals

![Calcium oxalate crystals](DI_ua2)

- **Findings:** envelope-shaped (bipyramidal) dihydrate crystals (arrowhead) and dumbbell / oval monohydrate crystals (arrow).
- **Meaning:** most common stone type; monohydrate needles / dumbbells in large numbers → ethylene glycol poisoning (with high anion and osmolar gap); also hyperoxaluria (enteric — Crohn's, gastric bypass).

### Image 3 — white blood cells

![White blood cells](DI_ua3)

- **Findings:** many round granular cells with lobulated nuclei, larger than RBCs.
- **Meaning:** pyuria → UTI; sterile pyuria → TB, interstitial nephritis, partially treated UTI, stones, chlamydia.

### Image 4 — squamous epithelial cells

![Squamous epithelial cells](DI_ua4)

- **Findings:** large flat polygonal cells with small central nuclei.
- **Meaning:** contamination from the urethra / vagina → poorly collected sample; repeat a clean-catch midstream specimen.

### Image 5 — uric acid crystals

![Uric acid crystals under polarized light](DI_ua5)

- **Findings:** rhomboid / plate-like crystals, birefringent (multicolored) under polarized light (inset).
- **Meaning:** acidic urine; gout, tumor lysis syndrome (acute uric acid nephropathy), uric acid stones (radiolucent).

### Image 6 — eosinophiluria (Hansel stain)

![Eosinophils in urine, Hansel stain](DI_ua6)

- **Findings:** cells with bilobed nuclei and bright red-orange granules on Hansel stain.
- **Meaning:** acute interstitial nephritis (drugs — β-lactams, PPIs, NSAIDs); also atheroembolic disease, pyelonephritis, prostatitis (low specificity).

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Casts:** hyaline = normal / concentrated urine; RBC = glomerulonephritis; WBC = pyelonephritis / AIN; muddy brown granular = ATN; waxy / broad = advanced CKD; fatty (oval fat bodies, Maltese cross) = nephrotic syndrome.
- **Crystals:** calcium oxalate (envelope / dumbbell); uric acid (rhomboid, acid urine); struvite (coffin lid, Proteus, alkaline urine); cystine (hexagonal, cystinuria); calcium phosphate (alkaline urine).
- **Smear clues:** schistocytes = MAHA; spherocytes = AIHA / hereditary spherocytosis; bite / blister cells = G6PD; teardrop = myelofibrosis; target cells = thalassemia, liver disease; sickle cells = SCD; smudge cells = CLL; Auer rods = AML; hypersegmented neutrophils = B12 / folate deficiency.
