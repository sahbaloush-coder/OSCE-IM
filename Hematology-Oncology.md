# Hematology & Oncology

## How to use this

The 17 hematology stations repeat a small set of patterns: low platelets (ITP vs TMA), hemolysis (AIHA vs G6PD), high counts (PV, CML), malignancy (AML, DLBCL), sickle cell, and transfusion / anticoagulation problems (often wrapped in an angry-patient station). Nearly every station asks for CBC, peripheral film, hemolysis panel and coagulation first.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; in bleeding or confused patients secure ABC and vitals first.
- **Stations covered:** thrombocytopenia (ITP ×2, TTP ×3, aHUS); hemolysis (warm AIHA ×2 incl. CLL, G6PD); myeloproliferative (PV, CML); malignancy (AML, DLBCL); sickle cell (VOC, TRALI + pregnancy counseling); angry patient (missed DVT prophylaxis ×2, warfarin given in error).

## 1. Core hematology history (every case)

- **Anemia symptoms:** fatigue, SOB, palpitations, chest pain, dizziness, syncope.
- **Bleeding:** site (skin, gums, nose, GI, urine, menses), onset, spontaneous vs after trauma, platelet-type (petechiae, mucosal) vs factor-type (joints, muscles, delayed); previous bleeding with surgery / dental work.
- **Hemolysis:** jaundice, dark urine, previous episodes, gallstones, transfusions.
- **Infection:** fever, recurrent infections, sick contacts, travel.
- **B symptoms:** fever > 38 °C for > 2 weeks, drenching night sweats, weight loss > 10% in 6 months; pruritus (after hot bath = PV), bone pain.
- **Lumps and organs:** lymph nodes, abdominal fullness / early satiety (splenomegaly).
- **Thrombosis:** DVT, PE, stroke, unusual sites (portal, hepatic, cerebral veins), recurrent miscarriage.
- **Neuro:** headache, confusion, seizures, focal weakness, visual change.
- **Renal:** oliguria, hematuria, bloody diarrhea (HUS).
- **Autoimmune:** joint pain, rash, mouth ulcers, hair loss (SLE / APS).
- **Women:** menstrual loss, pregnancy, postpartum, preeclampsia.
- **Drugs:** anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs, herbals; quinine, sulfa, penicillin, heparin (immune cytopenias); clopidogrel (TTP); oxidant drugs (G6PD); chemo / radiation; recent vaccination or transfusion.
- **Past history:** liver disease, CKD, malignancy, transplant, bariatric surgery (copper, B12, iron deficiency).
- **Family and social:** bleeding disorders, hemoglobinopathies, malignancy; smoking, alcohol, occupational chemicals / radiation.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Thrombocytopenia / petechiae

- **ITP:** isolated bruising and gum bleeding; recent viral illness or vaccination; drugs (aspirin worsens bleeding); no systemic features.
- **TMA (TTP / HUS):** fever, confusion, headache, seizures, chest pain, oliguria, bloody diarrhea (STEC-HUS), pregnancy / postpartum, HIV, drugs (quinine, clopidogrel, chemo), SLE / APS.
- **Other causes:** HIV, HCV, H. pylori, EBV, malaria; liver disease (hypersplenism); MDS; SLE; APS; heparin exposure (HIT); post-transfusion purpura; DIC (sepsis).

### B. Hemolytic anemia (jaundice + anemia)

- **Immune (AIHA):** autoimmune disease, lymphoma / CLL (nodes, B symptoms), drugs (penicillin, quinine), infections (EBV, CMV, mycoplasma).
- **G6PD:** young male, previous similar episodes with transfusion, trigger drug (sulfa, dapsone, primaquine, rasburicase, nitrofurantoin) or fava beans or infection; family history.
- **Others:** sickle cell / thalassemia (ethnicity, family), TMA, malaria, prosthetic valve.

### C. Polycythemia / thrombocytosis / splenomegaly

- **Hyperviscosity:** headache, blurred / transient loss of vision, dizziness, paresthesia.
- **MPN features:** aquagenic pruritus, erythromelalgia (burning red extremities), unusual-site thrombosis (portal vein), gout, abdominal fullness, weight loss, sweats.
- **Secondary causes:** smoking / shisha, COPD, OSA, cyanotic heart disease, high altitude, CO exposure, testosterone / anabolic steroids / EPO, renal or liver tumors.

### D. Lymphadenopathy

- **Nodes:** duration, painless vs painful, localized vs generalized, growth.
- **Malignancy:** B symptoms, pruritus, alcohol-induced node pain (Hodgkin).
- **Infection / exposure:** TB, brucella (unpasteurized milk), cat scratch, toxoplasma (undercooked meat), HIV / EBV / CMV, travel, sexual history.
- **Other:** SLE, Still, sarcoid; drugs (phenytoin, allopurinol).

### E. Pancytopenia / acute leukemia

- **Marrow failure triad:** anemia symptoms, bleeding (epistaxis, gums), recurrent infections.
- **Infiltration:** bone pain, gum hypertrophy, skin lesions, CNS symptoms, mediastinal mass symptoms.
- **Risk factors:** prior chemo / radiotherapy, benzene, MDS, bariatric surgery (copper / B12).

### F. Sickle cell crisis

- **Pain:** site, severity, usual crisis pattern, home analgesia and last dose, opioid tolerance.
- **Triggers:** cold, dehydration, infection, exertion, travel, alcohol, stress.
- **Red flags:** fever (infection / osteomyelitis), chest pain / SOB (acute chest syndrome), neuro symptoms (stroke), priapism, abdominal pain / falling Hb (sequestration).
- **Baseline:** hydroxyurea adherence, other drugs (L-glutamine, crizanlizumab, voxelotor), transfusions and chelation, previous complications (ACS, stroke, AVN, leg ulcers), vaccines.

### G. DVT / anticoagulation problems

- **DVT:** immobility, missed prophylaxis, surgery, cancer, pregnancy / OCP, previous VTE, family history.
- **Over-anticoagulation:** bleeding sites, interacting drugs and diet.

## 3. Core hematology exam (every case)

- **Vitals:** temperature, HR, BP (high in aHUS / PV), RR, SpO2, mental status.
- **General:** pallor, jaundice, plethora, cyanosis, cachexia, performance status (ECOG).
- **Skin and mucosa:** petechiae, purpura, ecchymoses, gum bleeding / hypertrophy, mouth ulcers, oral thrush, rash, leg ulcers, erythromelalgia.
- **Lymph nodes:** cervical, supraclavicular, axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal; Waldeyer ring; size, consistency, tenderness, fixation.
- **Abdomen:** hepatomegaly, splenomegaly (size in cm), CLD stigmata, masses, testicular mass.
- **Cardiorespiratory:** murmurs (flow, IE), heart failure, crackles, effusion, SVC obstruction signs.
- **Neuro:** GCS, orientation, focal deficit, cranial nerves, meningism, fundi (hyperviscosity, papilledema).
- **Joints and limbs:** arthritis (SLE), hemarthrosis, leg swelling / DVT, limb ischemia.

## 4. Exam add-ons by diagnosis

- **ITP:** petechiae and bruising only; no nodes, no spleen, no systemic signs (if present, think again).
- **TTP / HUS:** fever, confusion or focal deficit, petechiae, jaundice, pallor, high BP (aHUS), oliguria.
- **AIHA:** pallor, jaundice, splenomegaly; look for nodes (CLL / lymphoma) and SLE signs; heart failure if severe.
- **G6PD / hereditary hemolysis:** jaundice, pallor, splenomegaly, gallstones, family pattern.
- **PV:** plethora, conjunctival / retinal vein engorgement, splenomegaly, HTN, erythromelalgia, scratch marks, thrombosis signs.
- **CML / myelofibrosis:** massive splenomegaly (> 20 cm), pallor, bruising.
- **Lymphoma:** node map, Waldeyer ring, hepatosplenomegaly, B-symptom fever.
- **Acute leukemia:** pallor, petechiae, infection sites, gum hypertrophy (AML M4 / M5), nodes, hepatosplenomegaly, testes, meningism.
- **Sickle cell:** pallor, jaundice, dehydration, chest signs (ACS), spleen / liver (sequestration), hip range of motion (AVN, septic arthritis, osteomyelitis), neuro deficit, priapism.

## 5. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### Thrombocytopenia

#### 1. Primary immune thrombocytopenia (45F, petechiae and gum bleeding; two stations)

- **Clues:** 3 days petechiae, bruising, gum bleeding; on amlodipine and aspirin; no systemic features; exam only petechiae.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential) + peripheral film (exclude pseudothrombocytopenia — citrate tube, schistocytes, blasts); coagulation (PT / INR, PTT, fibrinogen); hemolysis screen if anemic (retics, LDH, bilirubin, DAT); secondary causes (HIV, HCV, HBV, H. pylori, ANA / APS, TSH, pregnancy test); bone marrow only if atypical or > 60 years.
- **Results:** platelets 12 ×10⁹/L (12,000/µL) with normal Hb, WBC and film (large platelets, no schistocytes); PT / PTT normal.
- **DDx:** ITP, drug-induced ITP, infection (HIV, HCV, H. pylori, EBV, malaria), hypersplenism, MDS, SLE, APS.
- **More tests:** Coombs, HCV, HIV, pregnancy test, blood group, ANA, TFT, antiphospholipid antibodies, H. pylori.
- **Bone marrow?** Not needed — no atypical features (blasts, dysplasia, other cytopenias, age > 60).
- **Management:** admit and control bleeding; stop aspirin; steroids ± IVIG / anti-D; PPI, calcium, vitamin D with steroids; tranexamic acid for mucosal bleeding; treat iron deficiency; hematology.
- **Second line:** TPO receptor agonists (romiplostim, eltrombopag), rituximab, splenectomy.

#### 2. Idiopathic TTP (30F, fever, fatigue, Hb 8, platelets 10)

- **Clues:** fever, exertional SOB, chest pain, bruising, mild confusion; no focal deficit.
- **Workup:** CBC + peripheral film (schistocytes); hemolysis panel (retics, LDH, haptoglobin, indirect bilirubin, DAT); coagulation (PT / INR, PTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); liver profile; ADAMTS13 activity + inhibitor; PLASMIC score; other (pregnancy test, HIV / hepatitis, ANA, troponin, urinalysis); imaging (CT brain if neurologic).
- **Results:** Hb 8 g/dL (80 g/L); WBC 6 ×10⁹/L; platelets 9–10 ×10⁹/L (10,000/µL); MCV 85 fL; PT 11 s, APTT 30 s (normal); creatinine 125–148 µmol/L (1.4–1.7 mg/dL); AST 70, ALT 50 IU/L; bilirubin 40 µmol/L (2.3 mg/dL); haptoglobin 0.1 g/L (low; 0.5–2.2); retics 5%; LDH 1,300 U/L (140–280); pregnancy test negative; film: schistocytes.
- **DDx:** TTP, STEC-HUS, aHUS, DIC, SLE, catastrophic APS, malignancy-associated TMA.
- **More tests:** ADAMTS13 activity and inhibitor; autoimmune screen; Coombs; ECG and troponin (cardiac involvement); HBV / HCV / HIV; cultures; CT / MRI brain.
- **Management:** ICU, central line, hematology; urgent plasma exchange (1–1.5 plasma volumes daily until platelets and LDH normal for 2 days); FFP only if TPE delayed; high-dose steroids (+ PPI, Ca, vitamin D); rituximab; caplacizumab (anti-vWF); platelets only for life-threatening bleeding or line insertion; aspirin after recovery.
- **Prognosis:** mortality fell from > 90% to ≈ 10% with TPE.

#### 3. Postpartum TTP (19F, 2 weeks after delivery)

- **Clues:** confusion, petechial rash, jaundice.
- **Workup:** CBC + film; hemolysis panel (retics, LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, DAT); coagulation (PT / INR, PTT, fibrinogen); renal profile; liver profile; pregnancy-related (urine protein, BP — HELLP / preeclampsia); imaging (CT brain).
- **Results:** WBC 10 ×10⁹/L; Hb 7 g/dL (70 g/L); platelets 9 ×10⁹/L; LFT and coagulation normal; creatinine 188 µmol/L (2.1 mg/dL); CT brain normal; film schistocytes.
- **DDx:** TTP, HUS, SLE, catastrophic APS (also HELLP / preeclampsia in pregnancy).
- **More tests:** ADAMTS13, ANA / dsDNA, Coombs, ECG, troponin, virology, MRI brain, cultures, 24-h urine protein.
- **Result:** ECG and troponin normal; Coombs negative; autoimmune and virology negative; MRI brain normal; ADAMTS13 0.1% (< 10%) → TTP.
- **Scores:** PLASMIC score; French score (platelets and creatinine).
- **Management:** ICU; TPE until platelets normal, then 2–3 more days; pulse steroids; rituximab × 4; caplacizumab.
- **Caplacizumab:** humanized nanobody binding vWF A1 domain, blocking platelet GPIb-IX-V binding.

#### 4. TTP with spontaneous bruising and confusion (35F)

- **Clues:** bruising for a week, intermittent disorientation, petechiae; only OTC analgesics.
- **Workup:** CBC + film; hemolysis panel (LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, retics, DAT); coagulation (PT, PTT, fibrinogen — normal in TTP, abnormal in DIC); renal profile; B12 (pseudo-TMA); ADAMTS13; imaging (CT brain).
- **Results:** Hb 9.5 g/dL (95 g/L); platelets 18 ×10⁹/L; WBC 9 ×10⁹/L; creatinine 122 µmol/L (1.4 mg/dL; mildly high); LDH and bilirubin high; film schistocytes.
- **DDx:** TTP, HUS, DIC, drug-induced TMA, SLE / APS, B12-deficiency pseudo-TMA.
- **Before results:** PLASMIC score to justify starting plasma exchange.
- **Management:** immediate plasma exchange in ICU; hematology; high-dose steroids; avoid platelets unless life-threatening bleeding or procedure; rituximab, caplacizumab.

#### 5. Atypical HUS (28F, petechiae, fatigue, dark scanty urine)

- **Clues:** recent minor URTI, no diarrhea; BP 170/110, HR 110; petechiae.
- **DDx:** TTP, HUS / aHUS, APS, SLE.
- **Workup:** CBC + film; hemolysis panel (retics, LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, DAT); coagulation (PT / INR, PTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN) + electrolytes (K); urine (urinalysis with microscopy, UPCR); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); immunology (ANA, ENA, ANCA, anti-GBM, C3 / C4, APS screen); ADAMTS13 activity; stool Shiga toxin / E. coli O157; viral screen (HBV, HCV, HIV); liver profile; imaging (renal US, CXR).
- **Results:** Hb 7 g/dL (70 g/L; normocytic); platelets 21 ×10⁹/L; numerous schistocytes; creatinine 350–470 µmol/L (≈ 4.0–5.3 mg/dL) in the two stations; K 5.2 mmol/L; coagulation normal; urine RBC casts; ANA, ANCA negative; C3 low, C4 normal; ADAMTS13 > 10% (excludes TTP); HBV, HCV, HIV negative.
- **Diagnosis:** atypical HUS (complement-mediated; ADAMTS13 not severely low).
- **Management:** monitored bed; supportive care, transfusion; eculizumab (C5 inhibitor); plasma exchange while awaiting ADAMTS13; BP control; dialysis if needed; nephrology, hematology, obstetrics; meningococcal vaccine before eculizumab.
- **Complications:** anemia, folate deficiency, VTE, renal failure, treatment complications.

### Hemolysis

#### 6. Warm AIHA secondary to CLL (70F, SOB, jaundice, fatigue)

- **Clues:** 1 week fatigue, jaundice, SOB; on ACEi; hepatosplenomegaly, raised JVP, crackles, edema.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential) + film (spherocytes, smudge cells); hemolysis panel (retics, LDH, haptoglobin, indirect bilirubin, urine urobilinogen); DAT (IgG, C3) + indirect Coombs; blood bank (group and crossmatch); renal profile; liver profile; coagulation; CLL work-up (flow cytometry, LDH, β2-microglobulin); imaging (US abdomen; CT CAP for staging).
- **Results:** WBC 55 ×10⁹/L, mainly lymphocytes with smudge cells; Hb 6 g/dL (60 g/L); platelets 500 ×10⁹/L; spherocytes; LDH 2,000 U/L; indirect bilirubin 120 µmol/L (≈ 7 mg/dL); retics 9%; DAT 3+ (IgG + C3); renal and coagulation normal; mild splenomegaly; urine urobilinogen 3+.
- **DDx:** warm AIHA, CTD / vasculitis, TMA, HUS, hepatitis, drug-induced AIHA, lymphoma.
- **More tests:** echo, ECG, cultures, iron / B12 / folate, ANA / dsDNA / C3 / C4, viral screen, CT CAP, flow cytometry, node or marrow biopsy.
- **Diagnosis:** warm AIHA secondary to CLL (flow cytometry, nodes above and below diaphragm).
- **Management:** ICU; transfuse least-incompatible blood if symptomatic; methylprednisolone 1 g × 3 then prednisolone 1 mg/kg taper; rituximab 375 mg/m² weekly × 4; Ca, vitamin D, PPI; folic acid; VTE prophylaxis; plasmapheresis if persistent; splenectomy last (vaccinate 2 weeks before).

#### 7. Primary warm AIHA (30M)

- **Workup:** CBC + film; hemolysis panel (retics, LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin); DAT (IgG, C3) + cold agglutinins; secondary causes (ANA, HIV, HBV, HCV, SPEP, CLL flow cytometry); imaging (CT chest / abdomen / pelvis for lymphoma).
- **Results:** WBC 6 ×10⁹/L; Hb 6 g/dL (60 g/L; microcytic hypochromic picture on the sheet) with spherocytes; platelets 500 ×10⁹/L; LDH 2,000 U/L; indirect bilirubin 120 µmol/L (≈ 7 mg/dL); retics 9%; DAT 3+ IgG / C3; ANA, viral screen, CT CAP negative.
- **Management:** monitored bed, hydration; transfusion and folic acid; pulse then tapering steroids; rituximab weekly × 4; IVIG if no response; plasmapheresis and splenectomy if persistent; VTE prophylaxis (D-dimer and Doppler if raised).
- **Splenectomy:** if steroids and rituximab fail or are not tolerated; vaccinate against pneumococcus, meningococcus and Hib ≥ 2–3 weeks before; thromboprophylaxis after.

#### 8. G6PD deficiency (19M, jaundice and dark urine after drug for dental infection)

- **Clues:** previous similar episode needing transfusion; tachycardia, jaundice, pallor, spleen 2 fingers.
- **Workup:** CBC + film (bite cells) + supravital stain (Heinz bodies); hemolysis panel (retics, LDH, haptoglobin, indirect bilirubin, urine hemoglobin); DAT; renal profile (pigment nephropathy); imaging (US gallbladder).
- **Results:** WBC 11 ×10⁹/L; Hb 6 g/dL (60 g/L); platelets 500 ×10⁹/L; LDH 2,000 U/L; retics 9%; indirect bilirubin 120 µmol/L (≈ 7 mg/dL); polychromasia, bite cells and Heinz bodies; DAT negative; gallstones on US.
- **DDx:** G6PD, hereditary pyropoikilocytosis, thalassemia, immune hemolysis, sickle cell.
- **Tests:** G6PD fluorescent spot test; quantitative enzyme assay after recovery (can be falsely normal during hemolysis due to young cells); molecular testing; Hb electrophoresis.
- **Diagnosis:** G6PD deficiency (X-linked recessive).
- **Management:** admit; transfuse; stop offending drugs; IV fluids; treat infection; folic acid 5 mg; monitor urine output (dialysis if AKI); avoid oxidant drugs and fava beans; splenectomy rarely.

### Myeloproliferative and malignancy

#### 9. JAK2-positive polycythemia vera (66M, headache, HTN, blurred vision)

- **Clues:** 2 months pain and tingling in extremities, headache, new HTN; plethora; splenomegaly.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential, Hct) + film; renal profile; liver profile; coagulation; urinalysis; imaging (abdominal US with Doppler — spleen, portal / hepatic veins).
- **Results:** WBC 16 ×10⁹/L (normal differential); Hb 19 g/dL (190 g/L); Hct 59%; platelets 660 ×10⁹/L; spleen 20 cm with chronic portal vein thrombosis.
- **DDx:** PV, ET, myelofibrosis, CML, EPO-secreting tumor, secondary polycythemia.
- **Tests:** molecular (JAK2 V617F → exon 12; BCR-ABL to exclude CML); serum EPO; B12, LDH, uric acid; imaging (CT CAP); bone marrow biopsy.
- **Results:** JAK2 positive, low EPO, high B12 / LDH, BCR-ABL negative.
- **High risk:** age > 60 or prior thrombosis.
- **Management:** venesection to Hct < 45%; hydroxyurea; aspirin 81 mg; lifelong anticoagulation for portal vein thrombosis; hematology.

#### 10. Chronic myeloid leukemia (70M, abdominal fullness, weight loss)

- **Clues:** fatigue, anorexia, SOB; splenomegaly without nodes.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential) + film; renal profile; liver profile; coagulation; LDH, uric acid; imaging (abdominal US with Doppler).
- **Results:** WBC 66 ×10⁹/L with neutrophilia and basophilia (whole myeloid spectrum on film); Hb 15 g/dL (150 g/L); platelets 660 ×10⁹/L; LFT, renal, coagulation normal; spleen 24 cm, no vein thrombosis.
- **DDx:** CML, ET, myelofibrosis, PV, solid tumor.
- **Tests:** molecular (BCR-ABL PCR / FISH, karyotype for Philadelphia chromosome; JAK2 if negative); B12, LDH; imaging (CT CAP); bone marrow (phase — blasts %) → BCR-ABL positive, JAK2 negative.
- **Diagnosis:** CML (BCR-ABL positive).
- **Management:** tyrosine kinase inhibitors (imatinib, nilotinib, dasatinib); hematology.
- **Phases:** chronic, accelerated, blast.

#### 11. Acute myeloid leukemia t(8;21) (50M, epistaxis and recurrent infections)

- **Clues:** 2 months worsening epistaxis, 3 chest infections in 3 months, fatigue, fever; bariatric surgery 15 years ago; pale, tachycardic.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential) + film (blasts, Auer rods); hematinics (iron, B12, folate, copper — exclude reversible causes); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); infection (blood cultures, CXR); hemolysis panel; coagulation (PT, PTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer — DIC); tumor lysis labs (K, PO4, Ca, uric acid, LDH, creatinine); liver profile; bone marrow with flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular (FLT3, NPM1, CEBPA, KIT); HLA typing; echo before anthracycline.
- **Results:** WBC 17 ×10⁹/L; Hb 9 g/dL (90 g/L); platelets 50 ×10⁹/L; film 22% blasts with dysplastic neutrophils; marrow t(8;21) RUNX1-RUNX1T1 (favorable, core-binding factor AML).
- **DDx:** AML, ALL, MDS, aplastic anemia, copper deficiency, reactive.
- **Confirm:** bone marrow with flow cytometry, cytogenetics, molecular tests; TLS labs (uric acid, LDH, K, PO4, Ca); viral screen; ECG, echo, HLA typing.
- **Result:** MPO, CD13, CD33, CD34, CD117 positive; t(8;21) → AML (favorable risk).
- **Management:** admit to hematology; transfuse; correct coagulopathy; febrile → cultures and broad-spectrum antibiotics; antimicrobial prophylaxis; fertility preservation; TLS prophylaxis (fluids, allopurinol / rasburicase); induction 7 + 3 (cytarabine + anthracycline); consolidation high-dose cytarabine.
- **After induction:** favorable risk = HiDAC only; intermediate / high risk = allogeneic SCT in first remission.
- **Poor prognosis:** adverse cytogenetics (monosomy 7, complex karyotype, FLT3-ITD), older age, poor PS, therapy-related or secondary AML, no remission after induction.

#### 12. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (40M, multiple neck masses)

- **Clues:** 1 month painless enlarging right neck node; fever > 3 weeks, drenching sweats, > 10% weight loss; hard cervical and axillary nodes 3–4 cm; no spleen.
- **DDx:** lymphoma / leukemia / metastasis, infection (TB, brucella, HIV, EBV, CMV), SLE / Still, sarcoid, HLH.
- **Workup:** CBC + film; renal profile; liver profile; LDH, uric acid; coagulation; infection screen (brucella, TB — QuantiFERON, HIV, EBV, CMV, HBV / HCV); ANA; imaging (contrast CT neck / chest / abdomen / pelvis, then PET-CT); tissue (excisional node biopsy with immunohistochemistry, FISH for MYC / BCL2 / BCL6); bone marrow.
- **Next:** excisional lymph node biopsy (not FNA).
- **Result:** large B cells CD19 / CD20 / CD22 / CD79a +, BCL2 +, MYC rearranged, Ki-67 > 80% → DLBCL.
- **Staging:** LDH, uric acid, HBV / HCV / HIV, PET-CT; nodes above diaphragm only + B symptoms = Ann Arbor stage IIB.
- **Management:** hematology-oncology; fertility preservation; R-CHOP (or DA-EPOCH-R for high-grade / double-hit); CNS prophylaxis if high risk; HBV prophylaxis before rituximab; CAR-T for relapse.

### Sickle cell and transfusion

#### 13. Sickle cell vaso-occlusive crisis with hip pain (23M)

- **Workup:** CBC + retics (vs baseline); hemolysis panel (LDH, bilirubin); infection (CRP, procalcitonin, blood cultures); renal profile; blood bank (extended-phenotype crossmatch); imaging (hip X-ray / MRI for AVN; CXR if chest symptoms).
- **Results:** WBC 3.2 ×10⁹/L; Hb 5.9 g/dL (59 g/L; baseline 8–9); platelets 322 ×10⁹/L; retics 2.5% (inappropriately low — consider aplastic crisis / parvovirus); procalcitonin normal; CRP unchanged from baseline.
- **DDx:** acute VOC, avascular necrosis of femoral head, osteomyelitis / septic arthritis, drug-seeking.
- **Management:** admit; incentive spirometry; O2 if SpO2 < 94%; hematology; IV fluids; transfuse for symptomatic Hb drop; rapid analgesia ladder (acetaminophen, NSAIDs, opioids / PCA); DVT prophylaxis; continue folic acid and hydroxyurea; septic screen; hip X-ray then MRI.
- **Hydroxyurea benefits:** raises HbF, lowers WBC and retics, improves red-cell deformability.
- **Hydroxyurea side effects:** myelosuppression, nausea, hair thinning, skin / mouth ulcers; teratogenic (stop 3 months before conception in women, 6 months in men).
- **Iron overload:** suspect when ferritin > 2500 or by liver MRI; chelate (deferoxamine, deferasirox, deferiprone); avoid unnecessary transfusion.
- **Long-term care:** yearly BP, urine protein, dilated eye exam; vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, meningococcal, Hib); transcranial Doppler to age 16; penicillin prophylaxis to age 5.

#### 14. TRALI during transfusion in sickle cell disease (30F)

- **Immediate:** stop the transfusion; keep the bag and tubing; check vitals; recheck patient ID and product label, look at the bag for discoloration / bubbles; assess volume status; inform blood bank.
- **Workup:** imaging (STAT CXR); transfusion reaction work-up (repeat group and crossmatch, DAT, hemolysis panel — LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, urine hemoglobin; blood cultures from patient and bag); BNP (TACO vs TRALI); CBC; renal profile; blood gas (PaO2 / FiO2); troponin; tryptase (anaphylaxis); coagulation (DIC); donor anti-HLA / anti-HNA antibodies.
- **DDx:** TRALI (hypotension, ± fever); TACO (hypertension, overload, high BNP, responds to diuretic); anaphylaxis (urticaria, angioedema, hypotension); severe febrile non-hemolytic; septic reaction (fever + hypotension); acute hemolytic reaction (fever, hypotension, DIC).
- **TRALI definition:** new non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema during or within 6 h of transfusion; donor anti-HLA / anti-neutrophil antibodies activate recipient neutrophils in the lung.
- **Management and prevention:** supportive (O2, ventilation; no diuretics); quarantine the donor; use plasma from male or never-pregnant donors.
- **TACO risk factors:** extremes of age, positive fluid balance, heart or kidney dysfunction, rapid plasma transfusion, emergency surgery.
- **Pregnancy counseling in SCD:** genetic counseling and partner's hemoglobinopathy status; update vaccines; folic acid 5 mg; stop hydroxyurea 3 months before, stop ACEi / ARB and chelators; echo for pulmonary HTN (maternal mortality up to 50%); risks of preeclampsia, preterm labor, abruption, IUGR, VOC, VTE, acute chest syndrome; avoid triggers (dehydration, cold, stress).

### Angry patient / anticoagulation stations

#### 15. DVT after missed prophylaxis (70M, bedbound with pneumonia; two stations)

- **Task:** angry patient framework (see General): ABC, safety, let him vent, empathize, apologize without blaming, explain plan and complaint process, follow-up.
- **Leg swelling DDx:** cellulitis / abscess; DVT, thrombophlebitis, venous / arterial insufficiency, post-thrombotic; lymphedema; myositis, rhabdomyolysis; compartment syndrome; Baker cyst; neuropathy; tumors.
- **Thrombophilia testing if:** recurrent VTE, VTE at young age, strong family history, unusual sites.
- **Anticoagulate superficial vein thrombosis if:** ≥ 5 cm long, within 5 cm of the deep system (saphenofemoral junction), or positive D-dimer.
- **Cancer-associated VTE (brain metastases):** LMWH (enoxaparin); duration indefinite while cancer active.

#### 16. Warfarin given in error with gum bleeding (55M)

- **Task:** angry patient framework.
- **Gum bleeding DDx:** gingivitis / periodontitis, hard brushing, infection, leukemia, hemophilia / coagulopathy, platelet disorders (ITP, TTP), vitamin C or K deficiency, anticoagulants, pregnancy.
- **Results:** INR 4 with only gum bleeding after brushing, no other bleeding.
- **Vitamin K or PCC?** No (minor bleeding only); stop warfarin and recheck INR in 24–48 h.
- **PCC complications:** thrombosis (≈ 1.4%), HIT (contains heparin).
- **PCC over FFP:** faster reversal, small volume (less overload), less TRALI.
- **Increase warfarin effect:** amiodarone, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, cotrimoxazole, metronidazole, fluconazole.
- **Decrease warfarin effect:** rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, OCPs, vitamin K-rich food.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **First labs for any cytopenia:** CBC with differential, peripheral film, retics, LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, DAT, coag.
- **Schistocytes + low platelets = TMA:** start plasma exchange for presumed TTP (PLASMIC ≥ 6) while ADAMTS13 is pending.
- **Platelet transfusion:** avoid in TTP / HIT unless life-threatening bleeding.
- **Splenectomy prep:** pneumococcal, meningococcal, Hib vaccines ≥ 2 weeks before; VTE prophylaxis after.
- **Steroid cover:** PPI, calcium, vitamin D.
- **Before rituximab:** hepatitis B screening.
