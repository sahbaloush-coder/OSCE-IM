# Communication & Counseling

## How to use this

Built from the two communication-skills files (Dr. Moayad Aljuhani's Arabic scripts, Family Medicine OSCE First Aid pages, KAMC-Jeddah examiner forms) and the counseling checklists (general counseling, inhaler technique, kidney biopsy, post-PCI care). The Qassim communication stations themselves (lung and colon cancer, angry patients, biologics, cyclophosphamide) are in the General and Miscellaneous tabs.

- **Language:** the counseling is done in Arabic in the exam — the key Arabic phrases are given with each framework.
- **Which framework:** major bad news → introduction then SPIKES; procedure or consent (transfusion, dialysis, biopsy) → introduction then ESAR; new drug → medication framework; angry patient or error → angry-patient framework.
- **Common mistake:** if a station starts as a history / exam case and turns into a communication task, introduce yourself again before the communication part.
- **Topics covered:** angry patient; medical error disclosure; breaking bad news (cancer, HIV, hepatitis B); hepatitis C counseling; pre-HIV-test counseling; blood transfusion consent; DNAR discussion; starting a medication (warfarin, steroids, methotrexate, metformin, statins); inhaler technique; hemodialysis initiation; kidney biopsy consent; post-PCI clinic; smoking cessation; pre-pregnancy counseling (SLE, sickle cell, pulmonary hypertension); pre-operative counseling.

## 1. Opening and closing every station

- **Opening (English):** introduce yourself and your role; confirm name and age / date of birth with two identifiers; explain the purpose; ask permission; private setting (phone and pager off, door closed); ask if they want a relative present; sit down at eye level.
- **Opening (Arabic):** "السلام عليكم، أنا الدكتور/ة ____ من قسم الباطنة والفريق المعالج"; "ممكن تزودني باسمك وعمرك؟"; "هل الوقت مناسب لمناقشة حالتك الصحية؟"; "هل ترغب بوجود أحد أفراد عائلتك خلال النقاش؟".
- **Ideas, concerns, expectations (ICE):** "ماذا تعرف عن حالتك الصحية؟"; "هل عندك أي مخاوف أو توقعات؟".
- **Closing (English):** summarize; check understanding (teach-back); invite questions; safety net (when to come to the ER); contact number; leaflets / websites / support groups; follow-up appointment; thank the patient.
- **Closing (Arabic):** "هل جميع الأمور واضحة؟ أو تحتاج أعيد نقطة معينة؟"; "إذا حصل أي أمر طارئ تقدر تجي على الطوارئ مباشرة أو تتواصل مع العيادة على هذا الرقم"; "هذي منشورات ومواقع ومجموعات دعم ممكن تستفيد منها"; "شكراً جزيلاً لحضورك ووقتك".

## 2. Frameworks

### SPIKES — breaking bad news

- **Setting:** private room, phones off, door closed, tissues and water, relative if wanted, sit down, enough time.
- **Perception:** "What do you understand about why we did these tests?" — "هل عندك خلفية عن سبب التحاليل؟".
- **Invitation:** "Would you like me to go through the results in detail?" — "هل ترغب أن أبدأ بالنتيجة وأشرح التشخيص؟".
- **Knowledge:** warning shot ("I'm afraid the results are not what we hoped"); plain words, small chunks; no jargon ("weakens" not "decreased", "blood test" not "serology"); no bluntness; never "nothing more we can do".
- **Emotions:** allow silence, keep eye contact; observe → name the emotion → link it to the news → empathic statement ("I can see this is very hard for you").
- **Strategy and summary:** MDT plan (oncology, surgery, palliative, social worker, psychologist); referral with a named specialist; follow-up soon; hope without false hope; offer help getting home.

### ESAR — procedures and consent

- **E — Explanation and knowledge:** what they know; why it is needed (their condition); what the procedure is in simple words; aim; duration.
- **S — Safety and risks:** benefits; common and serious complications and how they are minimized.
- **A — Alternatives:** other options and their limits; risks of refusing.
- **R — Reassurance and consent:** questions, written consent (name, signature, date), leaflets, follow-up, thank.

### Starting a medication

- **Explore:** what they know; current drugs and allergies; contraindications (pregnancy, liver / kidney disease); adherence barriers; hidden agenda.
- **Explain:** why it is started; how it works in lay terms; how to take it; how long; baseline and monitoring tests.
- **Risks:** common side effects; rare but serious ones; how to reduce them (e.g. alendronate with long-term steroids); specific hazards (adrenal crisis if steroids stopped suddenly).
- **Safety net:** what to report and when to seek urgent help; carry a steroid card / warfarin booklet; store away from children.
- **Close:** solutions to compliance problems (dosette box, alarms); leaflet; follow-up.

### Disease counseling

- **Content:** normal physiology → what the disease is → why it happens → complications → aims of treatment → lifestyle + medicines → red flags / when to seek help.

### Angry patient and error disclosure

- **Order:** stabilize the patient first (ABC) if unwell → introduce → safety (door open, sit near the door, safe distance; say it aloud) → let them vent without interrupting → acknowledge → apologize → explain → actions → complaint route → follow-up → close.
- **Arabic:** "ممكن تشرح لي سبب غضبك؟"; "أنا متفهم جداً لشعورك وقلقك"; "نعتذر لك على اللي حصل، وسنضع خطة لتخفيف الضرر الحاصل"; "سنرفع الأمر لرئيس القسم لتفادي حدوثه في المستقبل"; "نقدر نحولك للأخصائي الاجتماعي أو تجربة المريض لتقديم شكوى رسمية، وهذا حقك ولن يؤثر على سير علاجك".
- **Never:** argue; blame a colleague or department; be defensive.

## 3. Stations

### Angry patient and errors

#### 1. Angry patient (adverse event, e.g. drug allergy)

- **Emotions:** ask the reason; full attention, eye contact; no interruption; empathy.
- **Apology:** apologize sincerely and accept the grievance; the aim is to treat the problem (e.g. infection needed an antibiotic).
- **Actions:** treat the current problem (e.g. antihistamines, document the allergy in the file); report to the head of department and clinics; meeting with the treating team (involve nephrology etc. as needed); social worker / patient-experience office for a formal complaint; follow-up appointment or phone call.
- **Close:** emergency contact; leaflets; check understanding; questions; thank.

#### 2. Medical error disclosure (e.g. pneumothorax after central line; wrong antibiotic dose → AKI)

- **Approach:** same as the angry patient, but you volunteer the error.
- **Explain honestly:** what happened ("your condition needed an antibiotic, but I gave you an incorrect dose and this raised your kidney tests"), the harm, what is being done now.
- **Then:** apology; plan to reduce harm; full reports and results; incident report and review to prevent recurrence; complaint route; follow-up.

### Breaking bad news

#### 3. Breaking bad news — cancer

- **Framework:** SPIKES.
- **Knowledge (Arabic example):** "الفترة الماضية كنت تشكي من نقص في الوزن وفقدان شهية وعقدة في الغدة اللمفاوية، لذلك أخذنا خزعة للتأكد من التشخيص، وتبين وجود خلايا سرطانية" — in a calm voice, show empathy.
- **Strategy:** "الفريق الطبي رح يرسم لك خطة علاجية متكاملة"; referral to a named oncologist; early appointment; social worker / psychologist.

#### 4. Breaking bad news — HIV infection

- **Before the result:** rule out risk factors (multiple partners, tattoos, transfusion, surgery, IV drugs); occupation, marital status; ask if they like details.
- **Disclosure:** confirmed result only (repeat / confirmatory test); "I'm sorry to tell you your results came back positive for HIV" → pause, tissues, allow emotions, offer rest.
- **Knowledge:** a virus that weakens the immune system; spread by blood, sex, mother-to-child (pregnancy, delivery, breastfeeding), shared needles; treatment is available locally — not curative but controls the virus, near-normal life if taken regularly; earlier treatment = fewer complications.
- **Protecting others:** condoms (water-based lubricants), no sharing needles or razors, no blood or organ donation, tell your dentist and surgeons; inform the wife — MOH has a clinic for testing spouses and children; inform previous sexual partners.
- **Plan:** CD4, viral load, STI screen; ID referral; start ART immediately regardless of CD4 (START / TEMPRANO); social worker / psychologist.
- **Vaccines at next visit:** hepatitis B, influenza, pneumococcal, Td / Tdap for all; hepatitis A, MMR and varicella only if CD4 ≥ 200 cells/µL.
- **Children:** with effective ART (undetectable viral load) transmission to partner and baby is near zero; assess the wife's status first.
- **Board questions:** disclose only after confirmation; report to infection control and MOH; encourage the patient to tell his wife (hospital / MOH policy); safety net — fever, rash, chest infection, weight loss.

#### 5. Breaking bad news — hepatitis B

- **Setting and disclosure:** as for HIV; ask about jaundice, abdominal pain, vomiting, fatigue.
- **Knowledge:** common worldwide, preventable by vaccine; affects the liver; can clear, become a carrier, or become chronic (> 6 months).
- **Transmission:** blood, saliva, genital secretions — shared razors, blades, toothbrushes, sex; not by hugging, sneezing, coughing, breastfeeding or sharing cups.
- **Daily life:** no restrictions; balanced diet; avoid alcohol; test and vaccinate family and spouse; condoms; hepatitis A vaccine.
- **Complications:** cirrhosis / liver failure (≈ 20% of chronic) and hepatocellular carcinoma (≈ 15%) if not managed; antivirals slow damage.
- **Plan:** liver tests, STI screen, ultrasound, hepatology referral; safety net — jaundice, itching, bleeding, sleep disturbance.

#### 6. Counseling a patient with hepatitis C

- **Ask:** ICE; since when, how acquired, treatment so far; symptoms (weight loss, jaundice, dark urine, pale stool, abdominal pain); smoking, sexual activity, drugs.
- **Explain:** infects the liver; spread by needles, razors, toothbrushes, multiple partners (rare through modern transfusion); not by casual contact; 70–80% become chronic; cirrhosis in 15–30% after 20–30 years, then HCC.
- **Treatment (updated):** direct-acting antivirals for 8–12 weeks cure > 95% (the source page mentions interferon / lamivudine — outdated).
- **Protect yourself:** no alcohol; no drugs or herbs without advice; hepatitis A and B vaccines; safe sex.
- **Protect others:** don't share toothbrushes / razors / needles; clean blood spills with bleach; cover wounds; no blood donation; test the spouse.
- **Refer:** hepatology; addiction services if IV drug use / alcohol; safety net as for HBV.

#### 7. Pre-test HIV counseling

- **Explain the reason:** risk assessment — STIs, partner HIV-positive, men who have sex with men, partner from a high-prevalence country, IV drug use, transfusion abroad, sex work.
- **Knowledge:** HIV vs AIDS; most positive people are asymptomatic; transmission routes.
- **Benefits of testing:** if positive — effective treatment, normal life expectancy, can have children, reduces transmission, counseling available; if negative — ends uncertainty.
- **The test:** antigen / antibody blood test (4th generation); window period ≈ 3 months → repeat at 3 months if recent exposure; point-of-care tests miss HIV-2.
- **Confidentiality:** results kept confidential; consent; how and when results will be given.

### Procedures and consent (ESAR)

#### 8. Blood transfusion consent

- **Explanation:** because of bleeding / severe anemia / marrow failure your body is not making enough blood; transfusion = giving donated blood through a cannula in the arm; aim = replace blood and prevent low BP or organ failure; each unit takes 1–4 hours.
- **Risks:** acute hemolytic reaction; very rare viral infection (blood is screened for HIV, hepatitis B and C); fever, rash, breathlessness; fluid overload in heart or kidney patients.
- **Alternatives:** IV iron; erythropoiesis-stimulating agents — not as effective in an emergency.
- **Reassurance and consent:** every unit is carefully cross-matched; questions; written consent (name, signature, date); leaflet.

#### 9. Hemodialysis initiation (e.g. 60M, diabetic CKD, eGFR 10, nausea, itching)

- **Indications to explain:** kidneys can no longer remove waste, extra fluid or balance salts — uremia, volume overload, acidosis, high potassium.
- **What it is:** a machine acts as an "artificial kidney"; blood passes through tubing, is cleaned and returned; 3 sessions a week, 3–5 hours each.
- **Access:** AV fistula (best, small operation joining artery to vein in the arm); AV graft (synthetic tube if fistula fails); central venous catheter in the neck or groin (temporary).
- **Benefits:** less fatigue, nausea and breathlessness; better appetite and alertness; lower mortality.
- **Risks:** fatigue, low BP episodes, cramps, itching, dizziness, nausea, headache; access infection or thrombosis; (peritonitis and hernia with peritoneal dialysis).
- **Alternatives:** peritoneal dialysis at home (needs strict hygiene); kidney transplant (best long-term option, depends on donor); conservative / palliative care in very elderly or advanced illness.
- **Refusing:** toxin and fluid build-up → uremic coma, heart or lung failure, sudden death.
- **Close:** dialysis is not a cure but keeps life stable; involve family; patient's role in the decision; another appointment to reconsider; written consent; leaflet.
- **Same station — CKD complications:** anemia (iron if ferritin < 200 ng/mL and TSAT < 20%, then ESA; exclude B12 / folate / GI loss); hyperphosphatemia (diet, binders); metabolic acidosis (bicarbonate); volume (diuretics, BP control); hold metformin / sitagliptin; plan transplant vs HD / PD and access early.

#### 10. Kidney biopsy consent

- **Explanation:** a small needle takes a small piece of kidney under local anesthesia with ultrasound guidance; benefit = diagnosis that guides treatment; risk of not doing it = delayed diagnosis and wrong treatment.
- **Before:** stop antiplatelets / anticoagulants for the right time; CBC and coagulation; fast 8 hours; BP controlled (< 140/90).
- **Complications:** bleeding (hematuria, hematoma — may need transfusion or embolization), infection, pain.
- **After:** lie flat at least 4 hours; monitor vitals and urine color / output; analgesia; repeat CBC after 4–6 hours; most go home the same day; avoid strenuous activity.
- **Alternatives:** blood tests and imaging only (less precise).
- **Red flags:** visible blood in urine, confusion, dizziness / low BP, severe pain, fever, low urine output or inability to pass urine.
- **Close:** clinic follow-up for the result; questions; consent; thank.

#### 11. DNAR discussion (with patient or family)

- **Setting:** private, phone off, door closed; family present if wanted; explore what they understand.
- **Explain the situation:** the patient is critically ill in ICU and receiving all recommended treatment, but because of severity, chronic illnesses and age, the chance of responding is very low; three consultants met and agreed that CPR would not be in the patient's best interest if the heart stops.
- **What DNAR means:** no CPR if the heart stops; all other treatment continues (medicines, oxygen, feeding, fluids, comfort); "not withdrawing care — avoiding a futile intervention" — "القرار لا يعني سحب العلاج بل منع تدخل غير مجدٍ".
- **Review:** reversed if the patient improves; part of advance care planning; consultant signs the form; inform the MDT.
- **Emotions:** let them speak without interruption; "أتفهم تماماً صعوبة هذا القرار... نحن معكم ونحترم مشاعركم".
- **Close:** social worker; offer to explain to other family members; contact number; leaflet.

### Medication counseling

#### 12. Starting warfarin (AF with mitral stenosis)

- **Explain:** palpitations led to tests showing AF with a narrowed mitral valve → blood thinner warfarin, tablet once daily; reduces stroke risk by about 70%; LMWH injections until INR is therapeutic.
- **Monitoring:** INR blood tests (target 2–3; every 8–12 weeks once stable).
- **Side effects:** bleeding, bruising, rash, abdominal pain; teratogenic.
- **Warnings:** keep vitamin K foods steady (leafy greens, green tea); interactions — antibiotics, herbs, multivitamins, NSAIDs; tell the dentist; careful shaving; not safe in pregnancy — plan with your doctor; head injury → urgent review; wear a warfarin bracelet / carry the booklet.
- **Alternatives:** DOACs — not suitable with moderate–severe mitral stenosis or mechanical valves.

#### 13. Common drugs — summary table

- **Prednisolone:** stops the immune system attacking itself / reduces inflammation; not a painkiller; side effects — ulcer, osteoporosis, weight gain, Cushingoid, diabetes, HTN, infection, cataract; hazard — adrenal crisis if stopped suddenly; carry a steroid card, increase dose in illness (sick-day rules); monitor glucose, BP, DEXA; give alendronate for long courses.
- **Warfarin:** thins the blood to prevent clots; bleeding, bruising, rash, alopecia, teratogenic; interactions with alcohol, antibiotics, antiepileptics; INR monitoring; report bleeding or any head injury; bracelet.
- **Methotrexate:** once weekly — never daily; GI upset, mouth ulcers, infections, rash, hair thinning, marrow suppression, liver fibrosis, pneumonitis, teratogenic (men and women); baseline CBC, renal, LFT, CXR; folic acid; avoid alcohol; no live vaccines; report breathlessness, jaundice, fever, sore throat, bruising.
- **Metformin:** makes the body more sensitive to insulin; with meals; GI upset; no hypoglycemia alone; risk of lactic acidosis in kidney failure or hypoxia; hold before contrast and surgery; monitor renal function and HbA1c.
- **Statin:** lowers cholesterol and cardiovascular risk; muscle aches, liver injury; report muscle pain, weakness, dark urine or jaundice; lipid profile and LFT.
- **Biologics (RA / SpA):** see General tab — infection and TB screen, vaccines, injection technique.

#### 14. Inhaler technique (asthma — salbutamol + beclomethasone)

- **Explain asthma:** narrowing of the airways from inflammation / allergy; triggers (cold air, exercise, dust, pollen, smoke).
- **Reliever vs preventer:** blue salbutamol = reliever when symptoms (2 puffs; side effects tremor, palpitations, headache); brown beclomethasone = preventer, every day even without symptoms (sore throat / oral thrush — rinse mouth).
- **MDI steps:** check expiry and dose counter → remove cap, check mouthpiece → shake well → sit upright, chin slightly up → breathe out fully away from the inhaler → tight seal with lips → press once while breathing in slowly and deeply → hold breath 10 seconds (or as long as comfortable) → breathe out slowly → wait 30–60 seconds before a second puff → replace cap → rinse mouth after steroid.
- **Teach-back:** patient demonstrates; give feedback; explain spacer use if needed.
- **When to seek help:** no improvement after 4 puffs of the blue inhaler (UK guide: 10 puffs, 1 every 30–60 s, then call emergency); cannot speak full sentences; blue lips; drowsy.
- **Arabic steps:** "رج البخاخ جيداً، أزل الغطاء، أخرج النفس تماماً، ضع فمك حول الفوهة بإحكام، اضغط مرة واحدة أثناء الشهيق ببطء وعمق، احبس النفس 10 ثواني، أخرج النفس ببطء، وبعد بخاخ الكورتيزون تمضمض".
- **Close:** leaflet; follow-up to recheck technique; school nurse can hold inhalers; questions.

### Clinics and lifestyle

#### 15. First clinic after PCI

- **History:** chest pain and all cardiac symptoms; access-site pain or bleeding (radial / femoral); low mood; when PCI was done, number and type of stents, complications; medications, doses, adherence, side effects; home BP, comorbidities, smoking, diet; return to exercise, sex and driving.
- **Exam:** HR and BP (to titrate drugs); heart and lungs; leg edema; access site for hematoma, pseudoaneurysm, AV fistula.
- **Review:** cath report; echo (if EF low, repeat after ≥ 90 days of optimal therapy — ICD decision); creatinine, potassium, LDL, HbA1c, ECG.
- **Adjust:** DAPT duration by stent type and ACS vs elective; beta blocker to HR 50–60; high-intensity statin, add ezetimibe if LDL > 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL); HF drugs guided by creatinine, K and BP.
- **Lifestyle:** driving after 1–2 weeks if uncomplicated (4 weeks after cardiogenic shock); work and air travel after about 2 weeks; sex after 4–6 weeks (sooner if low risk); daily activities immediately; cardiac rehab; stop smoking; vaccines; weight loss; treat DM and HTN.

#### 16. Smoking cessation

- **History:** age started; cigarettes per day / pack-years; type (cigarette, shisha); previous quit attempts, how long, why relapsed; why quit now; symptoms (cough, dyspnea, weight loss, night sweats); support at home; depression, epilepsy (bupropion), alcohol / drugs.
- **Advise:** stopping is the single best thing for your health; link to their symptoms; benefits (heart, lungs, fertility, money, appearance).
- **Assess readiness:** stage of change; if ready → plan; if unsure → explore barriers gently.
- **Plan:** quit date within 2 weeks; smoking diary and gradual reduction; avoid triggers; tell family and friends; exercise when craving; withdrawal symptoms (irritability, appetite, headache, insomnia) are temporary.
- **Drugs:** nicotine replacement (gum 4 mg every 1–2 h, max 20/day, patches, inhaler; 8–12 weeks; mouth irritation, insomnia); bupropion 150 mg daily × 3 days then twice daily for 7–12 weeks, start 1 week before quit date (insomnia, dry mouth, headache, seizures — avoid in epilepsy); varenicline 0.5 mg → 1 mg twice daily for 12 weeks, start 1 week before (nausea, insomnia, vivid dreams).
- **Follow-up:** within 2–4 weeks; smoking-cessation clinic; screen for cardiovascular risk.

#### 17. Pre-pregnancy counseling

- **SLE:** plan pregnancy only after ≥ 6 months of inactive disease; stop methotrexate, mycophenolate, cyclophosphamide (switch in advance); continue hydroxychloroquine, azathioprine, low-dose steroids; folic acid; screen aPL, renal function, BP, urine protein, anti-Ro / La; risks — miscarriage, preeclampsia, preterm birth, IUGR, flare, neonatal lupus; joint rheumatology–obstetric care; low-dose aspirin.
- **Sickle cell disease:** genetic counseling and test the partner; vaccines (pneumococcal, influenza, hepatitis B); folic acid 5 mg daily; stop hydroxyurea (3 months before), ACEi / ARBs, iron chelators; baseline Hb, renal, liver, ferritin, echo if PH suspected; risks — miscarriage, preeclampsia, IUGR, crises, VTE, acute chest syndrome; avoid dehydration, cold, overexertion.
- **Pulmonary hypertension:** strongly advise against pregnancy (very high maternal mortality, up to 30–50%); reliable contraception — avoid estrogen, use progestin-only / IUD (bosentan reduces hormonal contraceptive efficacy); if pregnant → specialist cardiology + obstetric care, discuss early termination; psychosocial support.

#### 18. Pre-operative counseling

- **Functional capacity:** can climb 1–2 flights of stairs or walk briskly = ≥ 4 METs (good); < 4 METs = higher risk, needs evaluation.
- **RCRI (1 point each):** IHD; HF; stroke / TIA; insulin-treated DM; creatinine > 2 mg/dL (177 µmol/L); high-risk surgery → 0 = low (< 1%), 1–2 = intermediate (1–6%), ≥ 3 = high (> 6%).
- **Explain simply:** "your heart risk is low / moderate / high; we will take precautions"; risk ≠ cancellation.
- **Pre-op:** CBC, electrolytes, renal and liver function, ECG, CXR if indicated; optimize BP, DM, COPD, CKD; continue beta blockers and statins; hold ACEi / ARB on the morning of surgery if advised; plan anticoagulants and antiplatelets; stop smoking ≥ 4 weeks before.
- **Peri-op:** monitoring before, during and after; cardiology / anesthesia input; shared decision-making.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Examiner global rating:** organized approach; mixed open and closed questions; active listening; no jargon; responds to ICE; checks understanding; time management.
- **Who to involve:** social worker, psychologist, patient-experience office (complaints), named specialist, family (with consent).
- **Safety net phrase:** "If anything worries you — fever, bleeding, breathlessness, confusion — come to the ER directly or call the clinic on this number."
