# Neurology

## How to use this

The 18 neurology stations split into examination stations (cranial nerves ×3, extrapyramidal ×1) and history stations built around a few syndromes: headache with seizure (CVT ×2, IIH), weakness / fatigability (MG ×2, GBS, stroke / TIA), visual loss (optic neuritis ×2), movement (Parkinson, NMS) and cranial neuropathies (Bell, diabetic 3rd nerve). Localize first, then list causes.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; in acute weakness / seizure / respiratory distress do ABC, glucose, and (stroke) FAST and code stroke first.
- **Stations covered:** exam (cranial nerves ×3, EPS / Parkinson); headache (CVT ×2, IIH); NMJ (MG, myasthenic crisis); neuropathy (GBS, copper-deficiency myelopathy); vascular (acute stroke with thrombolysis, TIA); eye (optic neuritis / MS ×2, diabetic 3rd nerve palsy); other (Parkinson disease, Bell palsy, NMS); foodborne botulism (extra case file).

## 1. Core neuro history (every case)

- **Main symptom:** onset (sudden vs gradual), duration, progression, side, distribution, fluctuation (end of day = MG), previous episodes.
- **Headache red flags (SNOOP):** systemic (fever, weight loss, cancer, HIV), neurological deficit, onset sudden / thunderclap, older age > 50, pattern change, positional (worse lying / cough), papilledema, pregnancy / postpartum.
- **Seizure:** aura, tongue bite, incontinence, eye rolling, duration, post-ictal state, Todd paralysis, first or recurrent.
- **Vision:** acuity loss, central vs peripheral, pain on eye movement, color desaturation, diplopia (monocular vs binocular, which gaze), flashes, transient obscurations.
- **Motor / sensory:** weakness pattern (proximal vs distal, ascending vs descending), numbness, sensory level, gait, falls.
- **Bulbar and respiratory:** dysarthria, dysphagia, nasal speech, choking, SOB, orthopnea.
- **Autonomic:** postural dizziness, constipation, urinary retention / incontinence, impotence, sweating.
- **Cognition and mood:** memory, hallucinations, sleep (REM behavior), anosmia.
- **Systemic:** fever, rash, joint pain, oral / genital ulcers (CTD, Behçet), weight loss, cough (TB, cancer).
- **Past history:** stroke / TIA, DM, HTN, AF, IHD, malignancy, thrombosis, miscarriages (APS), autoimmune disease, thyroid, recent surgery or infection, vaccination.
- **Drugs:** OCP, anticoagulants, antiplatelets, antipsychotics / metoclopramide (parkinsonism, NMS), antibiotics and β-blockers (worsen MG), retinoids / tetracycline / steroids (IIH), chemo / isoniazid (neuropathy), zinc supplements (copper deficiency).
- **Family and social:** stroke, MS, epilepsy, thrombophilia, neuromuscular disease; smoking, alcohol, illicit drugs, occupation, driving.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Headache ± seizure in a young woman

- **CVT clues:** OCP, pregnancy / postpartum, previous DVT, miscarriages, dehydration, sinusitis / mastoiditis, IBD, cancer; headache worse lying and with cough; vomiting, blurred vision, seizure, 6th nerve palsy.
- **IIH clues:** obesity, transient visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, diplopia, drugs (retinoids, tetracycline, steroids, vitamin A, lithium), PCOS, hypothyroidism.
- **SAH clues:** thunderclap onset, neck stiffness, collapse.
- **Infection clues:** fever, neck stiffness, rash, travel.

### B. Diplopia / ptosis / fatigable weakness

- **MG:** worse at end of day, better after rest; ptosis; proximal weakness; bulbar and respiratory symptoms.
- **Triggers for crisis:** infection, surgery, stress, drugs (aminoglycosides, quinolones, macrolides, β-blockers, procainamide, magnesium, phenytoin).
- **Lambert-Eaton:** smoker, weight loss, proximal weakness improving with use, dry mouth.
- **3rd nerve palsy:** pain, pupil involvement, DM / HTN, headache (aneurysm), jaw claudication (GCA).

### C. Acute weakness

- **Stroke / TIA:** time last seen well, FAST features, AF, prior stroke; thrombolysis contraindications (recent surgery, GI bleed in 21 days, prior ICH, head trauma, anticoagulants, AVM / tumor).
- **GBS:** ascending, symmetric, preceded by diarrhea (Campylobacter), URTI or vaccination; back pain; facial / bulbar weakness; dyspnea; autonomic instability.
- **Cord:** back pain, sensory level, bladder / bowel involvement.
- **Myopathy / NMJ / metabolic:** low K, low PO4, botulism, snake bite.

### D. Visual loss

- **Optic neuritis:** subacute, painful eye movement, color desaturation, central scotoma; Lhermitte sign; prior episodes of numbness / weakness; family history of MS.
- **NMO:** severe bilateral loss, transverse myelitis, intractable hiccups / vomiting.

### E. Tremor / parkinsonism

- **PD:** asymmetric resting tremor, slowness, stiffness, falls, micrographia, soft voice; non-motor (anosmia, constipation, REM sleep disorder, depression, orthostasis).
- **Secondary / plus:** antidopaminergic drugs, strokes, early falls (PSP), early autonomic failure (MSA), hallucinations / early dementia (DLB).
- **Essential tremor:** action tremor, family history, better with alcohol.

### F. Fever + rigidity + confusion

- **NMS:** recent antipsychotic start / dose increase; rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability, confusion.
- **Mimics:** serotonin syndrome (SSRIs, clonus), malignant hyperthermia (anesthesia), CNS infection, thyroid storm, drugs (cocaine, amphetamines, lithium), withdrawal.

### G. Ataxia / numbness

- **Sensory ataxia:** falls in the dark, positive Romberg, numb feet.
- **Causes:** B12 / copper deficiency (bariatric surgery, celiac, zinc excess), alcohol, DM, paraneoplastic (smoker, weight loss), Sjögren, syphilis, HIV, cervical spondylosis, drugs.

## 3. Cranial nerve examination (examined ×3)

- **CN I:** ask about smell change; test each nostril with a non-irritant (coffee).
- **CN II:** pupils (size, shape, direct and consensual reflex, RAPD); acuity per eye with chart; red desaturation; visual fields in 4 quadrants per eye; mention fundoscopy.
- **CN III, IV, VI:** inspect for ptosis and squint; H-pattern pursuit asking about diplopia; comment on restriction and nystagmus; accommodation.
- **CN V:** sensation in V1, V2, V3; clench teeth (masseter, temporalis); jaw opening against resistance (pterygoids); jaw jerk; mention corneal reflex.
- **CN VII:** ask about taste and hyperacusis; inspect asymmetry and nasolabial folds; raise eyebrows, close eyes tight, puff cheeks, show teeth (forehead spared = UMN).
- **CN VIII:** whisper test; Rinne and Weber.
- **CN IX, X:** palate and uvula on "ahh"; cough; swallow / gag.
- **CN XI:** inspect SCM and trapezius; shrug shoulders and turn head against resistance.
- **CN XII:** tongue at rest for wasting and fasciculation; protrusion (deviates to weak side); push into cheek.
- **Close:** thank patient; summarize; suggest further tests.

## 4. Extrapyramidal (Parkinson) examination

- **Inspection:** hypomimia, reduced blinking, drooling, hypophonic monotone speech, stooped flexed posture, asymmetric resting pill-rolling tremor.
- **Gait:** start hesitation, shuffling short steps, reduced arm swing, festination, en-bloc turning; pull test for postural instability.
- **Upper limbs:** rigidity (cogwheel / lead-pipe, use reinforcement); bradykinesia with decrement (finger tapping, fist opening, pronation-supination); tremor re-emerging with distraction, settling with action; writing (micrographia).
- **Lower limbs:** tone, heel / foot tapping, power and reflexes normal (no pyramidal signs).
- **Exclude Parkinson-plus:** lying-standing BP (MSA); cognition (DLB); cerebellar signs (MSA-C); vertical gaze palsy (PSP); apraxia, asymmetric dystonia (CBD); early falls; poor levodopa response.
- **Cardinal features:** tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability.
- **PD vs essential tremor:** PD = resting, asymmetric, with rigidity and bradykinesia; ET = action / postural, bilateral, better with alcohol.

## 5. Core neuro exam and add-ons

- **General:** vitals (BP, temp, glucose), GCS, orientation, signs of trauma, skin rash, joints.
- **Higher function and speech:** dysphasia, dysarthria, scanning speech.
- **Meningism:** neck stiffness, Kernig, Brudzinski.
- **Fundi:** papilledema (CVT, IIH), optic disc swelling or pallor (optic neuritis), retinopathy.
- **Motor:** bulk, fasciculation, tone, power (proximal vs distal), reflexes, plantars.
- **Sensory:** light touch, pinprick, vibration, joint position, sensory level.
- **Coordination and gait:** finger-nose, heel-shin, dysdiadochokinesia, Romberg, tandem.
- **MG add-ons:** sustained upgaze for 60 s (fatigable ptosis), repeated arm abduction, count to 50, ice-pack test, bedside FVC / NIF.
- **GBS add-ons:** areflexia, symmetric distal-to-proximal weakness, facial / bulbar weakness, FVC / NIF, HR and BP lability.
- **Stroke add-ons:** NIHSS, glucose, AF on pulse, carotid bruit, murmur.
- **NMS add-ons:** temperature, lead-pipe rigidity, autonomic lability, GCS, no clonus (vs serotonin syndrome).

## 6. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### Headache, seizure and raised pressure

#### 1. Cerebral venous thrombosis with hemorrhage (24F, OCP, first seizure)

- **Clues:** sudden severe right temporo-occipital headache 1 day, worse lying and coughing, vomiting, blurred vision; first GTC seizure; married 4 months on OCP; prior unprovoked DVT; right 6th nerve palsy.
- **DDx:** SAH, meningitis, CVT, RCVS, ICH.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation (PT / INR, aPTT); electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, Mg) + glucose; renal profile; liver profile; blood gas; pregnancy test; infection (cultures, ESR, CRP); autoimmune (ANA, ANCA, C3 / C4); thrombophilia (factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, protein C / S, antithrombin — after the acute phase); APS antibodies; imaging (CT + CT venogram, MRI + MR venogram); cancer screen if indicated.
- **Results:** right transverse sinus thrombosis with small hemorrhage and edema.
- **Management:** ABCD; manage raised ICP (head up, mannitol); analgesia and hydration; antiseizure drug; anticoagulate (LMWH / UFH, even with hemorrhage) then oral anticoagulation; stop OCP; neurology.
- **Risk factors:** inherited thrombophilia (factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, protein C / S, antithrombin deficiency); acquired (OCP, pregnancy / puerperium, APS, cancer, infection, head injury, dehydration, IBD).

#### 2. Superior sagittal sinus thrombosis postpartum (37F, known migraine)

- **Clues:** 2 months postpartum; severe frontal throbbing headache different from usual migraine; vomiting, photophobia; normal exam and normal CT.
- **DDx:** CVT, migraine, meningitis, SAH / hemorrhagic infarct, mass, post-traumatic hemorrhage.
- **Workup:** imaging (CT → MRI with MR / CT venography); fundoscopy; CBC; coagulation; renal profile + electrolytes; thrombophilia and APS screen (after acute phase); LP only if meningitis suspected and no raised ICP.
- **Result:** superior sagittal sinus thrombosis on CT venogram.
- **Management:** admit, ABC, analgesia; LMWH / UFH then warfarin / DOAC for 3–6 months (continue even with hemorrhagic infarct); antiseizure drugs if seizures; no steroids; ICP measures; endovascular therapy if deteriorating; avoid estrogen contraception.
- **Examiner Qs:** commonest long-term problem = chronic headache; seizures 10–15%; mortality 5–10%, about 80% recover; CVT vs SAH = subacute headache with seizures / deficits and risk factors vs thunderclap with meningism and positive early CT.

#### 3. Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (30F, bilateral blurred vision + headache)

- **History:** visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, diplopia; headache worse lying / morning; drugs (retinoids, tetracycline, steroids, lithium, vitamin A); thrombosis risk; hypothyroid symptoms.
- **Workup:** CBC + iron studies; renal profile; electrolytes; endocrine (TFT, cortisol, vitamin D, lipids); ANA; drug review (tetracyclines, vitamin A, retinoids, OCP, steroid withdrawal); imaging (CT → MRI brain + MRV to exclude CVT); LP with opening pressure (> 25 cmH2O) and normal CSF; ophthalmology (fundoscopy, OCT, automated perimetry).
- **DDx:** IIH, mass (tumor, abscess), CVT, MS, SLE / vasculitis, hypothyroidism, PCOS.
- **Results:** slit ventricles, flattened posterior globes, distended optic nerve sheaths, tortuous optic nerves, empty sella; papilledema; enlarged blind spot.
- **Management:** education; weight loss; acetazolamide (± furosemide / topiramate); serial LPs if needed; steroids short term only.
- **If vision worsens:** optic nerve sheath fenestration or CSF shunt (LP / VP); neurosurgery.

### Neuromuscular junction

#### 4. Myasthenia gravis → cholinergic crisis (65M, DM, hypothyroid)

- **Clues:** 1 month binocular diplopia at end of day, right ptosis, difficulty climbing stairs, better after sleep; 40 pack-years.
- **DDx:** MG, Lambert-Eaton, botulism, diabetic cranial neuropathy, hypothyroid myopathy.
- **Workup:** CBC; electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, Mg) + glucose; renal profile; liver profile; metabolic / endocrine (TFT, HbA1c); antibodies (AChR, MuSK, LRP4; VGCC if LEMS suspected); neurophysiology (repetitive nerve stimulation, single-fiber EMG); bedside (ice-pack test, FVC / NIF); cardiac (ECG); imaging (CXR, CT / MRI chest for thymoma); cancer screen.
- **Results:** fatigable bilateral ptosis improved by ice pack; AChR antibody positive; > 10% decrement on RNS, jitter on SFEMG; no thymoma.
- **Management:** ABC; pyridostigmine for symptoms; prednisone then steroid-sparing (azathioprine, MMF, rituximab); thymectomy if thymoma; crisis (dyspnea, dysphagia) = ICU, IVIG or plasma exchange; neurology.
- **Cholinergic crisis:** after extra pyridostigmine → weakness plus diarrhea, abdominal pain, salivation, bradycardia, miosis; give atropine and reduce / stop pyridostigmine.

#### 5. Myasthenic crisis after surgery (71M, prostate surgery 10 days ago)

- **Clues:** recent diplopia investigated by GP; progressive dyspnea, pooled oral secretions, accessory muscle use, cannot maintain SpO2; proximal weakness 3/5; IHD.
- **DDx:** MG, GBS / Miller Fisher, MS, ALS, botulism, brainstem stroke / tumor, Lambert-Eaton, severe low K / PO4, snake envenomation, COPD / pneumonia.
- **Workup:** bedside respiratory (FVC, NIF, single-breath count); blood gas; CBC; coagulation; renal profile; electrolytes (K, Mg, PO4, Ca); liver profile; glucose; infection (ESR, CRP, procalcitonin, blood / urine cultures, urinalysis, CXR); cardiac (ECG); antibodies (AChR, MuSK); NCS with RNS; imaging (CT / MRI head and chest).
- **Results:** on face mask FiO2 0.4 — pH 7.32; PaO2 12.4 kPa (≈ 93 mmHg); PaCO2 5.9 kPa (≈ 44 mmHg — normal value in a tiring patient = danger); HCO3 28 mmol/L; lactate 1.2 mmol/L; BE +4.9; K 4.6 mmol/L.
- **Management:** ICU; ABC; VC and MIP every 2 h, intubate if VC < 20 mL/kg or MIP weaker than −30 cmH2O; hold pyridostigmine while ventilated; IVIG or plasma exchange; treat trigger; neurology; DVT and GI prophylaxis.
- **Crisis definition:** MG exacerbation causing neuromuscular respiratory failure.
- **Triggers:** infection, surgery, stress, drugs (aminoglycosides, FQ, macrolides, β-blockers, anesthetics).
- **Complications:** respiratory failure, arrhythmia, aspiration pneumonia, stress ulcer, VTE, pressure sores, death.

### Weakness and neuropathy

#### 6. Guillain-Barré syndrome (AIDP) after vaccination (27M)

- **Clues:** 2 days ascending leg numbness and weakness; vaccination 1 month ago; absent ankle reflexes, foot drop.
- **DDx:** GBS, transverse myelitis, cord compression, infectious neuropathy (HIV, CMV, botulism), vascular myelopathy.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; electrolytes (K, Mg, PO4); metabolic / endocrine (B12, TFT, glucose / HbA1c, CK); autoimmune and infection (ANA, HIV, hepatitis, stool Campylobacter, anti-ganglioside antibodies — GQ1b for Miller Fisher); LP (protein, cells, glucose); neurophysiology (NCS / EMG); imaging (MRI spine); respiratory (FVC, NIF).
- **Results:** high CSF protein with normal cells; slowed conduction and prolonged F waves; normal MRI.
- **Management:** monitored bed; FVC, NIF, SpO2 every 4–6 h; intubate if FVC < 15–20 mL/kg or NIF weaker than −30; DVT prophylaxis, bladder / bowel / skin care; IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 days or plasma exchange × 5; no steroids; physio, OT, rehab.
- **Causes:** Campylobacter (commonest), CMV, EBV, HIV, influenza, Mycoplasma, Zika, vaccination, surgery.
- **ICU if:** rapid progression, bulbar weakness, falling FVC / NIF, autonomic instability.
- **Prognosis:** about 80% recover fully; 5–10% residual deficit; mortality ≈ 5%.
- **Variants:** AIDP, AMAN, AMSAN, Miller Fisher (ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, areflexia; anti-GQ1b), Bickerstaff encephalitis.

#### 7. Copper-deficiency myelopathy from celiac disease (55M, 80 pack-years)

- **Clues:** 3 months progressive imbalance, falls, cane; numb feet and fingertips; 11 kg weight loss; cachexia; positive Romberg; absent vibration and joint position in legs; mild hip / knee flexion weakness; brisk arm reflexes; flexor plantars.
- **Localization:** dorsal columns ± lateral columns of the spinal cord (sensory ataxia with pyramidal signs).
- **DDx:** paraneoplastic neuronopathy / myelopathy, CIDP / Sjögren / demyelination, neoplasm, B12 / copper deficiency, cervical spondylosis, toxins (pyridoxine excess, chemo), infections (syphilis, HIV, CMV).
- **Workup:** CBC (MCV); vitamins and trace elements (B12 + methylmalonic acid if low-normal, folate, vitamin E, copper, ceruloplasmin, zinc); celiac serology (TTG-IgA); SPEP with immunofixation; infection (HIV, syphilis); metabolic (HbA1c); autoimmune (ANA, anti-DNA); paraneoplastic antibodies + cancer screen (80 pack-years); neurophysiology (NCS / EMG, SSEP); imaging (MRI spine); CSF analysis.
- **Results:** subtle dorsal column T2 signal; abnormal SSEPs; microcytic anemia; B12 normal 573 pg/mL (≈ 423 pmol/L; normal 211–946) → copper and zinc checked (copper low); TTG very high; duodenal biopsy villous atrophy.
- **Diagnosis:** subacute combined degeneration-like myelopathy from copper deficiency due to celiac disease.
- **Causes of copper deficiency:** gastric bypass / gastrectomy, IBD / celiac / CF, zinc excess (supplements, denture cream), chelators, long TPN, poor diet.
- **Management:** IV then oral copper; gluten-free diet; iron replacement.

### Vascular

#### 8. Acute ischemic stroke in a young woman — right M1 occlusion (25F)

- **Approach:** FAST → code stroke; ABC; BP monitor; glucose; ECG; two 18G lines; urgent CT to exclude hemorrhage; history and exam in parallel.
- **History:** last seen well (90 min); seizure / syncope; head trauma, prior stroke or ICH, tumor / AVM / aneurysm, intracranial or spinal surgery, GI bleed in 21 days, recent major surgery; family history; autoimmune / hematological disease.
- **Exam:** GCS, cranial nerves, motor, sensory, gait, NIHSS.
- **Workup:** bedside glucose first; imaging (non-contrast CT → CT angiography ± perfusion); CBC; coagulation (INR, aPTT); renal profile (before contrast); electrolytes; troponin; ECG; pregnancy test; young-stroke screen later (echo with bubble, Holter, thrombophilia — protein C / S, antithrombin, lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin; toxicology; autoimmune — ANA, dsDNA, ANCA; HbA1c, lipids).
- **Result:** NIHSS 10; CTA right M1 MCA occlusion = large-vessel occlusion.
- **Management:** IV alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg; 10% bolus, rest over 1 h) + mechanical thrombectomy.
- **First 24 h:** ICU / stroke unit; BP < 180/105 after lysis (< 185/110 before); neuro checks every 15 min × 2 h, 30 min × 6 h, hourly × 16 h; no antithrombotics for 24 h; avoid lines / Foley if possible; pneumatic compression; repeat CT if headache or deterioration.
- **Young stroke workup:** echo ± TEE (PFO), carotid / vertebral Doppler, MRI / MRA brain and neck, thrombophilia and APS screen, vasculitis screen.

#### 9. TIA (52M, right hemiparesis for 20 minutes)

- **History:** onset, duration, speech / facial / sensory symptoms, previous episodes, head trauma, HTN, DM, AF, IHD, stroke, anticoagulants, smoking.
- **Exam:** vitals, consciousness, cranial nerves, motor, sensory, cerebellar, NIHSS.
- **Workup:** imaging (MRI with DWI or CT; CTA / MRA head and neck; carotid Doppler); CBC; renal profile + electrolytes; coagulation; glucose; metabolic (HbA1c, lipid profile, TSH); cardiac (ECG, troponin, Holter / prolonged monitoring, echo).
- **Results:** HbA1c 9% (75 mmol/mol); LDL 100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L); CT and MRI no infarct.
- **ABCD2:** Age ≥ 60 (1); BP ≥ 140/90 (1); Clinical — weakness 2, speech only 1; Duration ≥ 60 min 2, 10–59 min 1; Diabetes 1 → here 5 = moderate risk.
- **Management:** admit; DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) for 21 days then single agent; high-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80); BP < 140/90; HbA1c < 7%; stop smoking, diet, exercise.
- **Anticoagulate instead if:** AF or other cardioembolic source.
- **TIA vs minor stroke:** TIA resolves (usually within minutes–hours) with no infarct on imaging; minor stroke persists or shows infarct.
- **Carotid revascularization:** symptomatic 70–99%; symptomatic 50–69% with high-risk features; asymptomatic 70–99% with life expectancy ≥ 5 years.
- **Crescendo TIA:** repeated TIAs with increasing frequency — unstable plaque or cardioembolic source, urgent workup.

### Eye and cranial nerves

#### 10. Optic neuritis from multiple sclerosis (30M; 32F — two stations)

- **Clues:** subacute monocular blurred vision ± pain on movement, flashes; tingling in right arm and leg over a month; fatigue; aunt with MS.
- **Exam:** acuity, color vision, RAPD, visual fields (central scotoma), fundoscopy; other cranial nerves, motor, sensory, cerebellar, gait.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile + electrolytes; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); vitamin D; mimics (ANA, ANCA, cryoglobulins, syphilis, B12); antibodies (AQP4-IgG, MOG-IgG); imaging (MRI brain + orbits + whole spine with gadolinium); LP (cells, protein, oligoclonal bands with paired serum); neurophysiology (visual evoked potentials); ophthalmology (OCT, visual fields).
- **Results:** CBC, renal, LFT normal; ESR 33 mm/h; vitamin D low; ANA, ANCA, cryoglobulins negative; MRI periventricular / juxtacortical white-matter lesions; CSF raised protein with oligoclonal bands (not in serum); delayed P100 on VEP; AQP4 negative.
- **DDx:** MS optic neuritis, NMO, vasculitis, B12 deficiency.
- **Typical MS MRI regions:** periventricular, cortical / juxtacortical, infratentorial, spinal cord.
- **Treat relapse if disabling:** vision loss, diplopia, weakness, cerebellar symptoms.
- **Management:** neurology; exclude infection; IV methylprednisolone pulse; calcium / vitamin D; vaccinations; education; disease-modifying therapy (natalizumab, ocrelizumab, dimethyl fumarate, fingolimod; older interferon, glatiramer); symptomatic care (fatigue, spasticity, pain, bladder, mood).
- **MS phenotypes:** clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting (commonest), secondary progressive, primary progressive.

#### 11. Bell palsy (34F, sudden right facial weakness)

- **Clues:** 1 day right facial weakness incl. forehead, can't close eye, hyperacusis; URTI a week ago; on OCP; isolated right LMN 7th palsy.
- **Red flags to ask:** ear vesicles / pain (Ramsay Hunt), other deficits, gradual onset, bilateral, recurrent, hearing loss / vertigo.
- **DDx:** Bell palsy, Ramsay Hunt, stroke (forehead spared), MS, CP-angle tumor, basal meningitis, sarcoid, CTD, Lyme.
- **Workup:** clinical diagnosis; basic labs (CBC, renal profile, liver profile, electrolytes, glucose, HbA1c, ESR / CRP); infection (Lyme serology if endemic, HIV, VZV if vesicles); autoimmune if CTD suspected (ANA, ANCA, complement); imaging (MRI with gadolinium only if red flags or no recovery by 3–4 months).
- **Management:** prednisolone within 72 h (± antiviral); eye care (lubricant, tape / patch at night); analgesia; facial physiotherapy; neurology.
- **Risk factors:** DM, HTN, obesity, pregnancy (3rd trimester) and early postpartum, recent viral infection.
- **Prognosis:** 80–90% recover in 6 weeks to 3 months; ≈ 40% complete recovery if ≥ 60 years.
- **Sequelae:** exposure keratitis, synkinesis, taste loss, cosmetic asymmetry.

#### 12. Pupil-sparing diabetic 3rd nerve palsy (65M, DM, HTN, prior stroke)

- **Clues:** acute painful diplopia with left ptosis; eye down and out; pupil spared; HbA1c 11%; BP 165/90.
- **DDx:** diabetic (microvascular) 3rd nerve palsy, PComm aneurysm, mass, MG, giant cell arteritis.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; electrolytes; metabolic (glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP — giant cell arteritis if > 50); imaging (CTA / MRA to exclude posterior communicating aneurysm, MRI brain / orbits); visual fields; MG tests if fluctuating.
- **Results:** HbA1c 11% (97 mmol/mol); CTA / MRA no aneurysm.
- **Management:** strict glucose (HbA1c < 7%), BP, lipids, secondary stroke prevention; analgesia; patch or prisms; reassure — recovers in 3–5 months; return if pupil dilates, pain worsens or new deficits.
- **Mechanism:** ischemia of central fibers; pupillary fibers run peripherally (pial supply) and are spared.
- **Ischemic vs compressive:** ischemic = pupil spared, recovers; compressive (PComm aneurysm, tumor) = dilated fixed pupil, progressive, emergency.
- **Cavernous sinus nerves:** III, IV, V1, V2, VI.
- **Other causes of cranial neuropathies:** DM, HTN, vasculitis, sarcoid, MS, TB, syphilis.

### Movement and toxicity

#### 13. Idiopathic Parkinson disease (62F, hand tremor)

- **Clues:** years of shoulder pain and anosmia; right resting tremor 2 years then left; slow gait, stiffness, recent falls; no antidopaminergic drugs.
- **Exam:** hypomimia, unintelligible speech, bilateral pill-rolling tremor, rigidity R > L, bradykinesia, flexed shuffling gait; normal eye movements; no ataxia; no orthostatic drop.
- **DDx:** idiopathic PD, essential tremor, PSP, MSA, secondary parkinsonism.
- **Workup:** clinical diagnosis; exclude secondary causes (CBC, electrolytes, glucose, renal profile, liver profile, TFT, B12; ceruloplasmin if < 40 years); drug review (antipsychotics, metoclopramide); imaging (MRI brain; DaT scan if unclear).
- **Management:** neurology; levodopa-carbidopa; dopamine agonists; MAO-B inhibitors; COMT inhibitors; amantadine; anticholinergics (young, tremor).
- **Dopamine agonist side effects:** impulse control disorders, sleep attacks, hallucinations / confusion, nausea, leg edema.
- **Levodopa side effects:** wearing-off, dyskinesia, hallucinations, nausea, orthostatic hypotension.
- **Pathology:** loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta with Lewy bodies (α-synuclein).

#### 14. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (75M, schizophrenia, dose increased last week)

- **Clues:** found febrile and immobile, not recognizing son, urine-soaked; T 40, HR 120, BP 160/90, RR 26; catatonic; lead-pipe rigidity.
- **DDx:** NMS, CNS infection, malignant hyperthermia, serotonin syndrome, malignant catatonia, CNS vasculitis, thyroid storm, drug toxicity (cocaine, amphetamines, ecstasy, lithium), withdrawal.
- **Workup:** CBC (leukocytosis); muscle injury (CK, LDH, AST, urine myoglobin); renal profile (creatinine, BUN — AKI); electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, PO4, Mg); blood gas + lactate (metabolic acidosis); septic screen (blood / urine cultures, CXR); TFT; toxicology; drug levels (lithium); imaging (CT brain); LP if meningitis / encephalitis possible.
- **Results:** temperature 40 °C (104 °F); BP 160/90 mmHg; HR 120/min; RR 26/min; rigid, mute; expected CK markedly raised with leukocytosis.
- **Management:** ICU; stop the antipsychotic; cardiorespiratory support; IV fluids and electrolytes; cooling; benzodiazepines; dantrolene, bromocriptine or amantadine; BP control (clonidine / nitroprusside); DVT prophylaxis.

### Toxin-mediated weakness (extra case file)

#### 15. Foodborne botulism (28M, sausage pasta at a restaurant)

- **Clues:** 1 day of nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, then sudden bilateral symmetric descending weakness (cranial nerves → trunk and arms → legs); blurred vision, diplopia, ptosis, dysphagia, dysarthria, facial weakness, fixed dilated pupils possible; urinary retention and constipation; alert with normal sensation; afebrile.
- **History asks:** food eaten (home-canned, sausage, fermented fish), others affected; wound or injected drug use; cosmetic botulinum toxin; dyspnea (diaphragm); fever (usually absent); vaccines; drugs.
- **Exam:** vitals and respiratory effort (single-breath count, FVC); cranial nerves; tone and power (flaccid, descending); reflexes normal or reduced; sensation normal; mental status normal; abdomen.
- **DDx:** myasthenia gravis; Guillain-Barré (Miller Fisher variant — ascending, areflexic, sensory, high CSF protein); brainstem stroke; tick paralysis; heavy metal / organophosphate poisoning; poliomyelitis; Lambert-Eaton.
- **Workup:** CBC normal; electrolytes, glucose, CK; CSF normal (vs GBS); EMG / NCS — reduced compound muscle action potential amplitude with increment on high-frequency stimulation; imaging (CT / MRI brain normal); confirmation (toxin assay / culture of serum, stool, gastric fluid and the suspected food).
- **Management:** ICU with serial FVC; intubation for respiratory failure; give heptavalent equine botulinum antitoxin (BAT, one vial IV) on clinical suspicion — do not wait for confirmation; infants get human botulism immune globulin; notify public health (outbreak); supportive care.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Intubation thresholds (MG / GBS):** FVC < 15–20 mL/kg, NIF weaker than −30 cmH2O, rising PaCO2, bulbar failure.
- **IVIG vs plasma exchange:** both work in MG crisis and GBS; steroids don't help GBS.
- **Anticoagulate CVT even with hemorrhage.**
- **Thrombolysis window:** alteplase within 4.5 h of last seen well; thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion (up to 24 h with imaging selection).
- **Drugs that worsen MG:** aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, β-blockers, magnesium, procainamide, neuromuscular blockers.
