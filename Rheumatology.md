# Rheumatology

## How to use this

Rheumatology stations are either a "systemic" history (fever, rash, weakness, Raynaud, cytopenia in a young woman) or a joint / spine exam (hand exam for RA, back exam for axial SpA, plus knee, shoulder and hip exams). The systemic stations all use the same CTD-screen history and head-to-toe exam with a urine dipstick, so learn that block once.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; vitals; BMI; always finish the exam with a urine dipstick and offer fundoscopy.
- **Stations covered:** scleroderma renal crisis; SSc with ILD (Raynaud); adult-onset Still disease; dermatomyositis; IgA vasculitis (HSP); EGPA; active SLE with ITP; SLE with TTP and seizure; Sjögren with distal RTA; RA (history + hand exam, and hand exam ×3); psoriatic arthritis; ankylosing spondylitis (history ×2, back exam ×3).; knee exam (acute monoarthritis / gout), shoulder exam, hip exam (extra checklists).

## 1. Core rheumatology history (every case)

- **Presenting complaint:** onset, duration, course, pattern, severity and effect on daily activity, aggravating / relieving factors, previous similar episodes, response to analgesics.
- **Constitutional:** fever (documented, pattern, response to antipyretics), weight loss, night sweats, fatigue, anorexia.
- **CTD screen:** joint pain / swelling, morning stiffness; malar / discoid rash, photosensitivity; hair loss; oral / genital ulcers; sicca (dry eyes / mouth); Raynaud; skin tightness, dysphagia / heartburn; serositis (pleuritic chest pain); muscle weakness; red painful eyes; thrombosis and miscarriages.
- **Systemic review:** CNS (LOC, seizure, headache, psychosis, weakness, numbness, visual loss); respiratory (SOB, cough, hemoptysis, sinusitis); cardiac (chest pain, palpitations, edema); GI (abdominal pain, N/V, bloody diarrhea, dysphagia, GERD); renal (hematuria, frothy urine, reduced output, edema); hematologic (bleeding, bruising, nodes).
- **Infection mimics:** sick contacts, recent URTI / gastroenteritis, raw milk (brucella), TB contact, travel, sexual history, transfusion.
- **Past history:** known autoimmune disease (SLE, RA, APS, Sjögren, psoriasis, IBD, AS) — when diagnosed, organs involved, flares, admissions; DM, HTN, CKD, thyroid.
- **Drugs:** analgesics / NSAIDs, steroids, DMARDs / immunosuppressants and adherence, HCQ, aspirin / anticoagulants, statins, new drugs (drug-induced lupus / vasculitis), allergies.
- **Obstetric:** miscarriages, preeclampsia, preterm birth, neonatal lupus / congenital heart block in a child, contraception.
- **Family and social:** autoimmune disease, psoriasis, IBD, AS; smoking, alcohol, occupation, socioeconomic state.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Raynaud / skin tightness

- **Raynaud:** triphasic color change (white → blue → red), cold / stress trigger, painful, unilateral vs bilateral, digital ulcers.
- **Secondary clues:** skin tightness (hands only vs proximal / trunk), dysphagia, heartburn, exertional dyspnea (ILD / PH), rash, joints.
- **Other causes:** vibrating tools, vasoconstrictors (beta blockers, ergots), hypothyroidism, smoking, paraproteinemia.
- **Renal crisis red flags:** new severe headache, blurred vision, seizures, reduced urine; recent steroids; diffuse disease.

### B. Fever + joint pain

- **Fever:** documented, daily pattern (quotidian evening spikes = AOSD), response to antipyretics, sick contacts, raw milk, URTI.
- **Joints:** distribution, symmetry, small vs large, morning stiffness, swelling.
- **Rash:** evanescent salmon-pink rash with fever (AOSD), malar (SLE), psoriasis.
- **Sore throat, nodes, abdominal pain:** AOSD, lymphoma, viral infection.

### C. Proximal weakness

- **Weakness:** proximal (combing hair, climbing stairs, rising from a chair) vs distal; painful vs painless; fatigability; diurnal variation; dysphagia; diplopia.
- **Rash:** facial photosensitive rash, heliotrope, hands (Gottron, mechanic's hands).
- **Lung:** dyspnea, cough (ILD in antisynthetase syndrome).
- **Mimics:** hypothyroidism, Cushing (steroids), statins, hypokalemia (episodic paralysis, diarrhea, diuretics), myasthenia (fatigable, ptosis), GBS (ascending), malignancy (DM is paraneoplastic in older adults).

### D. Purpuric rash (vasculitis)

- **Rash:** onset, site (legs), palpable, painful / itchy, ulcers, pigmentation.
- **Trigger:** recent URTI (IgA vasculitis), new drugs, hepatitis B / C.
- **Organs:** abdominal pain, GI bleeding; hematuria, frothy urine; joint pain; asthma, sinusitis, nasal crusting, hemoptysis; foot drop / numbness (mononeuritis multiplex); testicular pain.

### E. Back pain

- **Inflammatory pattern:** age < 40, insidious, > 3 months, morning stiffness > 30 min, improves with activity not rest, night pain (second half), alternating buttock pain, good NSAID response.
- **Mechanical / red flags:** trauma, fever, weight loss, sphincter or leg weakness, TB contact, raw milk (brucella spondylitis), cancer.
- **SpA features:** dactylitis, enthesitis (heel), peripheral arthritis, uveitis, psoriasis, IBD symptoms, recent GI / GU infection, urethritis, family history.

### F. Known SLE with new problem

- **Flare:** fatigue, fever, rash, hair loss, ulcers, arthritis, serositis, edema, frothy urine.
- **Bleeding / cytopenia:** epistaxis, gum bleeding, bruising, melena, hematuria; anticoagulants / aspirin.
- **Neuro:** seizure (onset, type, tongue bite, incontinence, post-ictal), headache, confusion, psychosis, visual loss.
- **Treatment:** current drugs, adherence, recent changes.

## 3. Core systemic exam (every "medical" rheumatology station)

- **General:** look, vitals (BP crucial — renal crisis, nephritis), temperature, BMI.
- **Head and neck:** pallor, jaundice, oral ulcers, dry mouth / dental caries, parotids, eyes (redness, Schirmer), cervical and axillary nodes, nasal / ear lesions.
- **Skin:** describe rash (distribution, size, color, palpable or not, ulcers), hair, nails; malar rash, livedo reticularis, purpura, psoriasis.
- **Joints:** signs of active arthritis (swelling, warmth, tenderness, deformity, limited movement); hands, enthesitis, dactylitis.
- **Neuro:** LOC, cranial nerves, tone, power, reflexes, sensation, meningeal signs, fundoscopy.
- **Chest:** breathing pattern, basal crackles (ILD), effusion, pleural rub.
- **CVS:** murmurs, rub, JVP, HF signs, loud P2 (PH), edema; pulses in all limbs, radio-radial and radio-femoral delay.
- **Abdomen:** tenderness, hepatosplenomegaly, ascites, PR exam (GI vasculitis).
- **Renal:** flank tenderness, uremic signs, fluid overload, urine dipstick.

## 4. Exam add-ons and dedicated exam stations

### Hand exam (RA station, 50–60 marks)

- **Start:** wash hands, introduce, explain, consent; expose hands, wrists and elbows; sit with hands on a pillow; ask about pain.
- **Look — general:** walking aids, splints, other joints.
- **Look — dorsum:** posture, ulnar deviation, MCP / wrist subluxation, swelling, skin (bruising, thinning from steroids, psoriatic plaques), Z-thumb, swan neck, boutonnière, Heberden (DIP) / Bouchard (PIP) nodes, interossei wasting, scars, nails (pitting, onycholysis, splinter hemorrhages).
- **Look — palms:** claw hand, contracture, scars (carpal tunnel release), palmar erythema, Dupuytren thickening, thenar / hypothenar wasting, Janeway / Osler lesions.
- **Feel — palms up:** temperature, radial / ulnar pulses, thenar / hypothenar bulk, palmar fascia, median and ulnar sensation.
- **Feel — palms down:** radial sensation (first web space), temperature over wrist, MCPs, elbows; MCP squeeze test; bimanual palpation of MCP, PIP, DIP, CMC, wrist, anatomical snuffbox; elbows for nodules / plaques.
- **Move:** active then passive finger and wrist flexion / extension, normal side first if painful.
- **Motor:** radial (finger and wrist extension), ulnar (finger abduction), median (thumb abduction).
- **Function:** power grip, pincer grip, pick up a coin; undo a button / write.
- **Special tests:** Tinel and Phalen for carpal tunnel.
- **Close:** thank and cover; offer neurovascular exam of the upper limbs, elbow and shoulder exam, and a full systemic exam.
- **Examiner Qs:** DDx RA, PsA, SLE (Jaccoud), OA; investigations = hand X-ray (then US / MRI), RF, anti-CCP, ANA, ESR / CRP, aspiration if indicated; RA X-ray = soft tissue swelling, periarticular osteopenia, joint space narrowing, marginal erosions (late subluxation / ankylosis).

### Back / spine exam (AS station, 70 marks)

- **Start:** introduce, confirm name, explain, consent, wash hands, expose the upper body, stand the patient, ask about pain.
- **Inspection:** general (habitus, aids, wasting); anterior (scars, shoulder asymmetry, pelvic tilt); lateral (cervical lordosis, thoracic kyphosis — question-mark posture, loss of lumbar lordosis); posterior (scoliosis, wasting, scars).
- **Gait:** limping, Trendelenburg, waddling.
- **Palpation:** spinous processes, interspinous ligaments, paraspinal muscles, SI joints.
- **Movement (active then passive):** cervical flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation; lumbar flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation.
- **Special tests:** occiput-to-wall distance; chest expansion (< 2.5 cm abnormal); modified Schober (< 5 cm increase abnormal); straight leg raise; femoral stretch; SI joint tests (FABER, Gaenslen, compression).
- **Complete with:** other joints, enthesitis (Achilles), dactylitis, nails and skin (psoriasis, keratoderma blennorrhagicum, circinate balanitis), eyes (uveitis), oral ulcers, lung apices (apical fibrosis), aortic regurgitation murmur, neuro exam of the legs.
- **Examiner Qs:** DDx AS, PsA, reactive arthritis, IBD arthropathy, brucellosis, TB; X-ray changes in AS = SI erosions / sclerosis → ankylosis, squaring of vertebrae, shiny corners, syndesmophytes, ligament calcification → bamboo spine.

### Knee exam (station: 45M, right knee pain and limited movement for 2 weeks)

- **Start:** wash hands, introduce, confirm name and DOB, explain, consent; expose both lower limbs; stand the patient first; ask about pain.
- **Look — general:** gait (antalgic), walking aids, other joints, psoriasis.
- **Look — knee:** anterior (scars, bruising, swelling, patella position, valgus / varus); lateral (fixed flexion, hyperextension); posterior (scars, popliteal swelling — Baker cyst); quadriceps wasting.
- **Feel:** temperature (compare sides); quadriceps bulk; joint lines, patella, quadriceps tendon, tibial tuberosity, fibular head; popliteal fossa (cyst, aneurysm).
- **Effusion tests:** sweep / bulge test (small); patellar tap (moderate–large); ballottement.
- **Move:** active then passive flexion and extension, normal side first; hyperextension.
- **Special tests:** anterior drawer and Lachman (ACL); posterior drawer / sag (PCL); valgus stress (MCL); varus stress (LCL); McMurray (menisci).
- **Close:** screen other joints; examine hip and ankle; neurovascular exam of the leg; thank and cover.
- **DDx (acute monoarthritis):** septic arthritis; gout; pseudogout (CPPD); reactive arthritis; hemarthrosis; systemic arthritis (RA, SLE, SpA); trauma (ligament / meniscus).
- **Workup:** joint aspiration (cell count and differential, Gram stain and culture, polarized microscopy for crystals); CBC; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); coagulation (PT / aPTT / INR) if hemarthrosis; uric acid; immunology (ANA, RF, anti-CCP, anti-dsDNA); imaging (X-ray both knees).
- **Acute gout treatment:** counsel; rest the joint, ice; NSAID if no contraindication or colchicine; otherwise steroid (intra-articular, IM or oral); IL-1 inhibitor (anakinra / canakinumab) if all contraindicated; low-purine diet, less alcohol and fructose; continue allopurinol if already on it; start urate-lowering later if recurrent attacks, tophi, CKD or stones (target urate < 6 mg/dL = 360 µmol/L).

### Shoulder exam (station: 60M, right shoulder pain and limited movement for 2 years)

- **Start (WIPER):** wash hands, introduce, confirm details, permission, position, pain, expose the upper body, stand on the patient's right; examine joints above and below; normal side first.
- **Inspect (front, side, back):** posture and symmetry; arm position (abducted + externally rotated = anterior dislocation; adducted + internally rotated = posterior); SC joint, clavicle, AC joint; skin (scars, bruising, swelling, sinuses); wasting (deltoid — squared shoulder, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, pectorals); winged scapula (push against wall — long thoracic nerve); axilla (nodes, effusion, bruising).
- **Palpate (skin → muscle / tendon → bone):** temperature, swelling; SC joint → clavicle → AC joint → greater and lesser tuberosities → glenohumeral joint → spine of scapula → coracoid; muscle bulk and crepitus; biceps tendon in the bicipital groove ("Popeye" sign = long-head rupture); supraspinatus insertion; watch the face.
- **Move (active, demonstrate first):** flexion 180°; extension 65°; abduction 180°; adduction 50°; external rotation 80° (elbows at sides, flexed 90° — lost early in adhesive capsulitis, < 30°); internal rotation (hand behind back to scapula, T6–T7 — Apley scratch); scapular movement; then passive in all directions feeling for crepitus.
- **Rotator cuff tests:** empty can / Jobe (supraspinatus); painful arc 60–120° (supraspinatus impingement); external rotation against resistance (infraspinatus, teres minor); external rotation in abduction (teres minor); Gerber lift-off (subscapularis).
- **Impingement:** Neer (passive full flexion with internal rotation); Hawkins-Kennedy (90° flexion + passive internal rotation).
- **AC joint:** scarf test (cross-body adduction → pain / crepitus at AC joint).
- **Instability:** apprehension test (abduction + external rotation) → relocation test → release; sulcus sign (inferior); anterior / posterior drawer of the humeral head.
- **Biceps:** Speed test (resisted forward flexion with elbow extended and supinated); Yergason test (resisted supination with elbow flexed 90°).
- **Close:** examine cervical spine and elbow; distal neurovascular exam; thank and cover; present findings.
- **DDx:** rotator cuff disorder (tendinitis, tear); glenohumeral disease (OA, crystals, RA, SpA, adhesive capsulitis); AC joint disease; referred pain (cervical spine, cardiac, diaphragm); trauma, infection (rare).
- **Workup:** imaging (shoulder X-ray — degeneration, crystals, erosions; ultrasound then MRI); US-guided aspiration (cells, crystals, culture) if effusion; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); immunology (RF, ACPA, HLA-B27); CBC, renal, liver profile.
- **Management (degenerative):** education; relative rest; NSAIDs if safe; physiotherapy (range of motion, strengthening, ice); intra-articular / subacromial steroid injection; orthopedics for arthroplasty or cuff repair.

### Hip exam (station: 55M, hip pain for 2 months)

- **Start:** wash hands, introduce, confirm details, explain, consent; expose lower limbs; stand first; ask about pain.
- **Look:** general inspection; lower limbs from front, side and back standing (scars, wasting, pelvic tilt, lumbar lordosis, leg rotation / shortening); gait (antalgic, Trendelenburg); repeat inspection lying.
- **Feel:** hip temperature; greater trochanter tenderness (trochanteric bursitis); apparent leg length (umbilicus → medial malleolus) and true leg length (ASIS → medial malleolus).
- **Move:** active flexion / extension; passive flexion (normal ≈ 120°); internal and external rotation with hip and knee at 90° (internal rotation lost first in OA); abduction and adduction (stabilize the pelvis); extension lying prone.
- **Special tests:** Thomas test (fixed flexion deformity — flex the opposite hip fully, the tested thigh lifts off the bed); Trendelenburg test (stand on the affected leg — pelvis drops on the opposite side = weak gluteus medius / hip pathology).
- **Close:** neurovascular exam of both legs; examine the lumbar spine and knee; imaging.
- **DDx:** hip OA; inflammatory arthritis (RA, AS); avascular necrosis (steroids, alcohol, sickle cell); trochanteric bursitis; septic arthritis; fracture; referred pain from lumbar spine (radiculopathy) or SI joint; meralgia paresthetica; hernia.

### Other exam add-ons

- **Scleroderma:** skin tightness and its extent (beyond MCPs / elbows / knees = diffuse), sclerodactyly, digital ulcers / pitting scars, calcinosis, telangiectasia, microstomia, nailfold capillaroscopy, tendon friction rub; basal crackles; loud P2; BP and fundus.
- **Dermatomyositis:** heliotrope rash, Gottron papules / sign, shawl and V signs, mechanic's hands; proximal power, tone, reflexes (normal), no fasciculation; swallow; crackles.
- **Sjögren:** dry mouth, caries, parotid enlargement, Schirmer test, nodes / spleen (lymphoma).
- **Vasculitis:** palpable purpura, livedo, ulcers, nasal crusting / saddle nose, wheeze, foot drop / sensory loss, BP, dipstick.

## 5. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → key positives → workup and results → diagnosis → management → examiner's questions.

### Scleroderma

#### 1. Scleroderma renal crisis (35F, diffuse SSc for 1 year, sudden headache)

- **Clues:** diffuse SSc (face, arms, chest), Raynaud, GERD; on aspirin, nifedipine, esomeprazole; first severe throbbing headache with blurred vision; BP 210/130, HR 110; sclerodactyly, telangiectasia, tendon friction rub; flame hemorrhages on fundoscopy; dipstick protein +++, blood +++.
- **DDx:** diffuse SSc with renal crisis; TTP; HUS; other MAHA.
- **Workup:** CBC + peripheral smear; hemolysis panel (LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, retics); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); urine (urinalysis with microscopy, 24-h protein or UPCR); immunology (ANA, anti-dsDNA, Scl-70, anticentromere, anti-RNA polymerase III, anti-U1 RNP, aPL, C3 / C4); mimics (glucose, HbA1c, TSH, FT4, HBV, HCV, HIV); imaging (CXR, HRCT, echo, CT brain + MRA / MRV, renal Doppler US); fundoscopy.
- **Results:** BP 210/130 mmHg; WBC 2.9 ×10⁹/L; Hb 12 g/dL (120 g/L); platelets 90 ×10⁹/L; schistocytes; creatinine 350 µmol/L (≈ 4.0 mg/dL); urea normal; ESR 60 mm/h; CRP 50 mg/L; ANA 1:320 nucleolar; Scl-70 and anti-RNA polymerase III positive; anti-dsDNA, anticentromere, aPL negative; C3 / C4 normal; 24-h protein 1 g; urine 15 RBC/hpf; imaging, glucose, TSH, viral serology normal; fundus flame hemorrhages.
- **Diagnosis:** diffuse systemic sclerosis with scleroderma renal crisis (with MAHA).
- **Management:** admit; rheumatology, nephrology and hematology consults; captopril 6.25 mg, then 12.5 mg, titrate up to 300 mg/day; target SBP fall of 20 mmHg in 24 h; follow Hb, platelets, creatinine, LDH, proteinuria; add CCB (amlodipine) or bosentan if BP still high; dialysis if needed; ACEi continued even on dialysis; avoid high-dose steroids.

#### 2. Systemic sclerosis with ILD (30F, painful finger color change with cold for 6 months)

- **History:** triphasic color change, fingertip ulcer, skin tightness of hands / face, rash, joints, oral ulcers, SOB, chest pain, heartburn / regurgitation, uni- vs bilateral, vibrating tools, vasoconstrictor drugs, hypothyroidism, known rheumatic disease, smoking.
- **Clues:** bilateral asymmetric painful attacks, one fingertip ulcer, tight skin of the dorsum of hands, exertional dyspnea.
- **Exam:** vitals; skin tightness beyond the MCPs and its extent; arthritis; face (tightness, telangiectasia, small mouth); hands (ulcers, sclerodactyly, capillaroscopy); lung bases; loud P2.
- **Findings:** tightness extending to trunk and legs, sclerodactyly, bibasal crackles.
- **DDx:** systemic sclerosis, SLE, vasculitis, paraproteinemia.
- **Workup:** CBC; liver profile; renal profile + urinalysis + BP (renal crisis); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); immunology (ANA, anti-topoisomerase / Scl-70, anticentromere, anti-RNA polymerase III, anti-Jo-1, anti-RNP); cardiopulmonary (echo for PASP, PFT with DLCO, 6MWT, NT-proBNP); imaging (CXR, HRCT); nailfold capillaroscopy.
- **Results:** anemia, ANA positive, anti-topoisomerase positive, ILD on CT; PFT restrictive.
- **Diagnosis:** diffuse systemic sclerosis with interstitial lung disease.
- **Renal crisis risk factors:** diffuse skin disease (early, rapidly progressive), glucocorticoids (prednisone > 15 mg), anti-RNA polymerase III, cyclosporine, large-joint contractures, new anemia, new cardiac events (HF, pericardial effusion).
- **Management:** avoid cold, keep hands warm, stop smoking; anti-reflux measures (small meals, nothing 2–3 h before bed, raise the head of the bed) + PPI; CCB for Raynaud (then PDE5i, iloprost for ulcers); PFT; pulmonary consult.
- **SSc-ILD:** low-dose steroid + mycophenolate; if no response → tocilizumab or rituximab; nintedanib for progressive fibrosis.

### Fever, rash and weakness

#### 3. Adult-onset Still disease (21F, fever and joint pain for 2 weeks)

- **Clues:** documented afternoon fever 39 not responding to antipyretics; sweating, fatigue; wrist and PIP arthritis starting with the fever; T 40, HR 110; left axillary node; rash (salmon-pink evanescent); hepatosplenomegaly; dipstick negative.
- **DDx:** AOSD, SLE, early RA, PsA, viral infection (hepatitis, HIV, EBV), lymphoma, brucella, TB.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential); renal profile; liver profile + albumin; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP, ferritin — glycosylated fraction, LDH); immunology (ANA, RF, anti-CCP, anti-dsDNA, C3 / C4); urine (urinalysis with microscopy, UPCR); coagulation + aPL (MAS — fibrinogen, triglycerides); infection (blood cultures, HBV, HCV, HIV, EBV, QuantiFERON, Brucella titer); imaging (CXR, echo, CT chest / abdomen / pelvis); node biopsy if lymphoma suspected.
- **Results:** temperature 40 °C; WBC 13 ×10⁹/L (neutrophilia); Hb 10 g/dL (100 g/L); MCV 90 fL; platelets 400 ×10⁹/L; renal normal; AST 120, ALT 150 U/L; albumin and bilirubin normal; ESR 90 mm/h; CRP 50 mg/L; ANA, RF, anti-CCP, anti-dsDNA negative; C3 / C4 normal; ferritin 2,000 µg/L (ng/mL); LDH normal; urine normal; CXR normal; CT hepatosplenomegaly, no lymphadenopathy; PT / PTT normal; viral serology, QuantiFERON and Brucella negative.
- **Diagnosis:** adult-onset Still disease (Yamaguchi criteria — diagnosis of exclusion).
- **Management:** admit; rheumatology; prednisone 1 mg/kg/day; methotrexate as steroid sparing; IL-1 (anakinra, canakinumab) or IL-6 (tocilizumab) if refractory; watch for macrophage activation syndrome (falling ESR, cytopenias, ferritin rising, high TG, low fibrinogen).

#### 4. Dermatomyositis (42F, weakness and malaise 2 months)

- **Clues:** progressive symmetric proximal weakness (combing hair, stairs); photosensitive facial rash; hand rash; mild dyspnea; arthralgia; proximal power 3/5, normal tone and reflexes, no UMN signs or fasciculation.
- **DDx:** idiopathic inflammatory myopathy (dermatomyositis), SLE, hypothyroidism.
- **Workup:** muscle enzymes (CK, LDH, AST / ALT, aldolase); CBC; electrolytes (K, Ca, Mg, PO4); renal profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); TFT; imaging (CXR → HRCT and PFT for ILD; muscle MRI); EMG; muscle biopsy; antibodies (myositis-specific — Jo-1, Mi-2, MDA5, TIF1-γ, NXP2, SRP, HMGCR; ANA); malignancy screen (age-appropriate + CT CAP); swallowing assessment.
- **Results:** WBC 7.8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 12.5 g/dL (125 g/L); platelets 310 ×10⁹/L; ESR 40 mm/h; CRP negative; CK 3,000 U/L (≈ 15× ULN); LFT, creatinine, chemistry normal; TSH / T3 / T4 normal; CXR normal; proximal power 3/5 with normal reflexes.
- **Further:** muscle MRI (edema), EMG (membrane irritability), muscle biopsy (CD4 infiltrate, capillary injury, perifascicular atrophy), myositis-specific / associated antibodies (anti-Jo-1, Mi-2, MDA5, TIF1-γ, NXP2).
- **Diagnosis:** dermatomyositis.
- **Management:** prednisone 1 mg/kg for 4–6 weeks then taper; steroid-sparing azathioprine or methotrexate (IVIG / rituximab if refractory); calcium, vitamin D, PPI; rheumatology; physiotherapy; education; screen for malignancy (age-appropriate + CT CAP) and ILD (HRCT, PFT).

#### 5. IgA vasculitis / HSP (17M, leg rash 5 days)

- **Clues:** URTI last month; abdominal pain and nausea; knee arthralgia; BP 140/82; violaceous palpable purpura on legs and feet; epigastric tenderness; dipstick protein ++, blood +.
- **DDx:** IgA vasculitis, SLE, other vasculitis, viral infection.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); urine (urinalysis with microscopy, 24-h protein or UPCR); immunology (ANA, anti-dsDNA, ANCA, serum IgA, C3 / C4); infection (HBV, HCV, HIV, ASO); coagulation + smear + hemolysis panel (exclude TTP / ITP); imaging (renal US with Doppler, abdominal X-ray / US for intussusception); skin biopsy with immunofluorescence.
- **Results:** WBC 7.1 ×10⁹/L; Hb 12.5 g/dL; platelets 210 ×10⁹/L (normal — non-thrombocytopenic purpura); ESR 19 mm/h; CRP negative; ANA, anti-dsDNA, ANCA negative; IgA moderately raised; urine scanty RBC casts, 24-h protein 300 mg; LFT, creatinine normal; coagulation normal; abdominal and renal US normal; HBV, HCV, HIV negative; skin biopsy IgA deposits.
- **Diagnosis:** IgA vasculitis (HSP) with mild IgA nephropathy.
- **Renal biopsy:** not needed (normal creatinine, protein < 500 mg); biopsy if rising creatinine, nephrotic range or persistent proteinuria.
- **Management:** admit; analgesia (avoid NSAIDs with renal involvement); ACEi for proteinuria; prednisolone 1 mg/kg for severe GI / renal disease, azathioprine as needed; DVT and GI prophylaxis; rheumatology, nephrology, dermatology; education; follow urine and BP.

#### 6. EGPA (42F, asthma 10 years, sinus congestion, foot drop, purpura)

- **Clues:** worsening asthma and sinusitis; 3 months of right foot tingling and weakness; leg purpura; bilateral wheeze; right foot drop.
- **DDx:** EGPA, GPA, MPA, hypereosinophilic syndrome, IgA vasculitis, PAN, diabetic / compressive neuropathy.
- **Workup:** CBC with absolute eosinophils; renal profile + urinalysis with microscopy; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); coagulation + smear; immunology (ANA, anti-dsDNA, c-ANCA / PR3, p-ANCA / MPO, IgE, IgA); infection (HBV, HCV, HIV, stool for Strongyloides); cardiac (troponin, ECG, echo — cardiac involvement = poor prognosis); imaging (CXR, CT chest, CT sinuses); PFT; neurophysiology (nerve conduction); tissue (skin / nerve biopsy).
- **Results:** WBC 15 ×10⁹/L (15,000/µL); eosinophils 4.0 ×10⁹/L (4,000/µL; > 1.5 = hypereosinophilia); ESR 50 mm/h; CRP raised; MPO-ANCA (p-ANCA) positive; CT patchy infiltrates; nerve conduction axonal neuropathy (mononeuritis multiplex).
- **Diagnosis:** eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis.
- **Biopsy:** when the diagnosis is uncertain, especially ANCA negative (only 30–40% are ANCA positive); skin, nerve or lung; shows eosinophil-rich granulomatous necrotizing vasculitis.
- **Management:** rheumatology, pulmonology, neurology, dermatology; induction high-dose steroids ± cyclophosphamide or rituximab if organ / life-threatening; maintenance azathioprine, methotrexate or rituximab; mepolizumab for relapsing / refractory non-severe disease; inhalers; PJP prophylaxis with cyclophosphamide.
- **Skin:** palpable purpura (legs, commonest), urticarial / erythematous rash, subcutaneous nodules, livedo.
- **Prognosis:** 5-year survival > 90%; worse with cardiac involvement, GI vasculitis, renal impairment (Five-Factor Score).

### SLE and Sjögren

#### 7. Active SLE with immune thrombocytopenia (39F, fatigue and epistaxis)

- **Clues:** SLE for 2 years on HCQ only; fatigue 3 weeks, hair fall, arthralgia, malar rash; one small spontaneous nosebleed; malar rash, PIP arthritis; dipstick normal.
- **Workup:** CBC + peripheral smear; coagulation; renal profile; liver profile + bilirubin; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); lupus activity (anti-dsDNA, C3 / C4); APS screen; urine (urinalysis with microscopy, 24-h protein or UPCR); hemolysis (LDH, retics, DAT); ferritin (MAS); viral screen (HIV, HCV, HBV); imaging (renal Doppler US).
- **Results:** WBC 3.5 ×10⁹/L with lymphopenia; Hb 12.5 g/dL (125 g/L); platelets 80 ×10⁹/L; ESR 40 mm/h; CRP negative; anti-dsDNA high; C3 and C4 low; ferritin 800 µg/L; urine and 24-h protein normal; LFT, creatinine normal; coagulation normal; smear normal (no schistocytes); APS and viral serology negative; renal Doppler normal.
- **Diagnosis:** active SLE with immune thrombocytopenia.
- **Activity scores:** SLEDAI, BILAG, CLASI (skin).
- **Management:** outpatient; education; rheumatology (flare, step up immunosuppression); hematology (exclude other causes); steroids (prednisolone or dexamethasone); IVIG if active bleeding, before surgery or steroid intolerance; then TPO receptor agonist, rituximab, splenectomy; mycophenolate; continue HCQ.

#### 8. SLE with TTP and seizure (23F, class III lupus nephritis, fever and seizure)

- **Clues:** fever 39.5 for a week; generalized tonic-clonic seizure with tongue bite and incontinence; headache, confusion; wrist arthritis; dark urine; non-adherent; GCS 13, febrile, hypertensive, tachycardic; malar rash, pallor, mild jaundice, hepatosplenomegaly; dipstick protein +, blood +.
- **DDx:** SLE flare with neuropsychiatric lupus; TTP / HUS; hypertensive encephalopathy / PRES; APS; CNS infection; metabolic; drug-induced seizure.
- **Workup:** CBC + peripheral smear (schistocytes); hemolysis panel (LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, retics, DAT); coagulation (PT, PTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer) + ADAMTS13 activity; renal profile + electrolytes + glucose; liver profile + albumin; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP, ferritin); lupus activity (anti-dsDNA, C3 / C4); APS serology; urine (urinalysis with microscopy, UPCR, ACR); infection (blood / urine cultures, CMV, EBV, HIV, HBV, HCV, TB, Brucella); LP; imaging (MRI brain with contrast ± MRA / MRV, echo); EEG; fundoscopy.
- **Diagnosis:** thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura in a patient with SLE.
- **Management:** ICU; neurology, rheumatology, hematology; ABC; IV benzodiazepine then levetiracetam load; PLASMIC score while ADAMTS13 is pending; urgent daily plasma exchange until platelets and LDH normalize (ADAMTS13 < 10%); hold ACEi before PLEX (bradykinin → hypotension); IV methylprednisolone pulses; rituximab / caplacizumab per hematology; RBC transfusion as needed, platelets only for life-threatening bleeding; dialysis if needed; control BP with CCB / beta blocker; continue HCQ; renal biopsy when safe.

#### 9. Sjögren syndrome with distal RTA (36F, 1 day of weakness, joint pain)

- **Clues:** 9 months of small-joint hand pain and sicca; daughter with congenital heart block; HR 120, RR 25; bilateral non-tender parotid enlargement; power 2/5 proximal and distal, generalized hyporeflexia; no rash.
- **DDx:** RA, autoimmune myopathy, Sjögren, neuromuscular (MG, MS), hypothyroidism, hypokalemic paralysis.
- **Exam add-ons:** ECG (U waves, ST depression, long QT), mouth dryness and caries, Schirmer test, parotids, nodes.
- **Workup:** electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, Mg, Cl → anion gap); blood gas (VBG — pH, HCO3); urine (pH, urine anion gap, urinalysis); renal profile; CK; CBC; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); TSH; immunology (ANA, anti-SSA / Ro, anti-SSB / La, RF, anti-CCP, anti-Jo-1, C3 / C4, SPEP, cryoglobulins); ECG; neurophysiology (EMG / NCS); minor salivary gland biopsy; renal US (nephrocalcinosis).
- **Results:** HR 120, RR 25; CBC normal; ESR 40 mm/h; CRP positive; LFT and renal normal; CK and TSH normal; K 1.9 mmol/L (1.9 mEq/L); VBG normal anion gap (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis; SSA and SSB positive; ANA and RF negative; ECG ST depression, U waves, long QT; lip biopsy lymphocytic infiltration.
- **Diagnosis:** primary Sjögren syndrome with distal (type 1) RTA and hypokalemic paralysis.
- **Management:** admit (cardiac monitor); rheumatology and nephrology; IV potassium (correct K before bicarbonate); oral alkali (potassium citrate); corticosteroids; methotrexate or rituximab if indicated; artificial tears and saliva, pilocarpine.
- **Lymphoma risk factors:** persistent parotid enlargement, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, cryoglobulins, positive RF, low C4, purpura / vasculitis.
- **Renal complications:** tubulointerstitial nephritis, distal RTA, glomerulonephritis (MPGN with cryoglobulins), CKD; also nephrogenic DI.
- **Pregnancy with anti-Ro / La:** congenital heart block (1–5%) and neonatal lupus; serial fetal echo / PR interval weeks 16–26; HCQ reduces CHB risk; continue safe drugs; preconception planning; neonatal check after delivery.

### Inflammatory arthritis

#### 10. Seropositive rheumatoid arthritis (40F, 2 months of small-joint pain)

- **History asks:** additive vs migratory pattern; number, symmetry, distribution; morning stiffness; NSAID response; inflammatory back pain, dactylitis, enthesitis; deformity; constitutional; psoriasis, malar rash, Raynaud, ulcers, sicca, red eyes; cough / dyspnea; IBD symptoms; hypothyroidism; drugs; family history; miscarriages; smoking, occupation.
- **Clues:** symmetric additive MCP / PIP / wrist arthritis; 2-hour morning stiffness; partial response to diclofenac; smoker; hypothyroid; no deformity, rash or nodules.
- **DDx:** RA, PsA, SLE arthritis, viral (parvovirus), hypothyroid arthropathy.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile (before methotrexate); electrolytes; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); immunology (RF, anti-CCP, ANA, anti-dsDNA, C3 / C4); TFT; vitamin D, uric acid; imaging (X-ray hands / wrists / feet; US / MRI for synovitis); pre-DMARD screen (HBV, HCV, HIV, TB — IGRA / PPD, CXR).
- **Results:** ESR 65 mm/h; CRP high; RF positive; anti-CCP strongly positive; ANA negative; CBC, renal, LFT normal; X-ray early periarticular osteopenia, no erosions.
- **Diagnosis:** seropositive rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Management:** education, stop smoking, rheumatology; physio / OT; vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, HBV); NSAIDs and short low-dose steroid bridge; methotrexate 10–15 mg weekly escalating with folic acid (alternatives leflunomide, sulfasalazine, HCQ); biologic (anti-TNF) or JAK inhibitor if inadequate response; treat to target (remission / low activity, DAS28 every 1–3 months); pre-treatment screening and baseline CBC, LFT, renal; manage CV risk.
- **Most specific antibody:** anti-CCP.
- **Poor prognosis:** many joints (> 6), high RF / anti-CCP titers, early erosions, extra-articular disease, high ESR / CRP, poor function.
- **Extra-articular:** nodules, episcleritis / scleritis, sicca, ILD, pleural effusion, pericarditis, vasculitis, Felty syndrome (RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia), anemia, amyloidosis.
- **Methotrexate toxicity:** hepatotoxicity, marrow suppression, pneumonitis, mucositis, teratogenic.
- **Start biologics:** no improvement by 3 months or target not reached by 6 months on csDMARDs (especially with poor prognostic factors).

#### 11. Psoriatic arthritis (21M, psoriasis 3 years, low back pain 6 months)

- **Clues:** morning back pain with 1-hour stiffness, better with activity, radiating to the right buttock; right 3rd / 4th DIP arthritis; active psoriatic plaques on elbows; nail pitting; positive FABER and Gaenslen; BMI 30.
- **DDx:** PsA, AS, reactive arthritis, brucellosis, TB, HIV-associated.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile + albumin; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); immunology (ANA, RF, anti-CCP, anti-dsDNA, HLA-B27); urinalysis; imaging (X-ray hands; X-ray SI joints — Ferguson view; MRI SI joints; CXR; echo); comorbidity (lipid profile, glucose, uric acid); infection (HBV, HCV, HIV, EBV, QuantiFERON, Brucella titer).
- **Results:** WBC 8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 14 g/dL; platelets 400 ×10⁹/L; renal and LFT normal; ESR 90 mm/h; CRP 50 mg/L; ANA, RF, anti-CCP, anti-dsDNA negative; urine normal; CXR normal; hand X-ray pencil-in-cup DIP erosions; MRI active sacroiliitis; viral serology, QuantiFERON and Brucella negative; BMI 30.
- **Diagnosis:** psoriatic arthritis (axial + DIP).
- **Management:** outpatient; rheumatology and dermatology; physio and weight loss; NSAIDs; methotrexate with folic acid (peripheral disease), leflunomide or apremilast; anti-TNF (etanercept, adalimumab, infliximab); IL-17 (secukinumab, ixekizumab); IL-12/23 (ustekinumab); for axial disease csDMARDs don't work — go to anti-TNF / IL-17.

#### 12. Ankylosing spondylitis (27M resident, back pain > 3 months) — with biologic counseling

- **History asks:** back pain analysis; weakness, numbness, sphincters; other joints, dactylitis, enthesitis; fever, weight loss; rash, oral / genital ulcers, eye symptoms; recent infection; IBD; TB contact; raw milk; family psoriasis; NSAIDs; trauma; sexual history, smoking; work / sport.
- **Clues:** inflammatory back pain with > 30 min stiffness improving with activity; no extra-articular features.
- **Exam:** vitals, BMI, eyes, skin / mucosa (psoriasis, keratoderma, balanitis, nails), joints, enthesitis, dactylitis; full back exam (section 4); neuro exam.
- **DDx:** AS, reactive arthritis, PsA, IBD arthropathy, brucellosis.
- **Workup:** CBC; liver profile; renal profile + urinalysis; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); HLA-B27; infection (Brucella titer, HBV, HCV, HIV, QuantiFERON — before anti-TNF); imaging (X-ray SI joints → MRI SI joints).
- **Results:** SI X-ray shows ankylosis of the SI joints.
- **Diagnosis:** ankylosing spondylitis (radiographic axial SpA).
- **Management:** outpatient; rheumatology; NSAIDs (2 for 4 weeks each); anti-TNF if NSAIDs fail; IL-17 or JAK inhibitors; physiotherapy and exercise.
- **Biologic counseling:** infection and TB reactivation risk; SC every 2 weeks (adalimumab); dose, duration and injection technique; allergy and side effects with leaflet; ask about HF, lung disease, demyelination, thrombosis (JAKi), malignancy; vaccines before starting (influenza, pneumococcal, recombinant zoster); pregnancy / breastfeeding.

#### 13. Axial SpA with back exam (26M, morning stiffness > 1 hour for 3 years)

- **Task:** back exam (section 4), then DDx AS, PsA, reactive arthritis, IBD arthropathy.
- **Workup:** inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); immunology (RF, ACPA, HLA-B27); imaging (X-ray SI joints, lumbar and thoracic spine → MRI SI joints and thoracolumbar spine).
- **Modified New York criteria (asked as "ASAS"):** clinical — low back pain ≥ 3 months improving with exercise not rest; limited lumbar movement in sagittal and frontal planes; reduced chest expansion for age / sex; radiologic — bilateral grade 2–4 or unilateral grade 3–4 sacroiliitis; AS = radiologic + ≥ 1 clinical.
- **ASAS axial SpA (for completeness):** back pain ≥ 3 months, onset < 45; sacroiliitis on imaging + ≥ 1 SpA feature, or HLA-B27 + ≥ 2 SpA features.
- **Management:** education; NSAIDs; rheumatology; TNF inhibitor, IL-17 inhibitor, JAK inhibitor.

#### 14. Ankylosing spondylitis — non-radiographic start (21M, chronic back pain)

- **Clues:** 3 months of morning pain with 1-hour stiffness improving with activity; no other joints, psoriasis, infection, TB contact or raw milk.
- **Workup:** CBC; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile + urinalysis; liver profile; immunology (HLA-B27, ANA, RF, anti-CCP); infection (TB, Brucella, HBV, HCV); imaging (X-ray SI joints, MRI SI joints, CXR, echo for AR).
- **Results:** high inflammatory markers; seronegative; infections negative; MRI SI bone marrow edema, more on the right = active sacroiliitis.
- **Diagnosis:** ankylosing spondylitis (axial SpA).
- **Management:** education and exercise / physiotherapy; NSAIDs; anti-TNF or IL-17 if NSAIDs fail; screen before biologics.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Pre-biologic checklist:** TB (IGRA / TST + CXR), HBV / HCV / HIV, CBC, LFT, renal; vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, recombinant zoster, no live vaccines during therapy); contraindications (active infection, HF for anti-TNF, demyelination, malignancy, thrombosis for JAKi, pregnancy).
- **MAHA in a CTD patient:** schistocytes + low platelets → think TTP (ADAMTS13), HUS, scleroderma renal crisis (very high BP), catastrophic APS, malignant HTN.
- **Antibody associations:** Scl-70 = diffuse SSc + ILD; anticentromere = limited SSc + PAH; RNA polymerase III = renal crisis; anti-Jo-1 = antisynthetase (ILD, mechanic's hands); anti-CCP = RA; SSA / SSB = Sjögren, neonatal lupus / CHB; anti-dsDNA and low complement = active SLE / nephritis.
- **Seronegative SpA features:** inflammatory back pain, enthesitis, dactylitis, uveitis, psoriasis, IBD, reactive (post GI / GU infection), HLA-B27.
- **Always exclude in Saudi stations:** brucellosis (raw milk) and TB before calling it autoimmune.
