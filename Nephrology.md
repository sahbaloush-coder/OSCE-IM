# Nephrology

## How to use this

All 18 nephrology stations in the Qassim 2021–2025 set run on one skeleton: core history, then the add-on block for your top differentials, then the general exam plus its add-on signs. Learn the core once; the marks that separate candidates sit in the add-ons and the examiner's follow-up questions.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; confirm name and age; in an unstable patient say ABC / MOVE first (Monitor, O2, IV line + labs, ECG); ask for vitals and a glucose.
- **Rest of history:** PAM-FOSS; Past medical/surgical incl. blood transfusion; Allergies; Medications; Family; Occupation; Social; Sexual.
- **Close every management answer:** ANERVVES; Admission; Nutrition; Education; Referral; Vaccination; VTE prophylaxis; Exercise; Smoking; plus nephrology referral.
- **Stations covered:** AKI (AIN ×2, gentamicin ATN, rhabdomyolysis); GN (IgA ×2, infection-related GN, lupus nephritis IV); nephrotic (MCD); CKD (ESKD counseling, CKD + HFrEF problem list); dialysis disequilibrium; Na disorders (thiazide hyponatremia, SIADH, hypernatremia); secondary HTN (Conn's, FMD/RAS, ADPKD).

## 1. Core nephrology history (ask in every case)

- **Presenting complaint:** onset, duration, course, previous similar episodes.
- **Urine:** output (oliguria; last 24 h volume if inpatient), color (red vs cola), clots, frothy urine, dysuria, frequency, flank pain, weak stream / dribbling.
- **Nephrotic / overload:** puffy face, leg swelling, weight gain, abdominal distension, exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, PND.
- **Nephritic / BP:** headache, dizziness, blurred vision, SOB, known high BP.
- **Uremia:** nausea, vomiting, anorexia, itching, fatigue, confusion / reduced LOC, pleuritic chest pain (pericarditis), peripheral neuropathy, bleeding.
- **Volume depletion:** poor oral intake, vomiting, diarrhea, bleeding, hypotension episodes, postural dizziness.
- **Infection:** fever, recent sore throat / URTI, skin infection, cough + sputum, night sweats, weight loss.
- **Autoimmune screen:** joint pain/swelling, malar or other rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, hair loss, Raynaud's, red eyes, ENT/sinus problems, hemoptysis, pleurisy, purpura.
- **Malignancy screen:** weight loss, anorexia, fatigue, neck masses / lymph nodes, breast lump.
- **Comorbidities:** DM (duration, control, retinopathy, neuropathy), HTN, CKD (baseline creatinine), HF, liver cirrhosis, hepatitis B/C, HIV, stones, BPH, SCD.
- **Drugs (the big list):** NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, diuretics, SGLT2i, PPI, antibiotics (aminoglycosides, penicillins, cephalosporins, vancomycin), recent IV contrast, lithium, herbal / gum arabic, illicit drugs (cocaine, amphetamines, heroin).
- **Past history:** previous admissions, surgery, procedures / arterial catheterization, blood transfusion, tattoos.
- **Family:** kidney disease, PKD, autoimmune disease (lupus), HTN, malignancy.
- **Social:** smoking, alcohol, IV drugs, sexual history, travel, occupation (chemical / benzene exposure).

## 2. History add-ons by differential

### A. AKI / rising creatinine

- **Always get:** baseline creatinine, urine output over 24 h, weight.
- **Pre-renal:** vomiting, diarrhea, poor intake, bleeding, hypotension overnight / fluid boluses, sepsis or septic shock, HF, cirrhosis, diuretics, ACEi/ARB, NSAIDs.
- **ATN — ischemic:** shock, sepsis, prolonged hypotension, recent surgery.
- **ATN — nephrotoxic:** aminoglycosides (gentamicin: dose, duration, levels); new hearing loss or tinnitus = ototoxicity; vancomycin; amphotericin; cisplatin; IV contrast.
- **AIN:** new drug days to weeks before (penicillins incl. Augmentin, cephalosporins, sulfonamides, PPI, NSAIDs); triad of fever, rash, arthralgia; also infection (UTI).
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** extreme exertion in heat; trauma / crush; prolonged immobilization or fall; seizure; muscle pain, cramps, weakness, dark (cola) urine; statins, cocaine, amphetamines, alcohol; heat stroke; viral illness; NMS / serotonin syndrome / malignant hyperthermia (antipsychotics, SSRIs, anesthesia).
- **GN / RPGN:** hematuria, puffy face, recent sore throat or skin infection, hemoptysis (pulmonary-renal), rash, joint pain, ENT symptoms.
- **Post-renal / obstruction:** BPH symptoms (weak stream, dribbling, retention), stones, pelvic or prostatic malignancy, blocked catheter, anuria.
- **Atheroembolic:** recent angiography / arterial catheterization, livedo, blue toes.
- **Abdominal compartment (ICU):** tense abdomen, massive ascites.

### B. Hematuria / nephritic

- **Is it true blood?** food, drugs (rifampicin), hemolysis or SCD (dark urine), myoglobin (muscle pain, exertion), menstruation, bleeding from other orifices, anticoagulants.
- **Timing after infection:** within 1–3 days of URTI = IgA (synpharyngitic); 1–3 weeks after sore throat or skin infection = post-infectious GN.
- **IgA:** recurrent episodes of gross (cola) hematuria with URTIs, flank discomfort, young male.
- **Infection-related GN:** current fever, sore throat, productive cough, oliguria.
- **Lupus nephritis:** young female, arthralgia, alopecia, photosensitive rash, oral ulcers, Raynaud's, serositis, seizures, miscarriages (APS).
- **ANCA vasculitis / anti-GBM:** hemoptysis, sinusitis, epistaxis, red eyes, hearing loss, purpura, neuropathy, smoking (anti-GBM).
- **TMA (TTP/HUS):** bloody diarrhea, bruising / petechiae, fever, confusion.
- **Cryoglobulinemia / MPGN:** hepatitis C, purpura, arthralgia.
- **Non-glomerular:** stones (renal colic); UTI (fever, dysuria); bladder or renal cancer (age > 40, painless gross hematuria with clots, smoking, benzene / aniline dyes, cyclophosphamide or chemotherapy); PKD; trauma.

### C. Nephrotic syndrome

- **Analysis of edema:** onset, uni- vs bilateral, puffy face in the morning, extent (ascites, scrotal, pleural).
- **Exclude other edema:** HF symptoms, liver disease, DVT, drugs (amlodipine).
- **Complications:** thrombosis (DVT, PE, renal vein, cerebral venous thrombosis), infections, hyperlipidemia.
- **MCD triggers:** NSAIDs, ampicillin, recent URTI / allergy / immunization, Hodgkin or non-Hodgkin lymphoma, leukemia.
- **Membranous triggers:** solid cancers (age-appropriate screening), hepatitis B, SLE, drugs (penicillamine, gold, NSAIDs).
- **FSGS triggers:** obesity, HIV, heroin, sickle cell, reflux, reduced nephron mass.
- **Diabetic kidney disease:** long DM, retinopathy (DKD without retinopathy is unusual), neuropathy.
- **Others:** amyloid / myeloma (bone pain, age > 50), syphilis, HCV.

### D. CKD / ESKD

- **CKD story:** cause, duration, how diagnosed (microalbuminuria → proteinuria → falling GFR), last creatinine / eGFR, follow-up, compliance, missed appointments.
- **Cause hunt:** DM, HTN, GN, PKD (family history), obstruction (stones, BPH, recurrent UTI), autoimmune disease, drugs.
- **Complications:** uremia (nausea, anorexia, itching, pericarditis, encephalopathy); overload; anemia (fatigue, SOB, bleeding, melena); bone disease (bone pain, fractures); acidosis; hyperkalemia (weakness, palpitations).
- **Comorbidity control:** DM (HbA1c, eye, nerve, foot ulcers, gastroparesis), HTN, HF (NYHA, last echo, ACS / PCI / CABG).
- **Meds review:** metformin, sitagliptin dose, NSAIDs, SGLT2i, ACEi/ARB, calcitriol, insulin, herbal (gum arabic).
- **RRT readiness:** previous dialysis catheter, AV fistula, transplant, PD, patient preference.

### E. Dialysis patient with neuro symptoms (seizure / confusion)

- **Timing:** first or early dialysis session; symptoms during or hours after HD = dialysis disequilibrium.
- **DDS prodrome:** headache, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, muscle cramps, tremor, blurred vision, confusion.
- **Seizure description:** aura, LOC, eye rolling, frothing, tongue bite, incontinence, post-ictal confusion, Todd's paralysis, time to recover.
- **Other causes:** uremic encephalopathy (missed dialysis); hypertensive encephalopathy (very high BP); stroke (focal deficit); CNS infection (catheter, fever, neck stiffness); hypoglycemia (insulin); electrolytes (Na, Ca — hyperparathyroidism); drugs.

### F. Secondary hypertension (young, severe or resistant HTN)

- **HTN story:** onset, previous readings, home BP, compliance, target-organ damage (headache, chest pain, SOB, visual changes, focal deficit, hematuria, oliguria), hypertensive emergencies.
- **Renal parenchymal / PKD:** family history of kidney disease, hematuria, flank pain, UTIs, stones, cramps, itching.
- **Renovascular (RAS / FMD):** young woman (FMD) or older vasculopath (atherosclerotic), flash pulmonary edema, AKI on ACEi/ARB, abdominal bruit.
- **Primary aldosteronism:** hypokalemia symptoms (muscle weakness, cramps, paresthesia, polyuria), family history of early HTN or stroke.
- **Pheochromocytoma:** episodic headache, sweating, palpitations, pallor, panic attacks.
- **Cushing's:** weight gain, striae, easy bruising, proximal weakness, bone pain.
- **Thyroid:** weight loss, heat intolerance, diarrhea, tremor, eye protrusion.
- **Acromegaly:** headaches, sweating, ring / shoe size change, visual field loss.
- **Coarctation:** HF symptoms, leg claudication.
- **OSA:** snoring, witnessed apnea, daytime sleepiness.
- **Drugs / substances:** steroids, NSAIDs, OCP, CNIs (cyclosporine), TCAs, decongestants, weight-loss pills, cocaine, amphetamines, alcohol, caffeine, salt.
- **Women:** menstrual cycle, pregnancy, pre-eclampsia.

### G. Hyponatremia

- **Symptoms / severity:** headache, nausea, vomiting, confusion, sleepiness, seizures, falls.
- **Hypovolemic:** vomiting, diarrhea, poor intake, diuretics (thiazide just started = classic), adrenal insufficiency (stopping steroids), salt-wasting.
- **Euvolemic (SIADH):** drugs (SSRIs, TCAs, carbamazepine, valproate, antipsychotics, cyclophosphamide); CNS disease (stroke, bleed, infection, trauma); lung disease (pneumonia, mass — smoker with weight loss = small cell); malignancy; pain / nausea / surgery; hypothyroidism (cold intolerance, weight gain); cortisol deficiency.
- **Hypervolemic:** HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, advanced CKD.
- **Water intake:** psychogenic polydipsia, beer potomania, tea-and-toast diet.
- **ODS risk:** alcoholism, malnutrition, liver disease, hypokalemia, Na < 105.

### H. Hypernatremia

- **Access to water:** elderly, bedbound, dependent, impaired thirst, dementia (commonest cause in elderly).
- **Extrarenal loss:** vomiting, diarrhea, sweating, fever, burns.
- **Renal loss:** loop diuretics; osmotic diuresis (hyperglycemia, mannitol); central DI (head trauma, surgery); nephrogenic DI (lithium, amphotericin B, demeclocycline, hypercalcemia, hypokalemia).
- **Iatrogenic:** hypertonic saline, sodium bicarbonate.

### I. Altered mental status in a renal / electrolyte patient

- **CNS:** headache, focal deficit, slurred speech, seizure, neck stiffness, photophobia.
- **Metabolic:** glucose (sulfonylurea, insulin, polyuria / polydipsia); Na; Ca (tetany, perioral numbness, malignancy, hyperparathyroidism); uremia; liver failure (acetaminophen, jaundice); hypothyroidism.
- **Drugs:** sedatives, SSRIs, antihistamines, anticholinergics, quinolones, steroids, alcohol.
- **Infection source:** chest, urine, abdomen, skin, bed sores.

## 3. General nephrology exam (every case)

- **HMF + general look:** ill / well, distress, GCS and orientation, body build and BMI, pallor, jaundice, cyanosis, uremic tinge, scratch marks.
- **Vitals:** BP (both arms in HTN, lying and standing for postural drop), HR and rhythm, RR, temperature, SpO2, blood glucose.
- **Volume status:** JVP, mucous membranes, skin turgor, capillary refill, postural BP, peripheral and sacral edema, daily weight and urine output.
- **Face / hands:** periorbital puffiness, malar rash, oral ulcers, alopecia, asterixis, AV fistula / graft (thrill, bruit), dialysis catheter scar.
- **CVS:** apex (displaced = LVH / HF), S3, S4, murmurs, pericardial rub.
- **Chest:** basal crackles (overload), coarse crackles / bronchial breathing (pneumonia, pulmonary hemorrhage), effusion.
- **Abdomen:** ballotable / polycystic kidneys, transplant scar (iliac fossa), renal or aortic bruit, palpable bladder, flank and suprapubic tenderness, liver and spleen, ascites (shifting dullness, fluid thrill), PD catheter.
- **Lower limbs:** pitting edema (level), DVT signs, livedo reticularis, rash / purpura.
- **Skin and joints:** rash (maculopapular, purpuric, malar), active synovitis.
- **Fundoscopy:** hypertensive and diabetic retinopathy.
- **Bedside:** urine dipstick (blood, protein, leukocytes, nitrites), ECG if K+ is a concern.

## 4. Exam add-ons by differential

- **AKI in ICU:** mental status / GCS; nose and chest for pulmonary-renal syndrome; tense abdomen or massive ascites → measure intra-abdominal pressure; skin rash (AIN, vasculitis); palpable bladder (obstruction); dipstick.
- **Pre-renal / hypovolemia:** tachycardia, postural hypotension, low JVP, dry mucosa, reduced turgor.
- **AIN:** fever, maculopapular rash over trunk / limbs.
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** muscle tenderness and swelling; power and reflexes (weakness, hyporeflexia); compartment syndrome; temperature (heat stroke); skin hot / dry / sweaty; signs of trauma; ECG for peaked T.
- **Atheroembolic:** livedo reticularis, blue toes, retinal plaques.
- **Nephritic / GN:** BP high; periorbital puffiness, edema; throat (tonsils, exudate) and skin for infection; ENT (saddle nose, sinus tenderness); chest crackles or hemoptysis signs; purpura; arthritis; murmur (endocarditis-related GN).
- **Lupus:** malar / discoid rash, oral ulcers, alopecia, synovitis, serositis (rub, effusion), lymphadenopathy.
- **Nephrotic:** periorbital and pitting edema (non-pitting = other cause); ascites; pleural effusion; DVT; xanthelasma; lymph nodes and organomegaly (lymphoma); breast / thyroid / prostate for cancer (membranous).
- **CKD / ESKD:** uremic look, pallor, scratch marks, asterixis, AV fistula / graft, catheter scars, transplant scar, polycystic kidneys, pericardial rub, S3, crackles, edema, livedo, bone tenderness / renal osteodystrophy, fundi.
- **Dialysis patient with seizure:** GCS, focal neurology, neck stiffness, fundi (papilledema), BP, catheter / access site for infection, tongue bite.
- **Secondary HTN:** both-arm BP, radio-radial and radio-femoral delay, weak femoral pulses, murmur (coarctation); renal / abdominal bruit (RAS, FMD); ballotable kidneys (ADPKD); abdominal mass (pheo); Cushingoid look (moon face, buffalo hump, supraclavicular fat, purple striae, bruising, proximal myopathy); thyroid signs (goiter, tremor, exophthalmos); acromegaly (large hands / feet, prognathism, widely spaced teeth); short neck / retrognathia (OSA), BMI; displaced apex / S4 (LVH).
- **Fundoscopy grades:** I arteriolar thickening; II AV nipping; III hemorrhages / cotton wool / exudates; IV papilledema.
- **Hyponatremia:** volume status decides the category (postural BP, JVP, edema, ascites); CNS exam (focal deficit, reflexes); chest for mass / effusion; thyroid and adrenal signs (pigmentation); PR if bleeding suspected.
- **Hypernatremia:** GCS; dehydration (dry mucosa, turgor); postural BP; CNS (tone, reflexes); HF signs if on diuretics; CLD stigmata.

## 5. Case scenarios (all 18 stations)

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### AKI

#### 1. Drug-induced AIN in ICU (two stations: 62M and 67F)

- **Stem:** respiratory failure from Gram-negative pneumonia; Augmentin failed → cephalosporin + gentamicin; creatinine 90 → 430 µmol/L.
- **Clues:** maculopapular rash on legs and arms, one overnight hypotension, UOP 690 mL/24 h (oliguric for 80 kg), high JVP, crackles.
- **Workup:** CBC (eosinophils); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN, urine output); electrolytes (Na, K, Cl); blood gas (VBG — pH, HCO3, PCO2, anion gap); liver profile; urine (urinalysis + microscopy, urine eosinophils, urine Na, urine osmolality, FeNa); CK; drug levels (gentamicin / vancomycin); immunology (ANA, C3 / C4, ANCA); imaging (renal US, CXR).
- **Results:** station 1 (62M): creatinine 430 µmol/L (4.8 mg/dL) from baseline 90 µmol/L (≈ 1.0 mg/dL); Na 131, K 5.1, Cl 95 mmol/L; VBG pH 7.29, HCO3 15 mmol/L, PCO2 42 mmHg (5.6 kPa) — high anion gap metabolic acidosis (AG 21) with inadequate respiratory compensation; urine Na 20 mmol/L, osm 300 mOsm/kg, FeNa < 1%; CK normal; urine +1 protein, no RBCs, WBC 40/hpf, WBC casts, eosinophiluria, no bacteriuria; ANA positive with normal C3 / C4; gentamicin level in range; kidneys 12 cm. Station 2 (67F): urine output 690 mL/24 h at 80 kg (≈ 0.36 mL/kg/h — oliguria); metabolic acidosis with K 5.1 mmol/L; WBC 14 ×10⁹/L; urine Na 15 mmol/L, osm 300 mOsm/kg, low FeNa; urine WBC with eosinophils; CXR congested; autoimmune normal.
- **DDx (5):** pre-renal (hypotension, sepsis); ATN (sepsis, gentamicin, rhabdo); AIN (Augmentin); GN; obstruction.
- **Diagnosis:** AKI from AIN (beta-lactam); confirm with renal biopsy (interstitial inflammatory infiltrate, normal glomeruli and vessels).
- **Management:** nephrology; stop culprit and nephrotoxins; treat sepsis; avoid hypotension; diuretics for overload; strict UOP and daily weight; low-K diet; correct K, PO4, Ca, Mg, acidosis; avoid hypo/hyperglycemia; assess dialysis need; add steroids once biopsy confirms AIN.
- **Examiner Qs:** urgent dialysis = refractory hyperkalemia; refractory acidosis; refractory volume overload; uremic symptoms; toxins (methanol, lithium).

#### 2. Gentamicin ATN + angry patient (62M)

- **Stem:** BPH, acute prostatitis on IV gentamicin; becomes oliguric with hearing trouble, now needs HD; angry.
- **Communication marks (50%):** ABC first; safety (door open, distance); let him vent; acknowledge anger empathically; apologize without blaming anyone or being defensive; explain plan; promise transparent investigation; explain the complaint process and that it won't affect his care; follow-up plan; resources; check understanding; thank him.
- **DDx for oliguria:** pre-renal (hypovolemia, sepsis, HF); intrinsic (aminoglycoside ATN, AIN from antibiotics, ischemic ATN, post-infectious GN / RPGN); post-renal (prostate, stones, clot, blocked catheter).
- **Pre-renal vs intrinsic:** pre-renal = urine Na < 20, FeNa < 1%, urine osm > 500; intrinsic = urine Na > 40, FeNa > 2%, urine osm < 350; casts = muddy brown (ATN), WBC (AIN), RBC (GN).
- **Workup:** renal profile (creatinine, urea / BUN, eGFR, urine output); electrolytes (K, Na, Ca, PO4, Mg); blood gas (HCO3, pH); urine (urinalysis + microscopy, urine Na, FeNa, urine osmolality); gentamicin trough level; CBC; ECG (hyperkalemia); imaging (renal US).
- **Results:** urea 22 mmol/L (BUN ≈ 62 mg/dL); creatinine 340 µmol/L (≈ 3.8 mg/dL); K 6.6 mmol/L (6.6 mEq/L); HCO3 14 mmol/L; urine Na 65 mmol/L; FeNa 3% (> 2% = intrinsic ATN); urine osmolality 280 mOsm/kg (isosthenuric); muddy brown granular casts; renal US normal.
- **Diagnosis:** AKI from gentamicin ATN.
- **Management:** admit; stop gentamicin; alternative non-nephrotoxic antibiotic; nephrology; volume status and fluid balance; treat K; HD (hyperkalemia + acidosis here); counsel on reversibility; DVT / GI prophylaxis.

#### 3. Rhabdomyolysis (22M, charity run in heat)

- **Stem:** 15-mile run, dizziness, cramps, back pain, leg weakness.
- **Clues:** HR 114, BP 95/50, dry mucosa, LL power 3/5 with hyporeflexia, normal sensation.
- **DDx:** rhabdomyolysis, trauma, viral myositis, heat stroke, malignant hyperthermia, NMS, serotonin syndrome, inflammatory myositis, pheo / thyroid storm, drugs (statins, cocaine, amphetamines).
- **Workup:** muscle injury (CK, urine myoglobin — dipstick blood positive without RBCs, LDH, AST); renal profile (creatinine, BUN, urine output); electrolytes (K, Ca, PO4, Mg, uric acid); blood gas (pH, HCO3, lactate); CBC; coagulation (DIC); liver profile; endocrine (TSH, cortisol); ECG (hyperkalemia); imaging (CXR; CT / MRI spine if back pain with neuro signs); EMG / NCS if weakness persists.
- **Management:** ICU; ABC; two large-bore cannulae; NS 200–250 mL/h targeting high UOP; correct electrolytes; consider urinary alkalinization (pH > 6.5, weak evidence); RRT and nephrology; DVT and GI prophylaxis.
- **ECG = peaked T (hyperkalemia):** IV calcium gluconate / chloride; insulin 10 units + dextrose; salbutamol nebs; RRT if refractory.
- **RRT indications here:** oliguric AKI; volume overload / pulmonary edema; refractory electrolytes; metabolic acidosis; symptomatic uremia.
- **Complications:** AKI, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia (early), hypermagnesemia, DIC, compartment syndrome, DVT / PE.

### Glomerular disease

#### 4. IgA nephropathy (two stations: 26M gross hematuria; 25M red urine 2 days after URTI)

- **Clues:** cola urine 1–2 days after URTI, recurrent episodes over years, flank discomfort, smoker, BP 150–156/90–98, LL edema.
- **DDx:** glomerular (IgA, lupus, post-infectious, MPGN); non-glomerular (cancer, stone, UTI).
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); albumin; urine (urinalysis + microscopy for dysmorphic RBCs / casts, urine culture, UPCR or 24-h protein); lipid profile; coagulation (PT / PTT — before biopsy); immunology (ASO, C3 / C4, ANA, anti-dsDNA, ANCA, cryoglobulins, serum IgA); infection (HBV, HCV, HIV, syphilis); imaging (renal US; CT if stones suspected); renal biopsy.
- **Results:** station 1 (26M): Hb 14 g/dL (140 g/L); creatinine 158 µmol/L (1.8 mg/dL); Na 139, K 4.9 mmol/L; albumin 38 g/L (3.8 g/dL); urine WBC 4/hpf, RBC 50/hpf (dysmorphic + RBC casts), protein 3+, culture negative; 24-h protein 1.3 g; LDL 3.9 mmol/L (≈ 151 mg/dL); PT / PTT, C3, C4, ANA, viral screen normal; US normal-size kidneys with mildly increased echogenicity. Station 2 (25M): albumin 30 g/L (3.0 g/dL); lipids normal; creatinine normal; 24-h protein 1,300 mg; autoimmune and viral screen negative; US-KUB normal.
- **Diagnosis:** nephritic syndrome → renal biopsy: mesangial IgA deposits = IgA nephropathy.
- **Management:** nephrology; BP control with ACEi / ARB (± SGLT2i if proteinuria persists); low-salt, low-protein diet; diuretics; statin; smoking cessation; fish oil; steroids if high risk / progressive; cyclophosphamide only for crescentic RPGN.
- **Examiner Qs:** PIGN comes 1–3 weeks after infection, IgA within days; poor prognosis = proteinuria > 1 g/day, HTN, low GFR at presentation, crescents.

#### 5. Infection-related GN (28F, joint pain, cough, fatigue)

- **Clues:** high fever, productive cough, dark red urine with low volume, swollen tonsils with pus, BP 165/95, basal crackles.
- **DDx:** infection-related GN, TTP / HUS / aHUS, ANCA vasculitis, SLE, anti-GBM, IgA, post-strep GN.
- **Workup:** CBC + film (exclude TMA); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN) + electrolytes (K); coagulation; urine (urinalysis + microscopy, UPCR); infection (blood / sputum cultures, throat swab, ASO, HBV, HCV, HIV, viral screen); immunology (ANA, ANCA, anti-GBM, C3 / C4); imaging (CXR, renal US); renal biopsy if not improving.
- **Results:** WBC 19 ×10⁹/L; Hb 10 g/dL (100 g/L); platelets 300 ×10⁹/L (no TMA); creatinine 470 µmol/L (≈ 5.3 mg/dL); K 5.2 mmol/L; ESR 92 mm/h; CRP high; coagulation normal; urine RBC casts; ANA, ANCA, anti-GBM negative; C3 / C4 and ASO normal; blood culture pending; HBV, HCV, HIV negative; CXR bilateral consolidation; renal US normal.
- **Diagnosis:** infection-related GN (concurrent infection, unlike post-strep).
- **Management:** ABC; admit; IV antibiotics (ID consult); supportive fluids; nephrology; diuretics and antihypertensives; dialysis if advanced.
- **Examiner Qs:** emergency dialysis = resistant hyperkalemia; resistant hypervolemia; resistant acidosis; uremic pleurisy / pericarditis; uremic encephalopathy.

#### 6. Lupus nephritis class IV (28F, protein +3, RBC 15/hpf)

- **Clues:** frothy urine, LL edema, joint pain, hair loss, no meds.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); electrolytes; albumin; urine (urinalysis + microscopy, UPCR / UACR or 24-h protein); metabolic (lipid profile, HbA1c); infection (HBV, HCV, HIV); immunology (ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith, C3 / C4, APS antibodies, ANCA, ASO, anti-PLA2R); paraprotein (SPEP / UPEP, free light chains); imaging (CXR, renal US); US-guided renal biopsy.
- **Results:** WBC 3.8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 12 g/dL (120 g/L); platelets 155 ×10⁹/L; PT / PTT normal; creatinine 1.3 mg/dL (≈ 115 µmol/L); urine pH 6.3, RBC 15/hpf, protein 3+, no leukocytes; albumin 3.2 g/dL (32 g/L); UPCR 1,800 mg/g (≈ 204 mg/mmol); ANA 1:640; anti-dsDNA 140 IU/mL (< 27); anti-Smith positive; C3 0.3 g/L (0.9–1.8) and C4 very low; HbA1c 5.4% (36 mmol/mol); cholesterol 180 mg/dL (4.7 mmol/L).
- **Diagnosis:** US-guided biopsy → diffuse proliferative LN class IV.
- **Management:** nephrology + rheumatology; education; ACEi / ARB, low salt, diuretics; hydroxychloroquine; IV methylprednisolone 250–500 mg for 1–3 days then oral 1 mg/kg; induction MMF or cyclophosphamide; maintenance MMF or azathioprine.
- **Biopsy in SLE if:** unexplained rising creatinine; proteinuria > 500 mg/day; active sediment (> 5 RBC/hpf and/or cellular casts).
- **Low-complement GN:** LN, cryoglobulinemia, post-infectious GN, MPGN, TMA, atheroembolic disease.
- **Safe in pregnancy:** steroids, hydroxychloroquine, azathioprine, CNIs (tacrolimus, cyclosporine).

#### 7. Nephrotic syndrome → minimal change disease (18M, puffy face + LL edema 2 days after URTI)

- **Clues:** bilateral pitting edema, no DVT signs, stable vitals.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation; renal profile; liver profile + albumin; urine (urinalysis + microscopy, ACR, 24-h protein); metabolic (lipid profile, HbA1c); infection (HBV, HCV, HIV, syphilis); immunology (ANA, anti-dsDNA, C3 / C4, anti-PLA2R, ANCA); imaging (renal US); age-appropriate cancer screen; renal biopsy in adults.
- **Results:** albumin 17 g/L (1.7 g/dL); LDL high; creatinine 55 µmol/L (≈ 0.62 mg/dL); no hematuria; 24-h urine protein 3,500 mg (nephrotic ≥ 3.5 g); HbA1c normal.
- **DDx:** MCD; FSGS; membranous; next step = renal biopsy.
- **Management (MCD):** nephrology; low salt; statin; DVT prophylaxis; prednisolone 1 mg/kg with calcium, vitamin D and PPI; diuretics; ACEi / ARB.
- **Steroid contraindications:** morbid obesity, uncontrolled DM, active infection.
- **MCD associations:** allergy, NSAIDs, ampicillin, Hodgkin / non-Hodgkin lymphoma, leukemia, SLE, DM, immunizations.

### CKD and dialysis

#### 8. Advanced CKD / ESKD from diabetic kidney disease (60M, eGFR 10, nausea, anorexia, itching)

- **Clues:** long DM with retinopathy; missed follow-up for 2 years; last eGFR 35; on lisinopril, sitagliptin / metformin; recently gum arabic; BP 150/90, basal crackles, +2 edema, no rub.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation; renal profile (creatinine, BUN, eGFR); electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, HCO3); albumin; CKD-MBD bone panel (Ca, PO4, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, ALP); anemia (ferritin, TSAT, B12, folate); urine (urinalysis, UACR); metabolic (glucose, HbA1c, lipids); infection before dialysis (HBV, HCV, HIV); imaging (renal US, CXR).
- **Results:** WBC 6 ×10⁹/L; Hb 8 g/dL (80 g/L); platelets 190 ×10⁹/L; PT / PTT normal; creatinine 7 mg/dL (≈ 619 µmol/L); BUN 100 mg/dL (urea ≈ 35.7 mmol/L); eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m²; urine pH 6.5, RBC 2–3/hpf, protein 3+; albumin 3.5 g/dL (35 g/L); HCO3 18 mmol/L; K 4.8 mmol/L; Na 135 mmol/L; Cl 110 mmol/L; Ca 8 mg/dL (2.0 mmol/L); PO4 6 mg/dL (≈ 1.9 mmol/L); PTH 350 pg/mL (≈ 37 pmol/L); 25-OH vitamin D 15 ng/mL (≈ 37 nmol/L); ferritin 120 ng/mL (µg/L); TSAT 12%; glucose 150 mg/dL (8.3 mmol/L); HbA1c 6.5% (48 mmol/mol); small echogenic kidneys.
- **Problem list:** advanced CKD near dialysis; anemia; metabolic acidosis; overload; CKD-MBD.
- **Management:** treat anemia, hyperphosphatemia, acidosis; diuretics and BP control; stop metformin / adjust sitagliptin; discuss RRT (transplant best, HD or PD); prepare access (AVF / AVG or PD catheter).
- **HD counseling:** why he needs it; what HD is; access (AVF vs catheter); risks and benefits; risk of refusing; alternatives (PD, transplant).
- **Anemia:** check ferritin / TSAT; IV iron if ferritin < 200 and TSAT < 20%; ESA once iron replete; exclude B12 / folate deficiency and GI blood loss.
- **Hyperphosphatemia:** low-phosphate diet; binders (calcium-based or sevelamer); hold vitamin D until PO4 controlled; dialysis.

#### 9. CKD + DM + HFrEF routine follow-up (68M) — problem list station

- **History:** 10-year course microalbuminuria → proteinuria → falling GFR; stable angina, SOB, edema; bone pain; reduced vision, numbness; poorly controlled DM.
- **Exam:** BP 160/110, SpO2 95% on 2 L, basal crackles, ankle edema, no melena.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); electrolytes (K) + blood gas (pH, HCO3); CKD-MBD bone panel (Ca, PO4, PTH, vitamin D); anemia (ferritin, TSAT); metabolic (HbA1c, lipids); urine (UACR or 24-h protein); viral screen (HBV, HCV, HIV); imaging (CXR, renal US).
- **Results:** Hb 7 g/dL (70 g/L); ferritin 100 ng/mL (low for CKD); TSAT 10%; creatinine 470 µmol/L (≈ 5.3 mg/dL); eGFR 17–27 mL/min/1.73 m²; K 5.2 mmol/L; PTH 350 pg/mL (≈ 37 pmol/L; high); PO4 1.8 mmol/L (≈ 5.6 mg/dL); pH 7.32, HCO3 22 mmol/L; HbA1c 10% (86 mmol/mol); proteinuria 3 g/24 h; coagulation normal; HBV, HCV, HIV negative; CXR no overload; kidneys 6 cm with poor corticomedullary differentiation.
- **CKD stages (G):** G1 ≥ 90; G2 60–89; G3a 45–59; G3b 30–44; G4 15–29; G5 < 15 mL/min/1.73 m²; albuminuria A1 < 30, A2 30–300, A3 > 300 mg/g.
- **Problem list:** advanced CKD → nephrology referral for RRT / transplant planning, add empagliflozin for proteinuria, raise calcitriol for PTH, renal dietitian; anemia → iron + ESA; DM → adjust insulin, lifestyle, ophthalmology referral; HTN → add amlodipine, raise diuretic dose (helps volume and K); dyslipidemia → statin.

#### 10. Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome (52M, first HD session, then seizure)

- **Clues:** DM, HTN, CKD; headache and vomiting then first tonic-clonic seizure on the day of first HD; no fever, focal weakness or neck stiffness.
- **DDx:** DDS, uremic encephalopathy, CNS infection (catheter), hemorrhagic or ischemic stroke, hypertensive encephalopathy, hypoglycemia, hypercalcemia (hyperparathyroidism), idiopathic.
- **DDS features:** nausea, vomiting, headache, dizziness, cramps, confusion, tremor, visual change, asterixis, seizure, coma, death; during or hours after HD.
- **Workup:** bedside glucose; electrolytes (Na, Ca, Mg, PO4); renal profile (pre- and post-dialysis urea / BUN); blood gas; CBC; PTH; imaging (CT brain, MRI); ECG; EEG; LP if infection suspected.
- **Result:** CT = symmetrical diffuse white-matter edema; normal electrolytes and EEG → DDS.
- **Management:** control BP; change HD prescription (shorter sessions, lower blood flow, smaller target urea reduction); correct metabolic issues (high PTH); no antiepileptic (provoked seizure).
- **Start antiepileptic after first seizure if:** abnormal neuro exam, MRI or EEG; Todd's paralysis; status epilepticus; high-risk job; family history of epilepsy; patient wish.

### Sodium disorders

#### 11. Thiazide-induced hyponatremia (65M, DM / HTN, altered mental status 1 day)

- **Clues:** new "diuretic" for BP started last week; sleepy and fatigued 2 days; BP 95/65, HR 100, postural hypotension, JVP 3; no focal deficit.
- **DDx:** electrolyte disturbance; CNS disease.
- **Workup:** electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, Ca, Mg, PO4); serum osmolality (measured); urine (osmolality, spot Na, urinalysis); renal profile (creatinine, urea / BUN); glucose; blood gas; liver profile + albumin; endocrine (TSH, 8 AM cortisol); muscle (CK); cardiac (ECG, troponin); CBC; coagulation; imaging (CXR, CT brain).
- **Results:** WBC 6 ×10⁹/L; Hb 12 g/dL; platelets 170 ×10⁹/L; creatinine 1.3 mg/dL (115 µmol/L); BUN 23 mg/dL (urea 8.2 mmol/L); Na 108 mmol/L; K 3.9 mmol/L; Cl 90 mmol/L; Ca 9 mg/dL (2.25 mmol/L); Mg 2.2 mg/dL (0.9 mmol/L); PO4 4 mg/dL (1.3 mmol/L); ALT 22, AST 25 U/L; albumin 3.6 g/dL (36 g/L); ALP 120 U/L; troponin 0.02 (normal); glucose 150 mg/dL (8.3 mmol/L); CK 120 U/L; TSH 1.8 mIU/L; ABG pH 7.41, PCO2 40 mmHg (5.3 kPa), HCO3 26 mmol/L; serum osmolality 230 mOsm/kg; urine osmolality 400 mOsm/kg; urine Na 60 mmol/L; CT brain normal.
- **Diagnosis:** hypovolemic hypo-osmolar hyponatremia with renal Na loss from thiazide.
- **Management:** ICU + nephrology; stop thiazide and hold antihypertensives; 3% saline 100 mL over 10–20 min, up to 3 times, to raise Na 4–6 or until symptoms improve; limit to 6–8 mmol/L in 24 h; then NS guided by Na; check Na every 2–4 h; input / output chart.
- **Other thiazide side effects:** hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia, hyperuricemia, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, hypotension, pancreatitis, allergy.

#### 12. SIADH (62M, DM, recent CVA, depression; nausea, headache, Na 120)

- **Clues:** weight loss; heavy smoker 15 years; on SSRI; euvolemic (no edema, JVP normal, no postural drop).
- **Workup:** electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, HCO3); serum osmolality; urine (osmolality, spot Na, urinalysis); renal profile; liver profile; metabolic (glucose, HbA1c, lipids — pseudohyponatremia); endocrine (TSH, cortisol); blood gas; cardiac (ECG, echo); imaging (CXR, US abdomen, CT brain); malignancy work-up (CT chest for SCLC).
- **Results:** Na 120 mmol/L; serum osmolality 240 mOsm/kg (calculated); urine osmolality 300 mOsm/kg (> 100 — inappropriately concentrated); urine Na 40 mmol/L (> 30); euvolemic (BP 120/80, no orthostatic drop, no edema); TSH and cortisol normal; on SSRI; CXR right lung mass.
- **Diagnosis:** hypotonic euvolemic hyponatremia from SIADH.
- **Three causes here:** lung malignancy (small cell, ectopic ADH); SSRI; stroke (any CNS insult).
- **Management:** admit; fluid restriction; oral salt tablets; frequent Na checks; DVT prophylaxis; education and nutrition; refer oncology, psychiatry (review SSRI), neurology; smoking cessation.
- **ODS risk factors:** Na < 105, alcoholism, malnutrition, advanced liver disease, hypokalemia.

#### 13. Hypernatremia from extrarenal loss (75M, DM / HTN / HF on diuretics; confusion + vomiting 3 days; Na 160)

- **Clues:** looks dehydrated, GCS 11/15, BP 110/70, HR 95.
- **DDx:** extrarenal loss (vomiting, diarrhea, insensible); DI (central or nephrogenic); osmotic diuresis (hyperglycemia, mannitol); drugs (diuretics, lithium).
- **Workup:** electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, Mg); serum osmolality; urine (osmolality, spot Na, urinalysis, 24-h volume); renal profile; glucose + HbA1c; liver profile; blood gas; CBC; coagulation; cardiac (ECG, echo); imaging (CXR, CT brain); water deprivation / desmopressin test if polyuric (DI).
- **Results:** Na 160 mmol/L (160 mEq/L); urine Na 10 mmol/L; urine osmolality 800 mOsm/kg (concentrated — kidney conserving) → extrarenal water loss; glucose, renal profile, CXR and CT brain normal.
- **Management:** ABC; ICU / HDU; stop diuretics (with cardiology); if hypovolemic NS until stable, then hypotonic fluid (D5W) per calculated water deficit; correct no faster than 10–12 mmol/L per day (0.5 mmol/L/h); Na every 4–6 h; treat vomiting; VTE prophylaxis; nephrology.
- **Examiner Qs:** slow correction prevents cerebral edema, seizures, herniation; commonest cause in elderly = inadequate water intake (impaired thirst or access); nephrogenic DI drugs = lithium, amphotericin B, demeclocycline.

### Secondary hypertension

#### 14. Primary aldosteronism / Conn's (22F, BP 188/105 ×3, cramps and weakness)

- **Clues:** HR 109, grade II hypertensive retinopathy, otherwise normal exam.
- **Workup:** electrolytes (Na, K, HCO3) + renal profile (urea, creatinine); bone (Ca); blood gas; endocrine screen (aldosterone / renin ratio — off interfering drugs; urinary / plasma metanephrines; 24-h urinary cortisol or 1 mg DST if Cushingoid; TSH; OGTT with GH if acromegalic); cardiac (ECG, echo, CXR for coarctation); imaging (renal US + Doppler or MRA; adrenal CT after biochemistry; adrenal vein sampling).
- **Results:** Na 144 mmol/L; K 3.0 mmol/L (3.0 mEq/L); urea 5.4 mmol/L (BUN 15 mg/dL); HCO3 29 mmol/L (hypokalemic alkalosis); creatinine 76 µmol/L (0.86 mg/dL); Ca 2.3 mmol/L (9.2 mg/dL); catecholamines normal; renal Doppler normal; ECG LVH; supine aldosterone 1,220 pmol/L (≈ 44 ng/dL; normal 100–500); plasma renin activity 0.2 (normal 1.1–2.7) → suppressed renin.
- **Next test:** correct K; stop interfering drugs (ACEi, beta-blockers, spironolactone); aldosterone and plasma renin activity → high aldosterone, suppressed renin.
- **Imaging:** adrenal MRI / CT → adenoma (surgery) vs bilateral hyperplasia (medical); benign features = < 10 HU, washout > 50%, < 4 cm, smooth, homogeneous.
- **Treatment:** adrenalectomy for adenoma; tumors > 4 cm always removed; HTN cured in only about two-thirds; medical = spironolactone (gynecomastia, impotence, menstrual irregularity) or amiloride / triamterene.

#### 15. Renal artery stenosis from FMD (21F, BP 180/105 on ambulatory monitoring)

- **Clues:** on/off headache, smoker, NSAIDs, strong family history of HTN, AV nicking, faint bruit left of and above the umbilicus.
- **DDx (5):** renal parenchymal disease / CKD, RAS, pheochromocytoma, hyperaldosteronism, drugs, coarctation.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, HCO3); urinalysis; endocrine screen (renin + aldosterone, metanephrines, TSH / FT4); cardiac (ECG, echo, CXR); imaging (renal US with Doppler → CT / MR angiography; catheter angiography with angioplasty).
- **Results:** CBC normal; creatinine 70 µmol/L (0.8 mg/dL); K 3.1 mmol/L; HCO3 25 mmol/L; Na and Ca normal; renin and aldosterone both mildly raised (secondary hyperaldosteronism); ECG LVH + left axis deviation; Doppler high resistive index and peak systolic velocity on the left.
- **Diagnosis:** left RAS from FMD; confirm with renal angiography / CTA / MRA.
- **Management:** at least two antihypertensives including ACEi / ARB; nephrology and angioplasty referral; low salt; quit smoking; exercise; weight loss.
- **Angioplasty indications in FMD:** recent-onset HTN, resistant HTN, intolerance of medication, progressive kidney dysfunction.

#### 16. ADPKD (35M, BP 150/90, family history of kidney disease)

- **Clues:** bilateral ballotable kidneys.
- **DDx:** ADPKD, RAS, endocrine (Cushing's, pheo, hyperthyroid), essential HTN, other CKD.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); urine (urinalysis, 24-h protein); liver profile; metabolic (lipids, HbA1c); blood gas; cardiac (ECG, echo); imaging (US abdomen / kidneys — cyst number and kidney size; MRI for total kidney volume; MRA brain if family history of aneurysm / SAH); genetic testing (PKD1 / PKD2) if unclear; metanephrines, cortisol, TFTs if suspected.
- **Results:** creatinine 56 µmol/L (≈ 0.63 mg/dL); hematuria without proteinuria; US multiple bilateral renal cysts.
- **Management:** education; low salt; stop smoking; weight control; exercise; high water intake; BP target < 110/75 with ACEi / ARB; tolvaptan for rapidly progressive disease; family screening and genetic counseling; nephrology; monitor BP, function, proteinuria; aneurysm screening in selected patients.
- **Examiner Qs:** extrarenal = liver cysts, intracranial aneurysms, valve disease (MVP, AR), diverticulosis; commonest cause of death = cardiovascular disease.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Emergency dialysis (AEIOU):** refractory acidosis; refractory hyperkalemia (K > 6.5 or ECG changes); toxins (methanol, lithium); refractory overload / pulmonary edema; uremia (pericarditis, pleuritis, encephalopathy, seizures).
- **Hyperkalemia with ECG changes:** IV calcium; insulin + dextrose; salbutamol; dialysis if refractory; stop K sources and offending drugs; low-K diet.
- **Urine indices:** pre-renal FeNa < 1%, urine Na < 20, urine osm > 500; ATN FeNa > 2%, urine Na > 40, osm < 350.
- **Casts:** RBC casts / dysmorphic RBCs = GN; WBC casts + eosinophils = AIN; muddy brown granular casts = ATN.
- **Biopsy rule:** proteinuria + hematuria or unexplained AKI with normal-size kidneys → renal biopsy confirms in GN, nephrotic and AIN stations.
