# General & Communication

## How to use this

The 6 general stations are communication stations with a medical tail: half the marks (≈ 50) are for the communication framework, the rest for examiner questions. Learn the three frameworks below once; they also reappear in other specialties (gentamicin ATN, warfarin, MTC, esophageal cancer).

- **Stations covered:** breaking bad news ×2 (lung cancer, colon cancer); angry patient with drug reaction ×2 (amoxicillin, ibuprofen → anaphylaxis); medication counseling ×2 (biologic for RA, cyclophosphamide for lupus nephritis).

## 1. Framework: breaking bad news (SPIKES)

- **Setting:** introduce yourself and role; confirm name and age; explain the purpose; consent; private room, phone off, door closed; sit down; ask if they want a relative present.
- **Perception:** ask what they already know or suspect; ideas, concerns, expectations, fears.
- **Invitation:** ask how much they want to know and whether they want the result today.
- **Knowledge:** warning shot ("I'm afraid the results are not what we hoped"); explain why the test was done; give the result clearly in plain words; avoid jargon; small chunks.
- **Emotions:** allow silence; acknowledge and name the emotion; listen without interrupting; eye contact; tissue / water.
- **Strategy:** outline the plan and MDT (oncology, surgery, radiotherapy, palliative care, psychology, social work); explore physical and psychosocial support; arrange follow-up.
- **Safety and close:** offer help getting home or a family meeting; contact numbers; leaflets, websites, support groups; check understanding; invite questions; summarize; thank.

## 2. Framework: angry patient after an adverse event

- **First:** say you will make sure the patient is stable (ABC) before talking.
- **Introduction:** introduce, confirm identity, explain why you are there, consent.
- **Safety:** door open, sit nearer the door, not too close, invite the patient to sit.
- **Emotions:** find the reason for anger; let him vent without interrupting; acknowledge empathically and non-judgmentally; calm tone, simple language; eye contact.
- **Apology:** apologize and accept the grievance; explain the aim is to treat him properly; never blame a colleague, department or service; not defensive.
- **Actions:** share the plan for the current problem; promise a transparent investigation to prevent recurrence; explain how to make a formal complaint; reassure that complaining will not affect his care.
- **Close:** follow-up plan; how to reach help; resources; check understanding; questions; thank.

## 3. Framework: medication counseling

- **Introduction:** introduce, confirm identity, purpose, consent, private setting, relative present?
- **Explore:** what she knows; ideas, concerns, expectations; current disease activity and treatment; adherence and reasons for failure; comorbidities (liver, kidney, heart, lung).
- **Explain:** the diagnosis in plain words; why this drug; name, route, dose, schedule; alternatives with their pros and cons.
- **Risks:** common and serious side effects; how they will be reduced (monitoring, prophylaxis).
- **Safety net:** when to stop and call (fever, rash, jaundice, bleeding, pregnancy); contact numbers; leaflet.
- **Close:** check understanding; questions; consent; thank.

## 4. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → communication task → examiner's questions.

### Breaking bad news

#### 1. Lung cancer (73M, heavy smoker, nodule on CXR, biopsy positive)

- **Task:** SPIKES (section 1).
- **Lung nodule DDx:** malignant (primary lung cancer, metastasis, lymphoma, carcinoid, sarcoma); benign tumor (hamartoma, fibroma); infection (TB, fungal — histoplasma, cocci, crypto, aspergilloma; abscess; hydatid); inflammatory (sarcoid, RA nodule, GPA); vascular (AVM, infarct); congenital (bronchogenic cyst, sequestration).
- **Complications of lung cancer:** SVC syndrome; Pancoast tumor with Horner syndrome / brachial plexus; hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve); pleural effusion; post-obstructive pneumonia; metastases; thrombosis; paraneoplastic (SIADH, hypercalcemia, Lambert-Eaton, Cushing); death.
- **Solitary pulmonary nodule:** single, < 3 cm, surrounded by normal lung, no lymphadenopathy, no effusion.

#### 2. Colon cancer (50M, rectal bleeding and weight loss)

- **Task:** SPIKES (section 1).
- **Risk factors:** age > 45, IBD, personal / family history of CRC or polyps, low-fiber high-fat diet with red / processed meat, inactivity, DM, obesity, male sex, alcohol, smoking.
- **Saudi high-risk group:** prior polyps; ≥ 1 first-degree or ≥ 2 second-degree relatives with CRC / polyps; UC or Crohn's; prior abdominal / pelvic radiation.
- **Prognostic factors:** stage (I–II better), node involvement, grade and histology, metastases, molecular markers (KRAS / BRAF worse; MSI-high / dMMR responds to immunotherapy), performance status (ECOG, Karnofsky), response to treatment.

### Angry patient with drug reaction → anaphylaxis

#### 3. Amoxicillin given despite penicillin allergy (62M, leg cellulitis)

- **Task:** angry patient framework (section 2).
- **Leg swelling DDx:** infection (cellulitis, abscess, necrotizing fasciitis); DVT / thrombophlebitis / post-thrombotic; venous or arterial insufficiency; lymphedema; ruptured Baker cyst; hematoma, compartment syndrome; myositis / rhabdomyolysis; tumor.
- **Turns into:** severe skin reaction, SOB, dizziness = anaphylaxis.

#### 4. Ibuprofen given despite aspirin allergy (60M, knee OA)

- **Task:** angry patient framework (section 2).
- **Flushing DDx:** anaphylaxis, carcinoid, pheochromocytoma, mastocytosis, medullary thyroid cancer, VIPoma, serotonin syndrome, menopause / emotional / autonomic, rosacea, drugs (niacin, CCB, vancomycin, steroids), alcohol, spicy food.
- **Turns into:** anaphylactic (distributive) shock.

#### Anaphylaxis management (both stations)

- **Immediate:** ABC; stop the trigger; IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (1:1000) in the thigh, repeat every 5 min.
- **Airway and breathing:** high-flow O2; salbutamol for wheeze; intubation or cricothyrotomy for laryngeal edema.
- **Circulation:** lie flat, legs up; large-volume IV fluids; adrenaline infusion / vasopressors in ICU if refractory; glucagon if on beta blockers.
- **Adjuncts:** IV antihistamine (diphenhydramine / chlorphenamine); IV steroid (hydrocortisone) to reduce biphasic reaction.
- **After:** observe / admit; allergy referral; document allergy; education and adrenaline auto-injector.

### Medication counseling

#### 5. Biologic therapy for RA not controlled on methotrexate (26F)

- **Explore:** disease duration and activity (joint count, morning stiffness, extra-articular features); doses and adherence; reasons MTX failed (smoking, interactions, fibromyalgia, OSA).
- **Infection screen:** recent infection; TB history or symptoms (cough, hemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss); shingles; HIV, hepatitis B / C and their risk factors.
- **Contraindications to ask:** heart failure, demyelinating disease, malignancy, lung disease, thrombosis (JAKi), pregnancy / breastfeeding.
- **Vaccines before starting:** influenza, pneumococcal, recombinant zoster (live vaccines before, not during).
- **Educate:** dose, schedule, injection technique; infection and TB reactivation risk; allergy; when to report; leaflet; consent.
- **Pre-biologic tests:** CBC (with differential); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); liver profile; lipid profile (tocilizumab, JAKi); urinalysis; infection screen (HBV — HBsAg, anti-HBc; HCV; HIV; latent TB — TST or QuantiFERON); imaging (CXR).
- **Approved biologics / targeted drugs:** TNF inhibitors (infliximab, etanercept, adalimumab, certolizumab, golimumab); IL-6 (tocilizumab, sarilumab); rituximab; abatacept; JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib).

#### 6. Cyclophosphamide for lupus nephritis class III with crescents (30F)

- **Explore:** SLE course and activity, renal and volume symptoms, obstetric history, thrombosis; current drugs and adherence; NSAIDs; comorbidities.
- **Regimens:** Euro-Lupus low dose = 500 mg IV every 2 weeks × 6; NIH high dose = 0.5–1 g/m² IV monthly × 6.
- **Side effects:** infertility / premature ovarian failure (higher if > 30 years); marrow suppression and infection; hemorrhagic cystitis and bladder cancer; teratogenic; SIADH; nausea, alopecia.
- **Risk reduction:** GnRH agonist (leuprolide 3.75 mg IM 10–14 days before each dose); IV hydration; MESNA; antiemetics (ondansetron, dexamethasone); PCP prophylaxis with TMP-SMX; contraception.
- **Stop and call if:** rash / allergy, fever or infection, severe vomiting, jaundice, blood in urine, planning pregnancy.
- **High dose preferred when:** adverse clinical features (nephritic sediment with GFR 25–80) or histology (crescents / necrosis in > 25% of glomeruli).
- **Induction:** cyclophosphamide or MMF; maintenance: MMF or azathioprine.
- **Antibody tracking activity / relapse:** anti-C1q (also anti-dsDNA with low complement).

## 5. Physical examination stations

The "Examination – General" file is a collection of system-exam stations. Most already sit in their specialty tab; the lymphoreticular exam is new and kept here.

- **Where each exam lives:** cardiac exam → Cardiology; respiratory exam (front / back) → Pulmonology; abdominal exam with chronic-diarrhea questions → Gastroenterology; thyroid exam → Endocrinology; cranial nerves and extrapyramidal exam → Neurology; hand, back, knee, shoulder and hip exams → Rheumatology.

### Lymphoreticular examination

- **Start:** wash hands; introduce; confirm name and DOB; explain; chaperone; consent; bed at 45°; expose; ask about pain.
- **General inspection:** pallor, bruising / bleeding, cachexia, jaundice, scratch marks; surroundings (medications, oxygen, sputum pot).
- **Cervical nodes (from behind, patient sitting, chin slightly down):** submental → submandibular → tonsillar / angle of jaw → preauricular → posterior auricular → anterior cervical chain down to the clavicle → posterior cervical chain up to the mastoid → occipital → supraclavicular and infraclavicular (tilt head to each side; left supraclavicular = Virchow node).
- **Axillary nodes (lying at 45°):** inspect for scars or masses; support the patient's forearm with your same-side hand so the muscles relax; palpate anterior (pectoral), central (medial), posterior (subscapular), lateral (humeral) and apical groups; repeat on the other side.
- **Epitrochlear nodes:** hold the wrist; fingers behind the olecranon, thumb just above the medial epicondyle (generalized lymphadenopathy, melanoma of the arm, secondary syphilis).
- **Inguinal nodes (lying flat):** horizontal group just below the inguinal ligament (ASIS → pubic tubercle); vertical group along the proximal great saphenous vein, about 3 cm lateral to the pubic tubercle.
- **Abdomen:** inspect; light and deep palpation; liver; spleen (start in the right iliac fossa, roll onto the right side).
- **Describe each node:** site, size (cm), number, consistency (hard = cancer, rubbery = lymphoma, tender soft = infection), tenderness, mobility / fixed, matted, overlying skin.
- **Close:** thank; summarize; suggest CBC with differential, blood film, LDH, HIV / EBV / CMV / TB tests, imaging (US / CT), excisional node biopsy.
- **Lymphadenopathy DDx:** infection (viral — EBV, CMV, HIV; TB; brucella; toxoplasma; bacterial); malignancy (lymphoma, leukemia, metastases); autoimmune (SLE, RA, Still disease, sarcoidosis); drugs (phenytoin, allopurinol, DRESS); others (Kikuchi, Castleman).
