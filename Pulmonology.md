# Pulmonology

## How to use this

The pulmonology stations fall into five presentations: chronic dyspnea (ILD, COPD, pulmonary hypertension), cough (asthma, bronchiectasis, HP), hemoptysis / mass (cancer, TB), pleural effusion, and acute dyspnea / chest pain (PE, CAP, ARDS). Learn the core history and the respiratory exam once, then add the block for the presentation.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; vitals with SpO2; note what the patient is connected to (O2, nebulizer, inhalers); check for respiratory distress before anything else.
- **Stations covered:** ARDS from COVID; CTEPH ×2 (one with APS); idiopathic PAH; IPF ×2; COPD ×2; asthma; CF bronchiectasis; post-TB bronchiectasis; hypersensitivity pneumonitis; Pancoast adenocarcinoma (EGFR); squamous lung cancer with hypercalcemia; pulmonary TB; CAP; parapneumonic effusion; hepatic hydrothorax; PE with APS → CAPS; respiratory system exam ×2.

## 1. Core respiratory history (every case)

- **Dyspnea:** onset, duration, course, continuous vs intermittent, triggers, relieving factors, severity (mMRC / NYHA / WHO class), at rest or exertion.
- **Cough:** onset, duration, dry vs productive, diurnal variation (night = asthma, morning = bronchiectasis), triggers (exercise, dust, perfumes, cold, work, season).
- **Sputum and blood:** sputum amount and color; hemoptysis (amount, frequency, fresh vs streaks).
- **Associated:** wheeze, chest pain (pleuritic or not), orthopnea, PND, leg swelling, palpitations, syncope, hoarseness.
- **Red flags / constitutional:** fever, night sweats, weight loss, loss of appetite, hemoptysis, hoarseness, bone pain.
- **Systemic review:** CTD (joint pain, rash, Raynaud, dry eyes / mouth, muscle weakness, oral ulcers, skin thickening); ENT (rhinitis, postnasal drip, sinusitis); GERD; renal (urine color, frothy urine); GI (jaundice, distension).
- **Past history:** asthma, COPD, TB (treated?), recurrent pneumonia / childhood infections, VTE, cancer, heart failure, CKD, liver disease, autoimmune disease, previous admissions / ICU / intubation.
- **Drugs:** inhalers and technique, steroids, anticoagulants, ACEi (cough), beta blockers, NSAIDs, amiodarone / methotrexate / nitrofurantoin / bleomycin (ILD), OCP / HRT (VTE), allergies.
- **Exposures:** smoking (pack-years), water pipe, alcohol; occupation (dust, asbestos, silica, metal / wood dust, farming); birds / pigeons / animals; radiation; TB contact; sick contacts; travel.
- **Family and social:** lung disease, familial IPF, CF, atopy, TB, cancer, thrombophilia; marital status, children / infertility (CF, PCD); vaccination.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Chronic exertional dyspnea (ILD / PH / COPD)

- **ILD:** dry cough, progressive dyspnea; occupational dust, birds (HP), drugs (amiodarone, MTX, nitrofurantoin, bleomycin), radiation; CTD symptoms; family history of IPF; GERD.
- **Pulmonary hypertension:** exertional chest tightness, fatigue, presyncope / syncope, ankle swelling; previous DVT / PE; CTD (scleroderma); liver disease (portopulmonary); HIV; appetite suppressants; OCP; congenital heart disease.
- **Obstetric (young woman):** miscarriages, stillbirths, preeclampsia → APS; plan for pregnancy (PH = avoid pregnancy).
- **COPD:** heavy smoking, chronic productive cough, exacerbations and admissions, inhaler use, home O2.

### B. Chronic cough

- **Asthma:** nocturnal, intermittent, triggered by exercise / dust / perfumes; wheeze; atopy; ER visits needing nebulization.
- **Upper airway / GERD:** rhinitis, nasal drip, sinusitis, heartburn.
- **Drugs:** ACEi, beta blockers.
- **Bronchiectasis:** copious morning sputum, recurrent infections, previous TB, childhood pertussis / measles, recurrent sinusitis, infertility (CF, Kartagener), immune deficiency.
- **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis:** pigeons / birds, farming, molds; symptoms improve away from exposure.

### C. Hemoptysis / lung mass

- **Bleed:** amount (tablespoon or more = significant), frequency, fresh, with sputum.
- **Cancer:** smoking pack-years, weight loss, hoarseness, bone pain, neck / shoulder pain to the ulnar fingers + ptosis (Pancoast); paraneoplastic (hypercalcemia — thirst, constipation, abdominal pain, confusion; SIADH; Cushing).
- **TB:** night sweats, weight loss, contact, origin from an endemic country, previous treatment.
- **Other:** bleeding elsewhere (hematologic), epistaxis / hematuria (GPA, Goodpasture), calf pain (PE), anticoagulants, mitral stenosis.

### D. Pleural effusion

- **Effusion:** pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea; preceding URTI / fever (parapneumonic).
- **Transudate causes:** HF (orthopnea, PND), cirrhosis (jaundice, distension, alcohol, hepatitis), nephrotic / CKD.
- **Exudate causes:** TB, malignancy (weight loss), CTD, PE, trauma, asbestos.

### E. Acute dyspnea / pleuritic chest pain (PE / CAP / ARDS)

- **PE:** calf pain / swelling, immobilization, long travel, surgery, trauma, cancer, previous VTE, OCP / HRT, pregnancy, recent COVID vaccine (VITT), fetal losses (APS), family thrombophilia.
- **Pneumonia:** fever, productive cough, pleuritic pain, sick contact, recent hospitalization, travel, aspiration risk.
- **Severe respiratory failure:** COVID contact, inability to talk; neuromuscular weakness (GBS, MG), myositis; asthma / COPD history.

## 3. Respiratory system exam (dedicated station, 80 marks)

- **Start:** introduce, permission, wash hands; position at 45° (front) or sitting upright with hands crossed on shoulders (back); ask about pain; expose to the waist.
- **From the foot of the bed:** well or ill, distress, position, O2 / nebulizer, accessory muscles, RR, audible wheeze, cyanosis, GCS; legs for edema, DVT, erythema nodosum (sarcoid); sacral edema.
- **Vitals:** BP, HR, RR, temperature, SpO2.
- **Hands:** clubbing, tar stains, muscle wasting (Pancoast), tremor / flapping tremor (CO2 retention), cyanosis, palmar erythema, joint deformity (RA).
- **Pulse:** rate, AF, bounding (CO2 retention), pulsus paradoxus (severe asthma).
- **Face:** pallor, jaundice, central cyanosis; Horner syndrome (ptosis, miosis); oral candidiasis (inhaled steroids).
- **Neck:** JVP; trachea position and cricosternal distance; cervical, supraclavicular and axillary nodes.
- **Inspection:** symmetry; scars (sternotomy, chest-drain scar in the axilla, thoracotomy / lobectomy); radiotherapy skin changes, telangiectasia; deformity (pectus excavatum / carinatum, barrel chest, kyphoscoliosis).
- **Palpation:** apex beat; chest expansion at three levels (symmetric or not); tactile vocal fremitus.
- **Percussion:** supraclavicular (apices), infraclavicular, chest wall, axillae, compare side to side; tidal percussion for the diaphragm.
- **Auscultation:** same areas front and back; vocal resonance; loud P2; TR murmur.
- **Close:** thank the patient and cover; offer CVS exam, PEFR, sputum pot.
- **Examiner Qs:** bronchial breathing with increased vocal resonance = consolidation, cavity / abscess, mass, collapse with patent bronchus, bronchiectasis. Consolidation vs effusion: both dull (effusion stony dull); vocal resonance increased in consolidation, reduced / absent in effusion.

## 4. Exam add-ons by presentation

- **ILD:** clubbing, bibasal end-inspiratory Velcro crackles, squeaks (HP); CTD signs (arthritis, skin thickening, rash), uveitis, parotids, nodes (sarcoid); signs of PH.
- **Pulmonary hypertension:** raised JVP with prominent a and v waves, RV heave, loud / palpable P2, pansystolic TR murmur louder on inspiration, right S3, tender hepatomegaly, ascites, ankle edema, central cyanosis.
- **COPD:** barrel chest, hyperresonance, reduced breath sounds, expiratory wheeze, pursed lips, flapping tremor, cor pulmonale.
- **Asthma:** expiratory wheeze; check ability to speak, PEFR.
- **Bronchiectasis / CF:** clubbing, coarse crackles, low BMI, nasal polyps; dextrocardia (Kartagener).
- **Effusion:** reduced expansion, stony dullness, reduced breath sounds and vocal resonance; trachea shifted away if large; look for the cause (liver stigmata, ascites, raised JVP, nodes, breast).
- **Consolidation (CAP):** bronchial breathing and crackles, increased vocal resonance; confusion, BP, RR for CURB-65.
- **Cancer:** clubbing, cachexia, nodes, Horner, wasting of hand muscles, collapse / effusion, hepatomegaly, bone tenderness, neurologic signs (paraneoplastic, brain mets).
- **PE / APS:** DVT signs (swollen warm tender calf, dilated superficial veins), livedo reticularis, loud P2, raised JVP, SLE signs (malar rash, joints).
- **Respiratory failure / ARDS:** nasal flaring, accessory muscles, tracheal tug, paradoxical abdominal breathing; skin and limbs for a source of infection; glucose and urine dipstick.

## 5. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → key positives → workup and results → diagnosis → management → examiner's questions.

### Pulmonary vascular disease

#### 1. CTEPH (45F, exertional dyspnea 1 year, PE 1 year ago on rivaroxaban)

- **Clues:** WHO class II–III, chest pain, fatigue; BP 100/55, HR 110, SpO2 91%; JVP with prominent a wave; loud palpable P2; TR murmur louder on inspiration; hepatomegaly; leg edema.
- **DDx:** pulmonary hypertension, CHF, CAP, COPD, bronchiectasis.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; cardiac (troponin, NT-proBNP, ECG, echo); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); blood gas; imaging (CXR, CT pulmonary angiography, V/Q scan); physiology (PFT with DLCO, 6-minute walk test); immunology (autoimmune profile, thrombophilia / APS screen); right heart catheterization.
- **Results:** WBC 8.55 ×10⁹/L; Hb 14 g/dL (140 g/L); renal and LFT normal; troponin normal; pro-BNP 1,200 pg/mL; autoimmune negative; DLCO low; 6MWT 300 m; echo EF > 55%, RV dilated, moderate TR, PASP 55 mmHg; ECG RA enlargement, RVH, strain; CXR prominent main PA, peripheral pruning; V/Q multiple mismatched defects; RHC mPAP 40 mmHg, PCWP 12 mmHg.
- **Diagnosis:** chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (WHO group 4).
- **Management:** diuretics; continue lifelong anticoagulation; pulmonary endarterectomy if operable; riociguat if inoperable or persistent PH after surgery; refer to a pulmonary hypertension center.

#### 2. CTEPH due to APS (46F, 18 months of dyspnea, unprovoked PE, 2 miscarriages)

- **Clues:** WHO class III, chest tightness, presyncope, ankle swelling; SpO2 90%, central cyanosis; JVP with prominent a wave; parasternal heave; loud P2; TR murmur; tender hepatomegaly.
- **DDx:** pulmonary hypertension, heart failure, ILD.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; coagulation; cardiac (ECG, echo, BNP); blood gas; physiology (PFT, 6MWT); imaging (CXR, CTPA, V/Q scan); immunology (autoimmune profile, APS — lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2-GPI); right heart catheterization.
- **Results:** CBC, renal, liver, coagulation normal; BNP 2,000 pg/mL; lupus anticoagulant and anticardiolipin positive; ECG right axis, RVH, RBBB; CXR enlarged pulmonary arteries; V/Q multiple segmental mismatched defects; RHC mPAP 48 mmHg, PCWP 12 mmHg, high PVR; SpO2 90%.
- **Diagnosis:** CTEPH secondary to antiphospholipid syndrome (WHO group 4).
- **Risk factors for CTEPH after PE:** APS / thrombophilia; inadequate anticoagulation; large or recurrent PE; chronic inflammation or infection; HF, COPD.
- **Management:** PH center referral; education, vaccines, O2, diuretics; lifelong warfarin (not DOAC in APS); pulmonary endarterectomy if operable; riociguat if inoperable or persistent; pulmonary rehab.

#### 3. Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (30F dental nurse, 2 years of dyspnea)

- **Clues:** exertional progressive dyspnea WHO II, fatigue; non-smoker, on OCP; BP 105/50, SpO2 95%; loud P2; chest otherwise normal.
- **DDx:** idiopathic PH, ILD, HF, PE, CTEPH, anemia.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; hematinics (iron, B12, folate); TSH; cardiac (ECG, echo, pro-BNP); blood gas; imaging (CXR, HRCT, abdominal US); PFT with DLCO; D-dimer; then V/Q scan, HIV, CTD antibodies (ANA, Scl-70, RF, anti-CCP), schistosoma serology, right heart catheterization with vasoreactivity.
- **Results:** CBC, renal, LFT normal; D-dimer 0.2 mg/L (normal); CXR normal; echo normal LV, mildly dilated RA / RV, RVSP 55 mmHg; PFT normal spirometry with isolated low DLCO; HIV, CTD and schistosoma negative; V/Q normal; RHC mPAP 66 mmHg; nitric oxide vasoreactivity negative.
- **Further tests:** RHC, V/Q scan, HIV, CTD antibodies (ANA, Scl-70, anti-CCP, RF), schistosoma antibodies → all negative, V/Q normal; RHC mPAP 66, negative vasoreactivity (nitric oxide).
- **Diagnosis:** idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension.
- **Management:** PH center; education; avoid pregnancy (contraception — stop estrogen OCP); vaccines; PDE5 inhibitor (sildenafil / tadalafil) + ERA (macitentan / bosentan); diuretics for RHF; home O2 if PaO2 < 60; anticoagulation / digoxin in selected cases; IV prostanoid (epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost) if severe; pulmonary rehab; atrial septostomy or heart-lung transplant if advanced.
- **Risk assessment factors:** clinical RHF, symptom progression, syncope, WHO class, 6MWT, cardiopulmonary exercise test, NT-proBNP, echo / cardiac MRI, hemodynamics.

#### 4. PE with APS → catastrophic APS (30F, sudden pleuritic chest pain, 6 hours)

- **Clues:** left calf pain and swelling for a week; 3 third-trimester fetal losses; HR 110, RR 25, SpO2 92%; raised JVP; loud P2; swollen warm tender left calf with dilated veins and livedo reticularis.
- **DDx:** PE / DVT due to APS, HF, IHD, pericarditis, pneumonia, pneumothorax, aortic dissection.
- **Workup:** pretest probability (Wells / modified Geneva); CBC; coagulation (PT, PTT); renal profile (before contrast); liver profile; pregnancy test; blood gas; D-dimer; cardiac (ECG, troponin, BNP, echo); APS screen; imaging (CTPA, lower-limb Doppler).
- **Results:** pregnancy test negative; CBC, renal, PT, LFT normal; PTT prolonged (lupus anticoagulant); ABG hypoxemia (SpO2 92% → 85%); D-dimer, BNP and troponin raised; APS screen positive; ECG sinus tachycardia with nonspecific ST–T changes; echo RV strain; DVT left common femoral vein; CTPA filling defect in the right main pulmonary artery; then BP 80/40 mmHg for 30 min.
- **Then:** hypotension 80/40 for 30 min despite resuscitation = high-risk (massive) PE → IV thrombolysis if no contraindication.
- **Thrombolysis contraindications:** prior ICH; structural cerebrovascular lesion; intracranial tumor; ischemic stroke within 3 months (unless within 4.5 h); suspected aortic dissection; active bleeding; bleeding diathesis; head / facial trauma within 3 months.
- **APS (revised Sapporo / Sydney):** ≥ 1 clinical + ≥ 1 lab; clinical = vascular thrombosis or pregnancy morbidity (≥ 1 fetal death ≥ 10 weeks, ≥ 1 premature birth < 34 weeks from preeclampsia / placental insufficiency, ≥ 3 consecutive losses < 10 weeks); lab = anticardiolipin IgG / IgM, anti-β2-GPI IgG / IgM, lupus anticoagulant (repeat after 12 weeks).
- **Day 4:** cerebral venous thrombosis + mesenteric ischemia = catastrophic APS.
- **CAPS treatment:** therapeutic heparin; high-dose steroids; IVIG and / or plasma exchange; rituximab if refractory.

### Interstitial lung disease

#### 5. Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (65M smoker, dry cough 3 years)

- **Clues:** progressive dyspnea WHO 3; no CTD symptoms, exposures or drugs; SpO2 87% on room air; clubbing; bibasal Velcro crackles; no PH signs.
- **DDx:** ILD, chronic lung infection, bronchiectasis, lung abscess, lung cancer.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential); renal profile + electrolytes; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); blood gas; urinalysis; autoimmune profile (ANA, RF, anti-CCP, Scl-70, ENA, ANCA, myositis panel); imaging (CXR, HRCT); PFT with DLCO; 6MWT; echo if PH suspected.
- **Results:** WBC, renal, LFT normal; ESR and CRP normal; autoimmune negative; SpO2 87% on room air; CXR diffuse reticular markings; HRCT bibasal peripheral honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis, reticulation = UIP.
- **Diagnosis:** IPF.
- **Management:** smoking cessation; vaccines; pulmonary rehab; O2; nutrition; anti-reflux therapy; pirfenidone or nintedanib; lung transplant referral; education; pulmonary referral.
- **Complications:** VTE, lung cancer, pulmonary hypertension, acute exacerbation, respiratory failure.

#### 6. IPF (63M smoker, 2 years of dyspnea, now at rest)

- **History asks:** dyspnea, cough, palpitations, chest pain, joint pain / RA, radiation, smoking, drugs (MTX, nitrofurantoin, bleomycin), occupational exposure, previous TB / infections.
- **Exam:** SpO2 88%, clubbing, bilateral end-inspiratory crackles.
- **Workup:** blood gas; CBC (eosinophils); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile; liver profile; immunology (ANA, RF, anti-CCP, Scl-70, ENA, ANCA, serum precipitins, IgE, ACE); infection (AFB smear, culture, PCR); imaging (CXR, HRCT, echo); PFT with DLCO; bronchoscopy with BAL; transbronchial / surgical biopsy or cryobiopsy if needed.
- **Results:** SpO2 88%; ABG hypoxemia with hypocapnia; PFT FVC 1.93 L (56%), FEV1 2.03 L (60%), FEV1/FVC 93%, TLC 3.21 L (53%), DLCO 42% → restrictive with low DLCO; HRCT honeycombing (UIP).
- **HRCT hallmarks (UIP):** subpleural, basal predominance; reticulation; honeycombing; traction bronchiectasis; no features of another diagnosis (extensive ground glass, nodules, consolidation).
- **Risk factors:** age > 50, male, smoking, family history, dust (wood, metal, silica), farming / birds, GERD.
- **Management:** education, stop smoking, rehab, long-term O2, antifibrotic (nintedanib / pirfenidone), lung transplant; no long-term steroids or immunosuppressants.
- **Steroids:** harmful long term (PANTHER-IPF); short course only in acute exacerbation.
- **Antifibrotics:** slow FVC decline by about 50% and reduce exacerbations but do not reverse fibrosis.
- **Monitoring:** PFT (FVC, DLCO), SpO2, 6MWT, symptoms, imaging if needed; cancer screening, vaccines.
- **Prognosis:** median survival 3–5 years; most die of respiratory failure.

#### 7. Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (55M farmer breeding pigeons, dry cough 10 months)

- **Clues:** dyspnea and fatigue; non-smoker; no drugs; SpO2 95%; reduced breath sounds and bilateral crackles.
- **DDx:** HP, occupational lung disease, sarcoidosis, IPF, chronic eosinophilic pneumonia, CTD / vasculitis, TB.
- **Workup:** CBC (eosinophils); renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); TB tests (PPD / QuantiFERON, AFB); cardiac (ECG, echo, pro-BNP); blood gas; imaging (CXR, HRCT); PFT with DLCO; immunology (autoimmune profile, serum precipitins / specific IgG to avian antigen); bronchoscopy with BAL (lymphocytosis).
- **Results:** CBC, renal, LFT normal; CRP 52 mg/L; ESR 45 mm/h; autoimmune negative; echo normal; PPD / QuantiFERON negative; SpO2 95%; CXR bilateral reticular opacities; HRCT centrilobular micronodules and mosaic attenuation; PFT restrictive with low DLCO.
- **Diagnosis:** hypersensitivity pneumonitis (extrinsic allergic alveolitis).
- **Management:** antigen avoidance (key); corticosteroids; bronchodilators; vaccines; pulmonary referral; rehab; lung transplant if end-stage.
- **Poor prognosis:** smoking, low baseline VC, no BAL lymphocytosis, ongoing exposure, unidentified antigen (plus fibrosis on HRCT).

### Airway disease

#### 8. COPD GOLD 3 group B (66M, 40 pack-years)

- **Clues:** 3 months of progressive dyspnea mMRC 2, productive yellow sputum; barrel chest, hyperresonance, expiratory wheeze; SpO2 93%.
- **DDx:** COPD, asthma, HF, bronchiectasis.
- **Workup:** CBC (polycythemia, eosinophils); renal profile; liver profile; blood gas; cardiac (ECG, echo, BNP); sputum (culture, AFB); alpha-1 antitrypsin level (once in all COPD); imaging (CXR, CT chest); spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility.
- **Results:** CBC and chemistry normal; echo and BNP normal; sputum culture and AFB negative; SpO2 93%; CXR hyperinflation, flat diaphragms; CT bilateral emphysema; PFT obstructive (FEV1/FVC < 0.7), not reversible; FEV1 30–49% predicted = GOLD 3; mMRC 2 with no frequent exacerbations = group B.
- **Diagnosis:** COPD (emphysema), GOLD 3 (FEV1 30–49%), group B (mMRC ≥ 2, no frequent exacerbations).
- **Management:** education; smoking cessation; vaccines; pulmonary rehab; LAMA + LABA; lung cancer screening; LVRS referral; assess for home O2 / NIV.
- **Reduce mortality:** smoking cessation, long-term O2 (PaO2 ≤ 55 or SpO2 ≤ 88%), triple therapy (in frequent exacerbators), pulmonary rehab (after exacerbation), NIV in chronic hypercapnia, LVRS in selected patients.

#### 9. COPD with back-of-chest exam (57M, 40 pack-years, mMRC 4)

- **Exam from the back:** sitting upright, hands crossed on shoulders; inspect (asymmetry, accessory muscles, scars, radiotherapy marks, kyphoscoliosis, barrel chest); palpate (tenderness, expansion at 3 levels, TVF); percuss each posterior space plus tidal percussion; auscultate with vocal resonance; legs for edema.
- **Findings:** barrel chest, hyperresonance, expiratory wheeze, SpO2 93%.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; blood gas; cardiac (ECG, echo, BNP); sputum (culture, AFB); alpha-1 antitrypsin; imaging (CXR, CT chest); spirometry.
- **Results:** CBC, chemistry, echo and BNP normal; sputum culture and AFB negative; SpO2 93%; CXR hyperinflation; CT emphysema; PFT obstructive with no reversibility.
- **Diagnosis:** COPD (emphysema).
- **Management:** education, smoking cessation, vaccines, rehab, LAMA + LABA, lung cancer screening, LVRS referral, home O2 / NIV assessment.

#### 10. Bronchial asthma (18M student, cough 3 months)

- **Clues:** productive small-volume cough, worse at night, with exercise, perfumes and dust; dyspnea playing football; ER nebulization last week; SpO2 97%, bilateral expiratory wheeze.
- **Workup:** CBC with eosinophils; total IgE ± specific IgE / skin-prick tests; FeNO; cardiac (BNP, echo) to exclude cardiac cause; imaging (CXR); spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility, PEFR variability (± methacholine challenge if spirometry normal).
- **Results:** CBC normal; BNP and echo normal; CXR normal; FVC 3.15 L (89%); FEV1 2.07 L (66%); FEV1/FVC 65%; after bronchodilator FEV1 2.52 L (+14%, +450 mL) = significant reversibility (≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL).
- **Diagnosis:** bronchial asthma.
- **Management:** pulmonology referral; education (disease course, trigger avoidance, inhaler technique, side effects, adherence, when to seek urgent help, written action plan); weight loss, no smoking; ICS-LABA per GINA / SINA; regular follow-up for control, technique, adherence and comorbidities (GERD, rhinosinusitis).
- **Control (last 4 weeks):** daytime symptoms > twice / week; night waking; SABA reliever > twice / week; activity limitation.
- **Near-fatal asthma risks:** previous intubation / ICU; current or recent oral steroids; not on ICS; SABA overuse (> 1 canister / month); poor adherence / no action plan; psychiatric or psychosocial problems.

#### 11. Cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis (24M, productive cough > 2 years)

- **Clues:** yellow sputum, mild dyspnea; married 2 years without children; recurrent sinusitis and chest infections; low weight; bilateral coarse crackles.
- **DDx:** CF, Kartagener / primary ciliary dyskinesia, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, hypogammaglobulinemia.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential); renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); sputum (bacterial culture incl. Pseudomonas / S. aureus, AFB, PPD / QuantiFERON); imaging (CXR, HRCT); PFT; etiology (sweat chloride, CFTR genotype, immunoglobulins, alpha-1 antitrypsin, nasal NO / ciliary biopsy, autoimmune profile, ABPA screen — IgE, Aspergillus IgE / IgG); semen analysis.
- **Results:** WBC 11.5 ×10⁹/L with 77% neutrophils; renal and LFT normal; ESR 55 mm/h; PPD / QuantiFERON negative; autoimmune negative; PFT moderate obstruction; sweat chloride 70 mmol/L (≥ 60 = CF; 30–59 intermediate); CFTR mutation positive; CXR hyperinflation, bronchial wall thickening; HRCT bilateral cystic bronchiectasis with mucus plugging.
- **Diagnosis:** bronchiectasis due to cystic fibrosis.
- **Management:** admit if exacerbation; bronchodilators; culture-guided antibiotics, inhaled tobramycin for Pseudomonas; dornase alfa; hypertonic saline 3–7%; chest physiotherapy, exercise, rehab; nutrition, pancreatic enzymes, fat-soluble vitamins, B12, iron; vaccines; CFTR modulators; lung transplant evaluation; pulmonary referral and education.
- **Complications:** recurrent infection, hemoptysis, pneumothorax, DM, pancreatitis, malabsorption / weight loss, distal intestinal obstruction, osteoporosis, infertility.

#### 12. Post-TB bronchiectasis (55M smoker, productive cough 3 months)

- **Clues:** copious morning sputum, minimal hemoptysis, improved with antibiotics before; treated pulmonary TB 2 years ago; 20 pack-years; coarse crackles, no clubbing.
- **DDx:** post-TB bronchiectasis, lung cancer, TB reinfection.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile + electrolytes; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); sputum (culture, AFB × 3, TB PCR); imaging (CXR, HRCT); PFT + blood gas; immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM, IgE); ABPA screen.
- **Results:** CBC, chemistry and ESR normal; sputum culture and AFB negative; CT right upper lobe bronchiectasis; spirometry FEV1 1.36 L (51%), FEV1/FVC 45%, post-bronchodilator +3% (not reversible).
- **Diagnosis:** post-TB bronchiectasis.
- **Other causes:** post-infective (pertussis, measles, recurrent pneumonia); immunodeficiency (hypogammaglobulinemia, HIV); mucociliary defect (CF, PCD); ABPA; autoimmune (RA, Sjögren, SLE, IBD); obstruction (foreign body, tumor).
- **Management:** education, pulmonology; exercise, nutrition, smoking cessation; O2 assessment; bronchodilators if obstruction; vaccines; airway clearance (postural drainage, flutter device, saline nebulizers); culture-guided antibiotics for exacerbations; long-term macrolide if ≥ 3 exacerbations / year.
- **Complications:** infective exacerbations, hemoptysis, pneumothorax, respiratory failure (and cor pulmonale, amyloidosis).

### Lung cancer and TB

#### 13. Pancoast tumor — EGFR-mutant adenocarcinoma (35F non-smoker teacher)

- **Clues:** months of dyspnea, cough, hemoptysis not improving on inhalers; right neck pain radiating to shoulder and ring / little fingers (C8–T1); 10 kg weight loss; clubbing, pallor; reduced air entry right apex; right ptosis; CXR right upper lobe mass.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation (PT / PTT — before biopsy); renal profile; liver profile; electrolytes + Ca; imaging (CXR, contrast CT chest); tissue (CT-guided or bronchoscopic biopsy with histology + molecular — EGFR, ALK, ROS1, PD-L1, KRAS, BRAF); staging (CT abdomen, MRI / CT brain, bone scan or PET-CT); MRI of the brachial plexus / thoracic inlet for Pancoast.
- **Horner syndrome:** ipsilateral ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis, enophthalmos (sympathetic chain invasion).
- **Pathology:** adenocarcinoma → young non-smoker → request molecular testing (EGFR, ALK, ROS1; also PD-L1, KRAS, BRAF).
- **Staging:** CT chest / abdomen, brain MRI or CT, bone scan (or PET-CT) → liver and bone metastases = stage IV.
- **Management:** MDT. The checklist says neoadjuvant chemo → surgery → radiotherapy with no adjuvant role; for metastatic EGFR-positive disease the standard answer is an EGFR TKI (osimertinib) plus palliative RT for pain / bone.

#### 14. Squamous cell lung cancer with hypercalcemia (66M, 35 pack-years, hemoptysis 2 days)

- **Clues:** tablespoon-sized fresh hemoptysis 2–3 times; dry cough 3 months; 12 kg weight loss; abdominal pain and constipation (hypercalcemia); clubbing; left mid-zone crackles.
- **DDx:** lung cancer, bronchiectasis, TB, lung abscess.
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); electrolytes (Na — SIADH); bone panel (Ca, albumin, PO4, PTH, PTHrP); urinalysis; sputum (cytology, culture, AFB, PPD / QuantiFERON); D-dimer; autoimmune profile; imaging (CXR, contrast CT); bronchoscopy; PFT (fitness for surgery).
- **Results:** WBC 10.5 ×10⁹/L (65% neutrophils); Hb 13.5 g/dL (135 g/L); renal and LFT normal; ESR 55 mm/h; CRP 40 mg/L; Ca 3.0 mmol/L (≈ 12 mg/dL; normal 2–2.6 mmol/L = 8–10.4 mg/dL); Na 135 mmol/L (low-normal); D-dimer 0.3 mg/L; TB tests and autoimmune negative; urinalysis normal; CXR large well-circumscribed left mid-zone mass; CT large left lower lobe mass.
- **Diagnosis:** lung cancer, likely squamous cell (smoker, central mass, PTHrP hypercalcemia).
- **Management:** ABC; isolate until TB excluded; admit; CT body, PET, brain MRI; bronchoscopic or CT-guided biopsy; treat hypercalcemia (IV saline, then bisphosphonate / denosumab; loop diuretic only once volume replete); counseling and smoking cessation; refer pulmonology, oncology, thoracic surgery.
- **Risk factors:** smoking, asbestos, arsenic, radon.
- **Screening (USPSTF):** annual low-dose CT; age 50–80; ≥ 20 pack-years; current smoker or quit within 15 years.

#### 15. Pulmonary TB (45M from India, hemoptysis 1 month)

- **Clues:** minimal hemoptysis 2–3 times / day, yellow sputum, weight loss, night sweats; non-smoker; arrived in KSA 3 months ago; right lung crackles.
- **DDx:** TB, lung cancer, bronchiectasis, lung abscess.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile (baseline before RIPE); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); sputum (AFB smear × 3, TB PCR / GeneXpert with rifampicin resistance, culture with sensitivity); HIV; baseline visual acuity and color vision; imaging (CXR, CT chest).
- **Results:** CBC, renal and liver normal; AFB positive in 3 samples; TB PCR positive; culture pending; ESR 108 mm/h; CXR multifocal patchy opacities in the right upper lobe.
- **Diagnosis:** pulmonary TB.
- **Management:** airborne isolation; notify public health; education; RIPE (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) + pyridoxine; HIV screen; baseline CBC, renal, LFT; visual acuity / ophthalmology (ethambutol); contact screening; pulmonology referral; DOT.
- **Duration:** 6 months (2 months RIPE + 4 months RI).
- **INH side effects:** hepatitis, peripheral neuropathy, drug-induced lupus, nausea (plus rash, B6 deficiency).

### Infection and pleural disease

#### 16. Community-acquired pneumonia, severe (62M, fever and pleuritic chest pain 2 days)

- **Clues:** dyspnea at rest, productive sputum, fever 39; HTN; wife had URTI; came from India; confused; T 38.9, HR 105, BP 88/52, RR 31, SpO2 88%; bronchial breathing and crackles right lower lung.
- **DDx:** bacterial or viral pneumonia, aspiration pneumonia, bronchitis, TB, PE, endocarditis.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (urea for CURB-65, creatinine); electrolytes; blood gas; infection (sputum Gram stain and culture, 2 blood cultures, procalcitonin, COVID / viral PCR, sputum AFB, urine Legionella / pneumococcal antigen in severe CAP); imaging (CXR).
- **Results:** WBC 19 ×10⁹/L (19,000/µL); urea 7.8 mmol/L (BUN 22 mg/dL; > 7 mmol/L scores in CURB-65); sputum Gram-positive diplococci in pairs (pneumococcus); cultures and AFB pending; procalcitonin 2 ng/mL (bacterial); COVID PCR negative; BP 88/52 mmHg, RR 31, SpO2 88%; CXR right lower zone opacity.
- **Diagnosis:** severe CAP (CURB-65 = 4: confusion, urea > 7, RR ≥ 30, low BP).
- **Management:** resuscitate (IV fluids, O2); ICU; negative-pressure isolation until TB excluded; ceftriaxone / ertapenem / ampicillin-sulbactam + azithromycin.
- **Anti-pseudomonal indications:** prior Pseudomonas, structural lung disease / severe COPD / bronchiectasis, frequent antibiotics or steroids, recent hospitalization, Gram-negative bacilli on Gram stain.
- **Anti-pseudomonal regimen:** piperacillin-tazobactam / imipenem / meropenem / cefepime / ceftazidime + ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin.
- **Severity scores:** CURB-65 and PSI (also ATS / IDSA criteria for ICU).

#### 17. Parapneumonic effusion (42M police officer, dyspnea 3 weeks)

- **Clues:** preceding URTI with low-grade fever; green sputum, right pleuritic pain; sick contact at work; T 37.5, SpO2 93%; reduced expansion, stony dullness and reduced breath sounds right lower zone.
- **DDx:** pleural effusion, pneumonia, lung abscess, bronchiectasis.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); cardiac (enzymes, pro-BNP); electrolytes; serum total protein + LDH (for Light's criteria); sputum culture; autoimmune profile; imaging (CXR, chest US, CT chest); PFT; thoracentesis.
- **Results:** WBC 11 ×10⁹/L, mainly neutrophils; ESR 73 mm/h; CRP 30 mg/L; cardiac enzymes normal; serum LDH 185 U/L; serum total protein 9 g/dL (90 g/L); sputum culture pending; autoimmune negative; SpO2 93%; CXR blunted right costophrenic angle with meniscus.
- **Pleural fluid request:** appearance, cell count and differential, protein, LDH, albumin, glucose, pH, cholesterol, Gram stain and culture, cytology, ADA.
- **Fluid result:** yellow, turbid; 70% neutrophils; pH 7.27; glucose 55 mg/dL (3.1 mmol/L); total protein 6.5 g/dL (65 g/L; ratio 0.72); LDH 130 U/L vs serum 185 (ratio 0.70); culture negative; cytology negative; ADA 20 U/L (low — against TB).
- **Interpretation:** exudate (Light: fluid / serum LDH > 0.6, protein ratio > 0.5); pH < 7.30 and low glucose = complicated parapneumonic effusion (pH < 7.2 needs drainage).
- **Diagnosis:** parapneumonic effusion.
- **Management:** analgesia; pulmonary and thoracic surgery consults; DVT prophylaxis; broad-spectrum antibiotics; chest tube if large, pH < 7.2, pus or positive Gram stain / culture; intrapleural tPA / DNase or VATS if loculated.

#### 18. Hepatic hydrothorax (60M retired soldier, dyspnea 3 weeks)

- **Clues:** smoker, BMI 35; dry cough; clubbing, palmar erythema, jaundice; reduced right expansion, stony dullness, reduced breath sounds right mid-lower zone; distended abdomen with ascites and dilated abdominal veins.
- **DDx:** pleural effusion, lung cancer, liver cirrhosis, HF, CAP.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile + albumin + total protein; coagulation (PT / INR, PTT); cardiac (enzymes, pro-BNP, echo); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); serum LDH; autoimmune profile; TB (PPD / QuantiFERON); viral and autoimmune hepatitis screen; urinalysis; imaging (CXR, chest US, CT chest, abdominal US with Doppler); diagnostic thoracentesis (cell count, protein, LDH, glucose, pH, culture, cytology, ADA).
- **Results:** WBC 9 ×10⁹/L; cardiac enzymes and pro-BNP normal; LFT and bilirubin high; INR 2; albumin 3 g/dL (30 g/L); serum total protein 5 g/dL (50 g/L); LDH 70 U/L; ESR 30 mm/h; CRP 10 mg/L; PPD / QuantiFERON positive (latent only); autoimmune and hepatitis screen negative; US shrunken nodular liver with large ascites; fluid clear straw-colored, WBC 150, normal pH and glucose, fluid / serum protein < 0.4, LDH ratio 0.3 → transudate.
- **Fluid:** clear straw-colored, WBC 150, normal pH and glucose; protein ratio < 0.5, LDH ratio 0.3 = transudate.
- **Diagnosis:** transudative effusion from cirrhosis = hepatic hydrothorax.
- **Management:** hepatology referral and work up the cause of cirrhosis; treat ascites (salt restriction + diuretics); education, nutrition, vaccines; therapeutic thoracentesis for symptoms; TIPS if refractory; liver transplant; avoid chest tube (renal failure, protein loss, infection, circulatory collapse).
- **Complications:** spontaneous bacterial empyema, fibrothorax / trapped lung, respiratory failure.

### Critical care

#### 19. ARDS from COVID-19 pneumonia (32M, cough 2 days, now dyspneic)

- **History asks:** cough and sputum; fever, chest pain; NYHA; recent flu-like illness, sick or COVID contact; CVS, renal, autoimmune (rash, arthralgia, conjunctivitis); asthma, DM, HTN; previous ICU; drugs, smoking.
- **Exam:** GCS, vitals, distress signs (nasal flaring, accessory muscles, tracheal tug, abdominal breathing); full chest, CVS, CNS, volume and abdomen; skin / MSK for a source; glucose, urine dipstick.
- **DDx:** type 1 respiratory failure / ARDS from COVID, asthma or COPD exacerbation, neuromuscular disease (GBS, MG), inflammatory myositis.
- **Workup:** blood gas + lactate; CBC; coagulation; renal profile; liver profile; electrolytes (K, Mg, Ca, PO4); cardiac (ECG, CK, troponin, D-dimer); inflammatory markers (CRP, LDH, ferritin); infection (blood / urine / sputum cultures, COVID and respiratory viral PCR); imaging (CXR).
- **Results:** accessory-muscle use; PaO2 52 mmHg (6.9 kPa); lactate 4 mmol/L (36 mg/dL); CXR bilateral alveolar-interstitial infiltrates; COVID PCR positive; after 1 h on NIV SpO2 82% with paradoxical breathing → intubated.
- **Diagnosis:** type 1 respiratory failure / ARDS due to COVID-19 pneumonia.
- **Management:** ABC; ICU; NIV / HFNC; dexamethasone; antibiotics if bacterial infection suspected; antiviral (remdesivir / favipiravir per MOH); electrolytes; DVT and GI prophylaxis; ID consult.
- **After NIV failure (SpO2 82%, paradoxical breathing):** intubate and ventilate.
- **Lung-protective ventilation:** tidal volume 4–6 (up to 8) mL/kg ideal body weight; plateau pressure ≤ 30 cmH2O; SpO2 target 88–94%; PEEP ≥ 5 to prevent atelectrauma.
- **Non-ventilatory measures:** treat the cause; permissive hypercapnia (pH ≥ 7.25); neuromuscular blockade in severe ARDS; prone positioning (PaO2/FiO2 < 150); conservative fluids.
- **Tocilizumab:** within 24 h of ICU admission when O2 need escalates to HFNC, NIV or MV, and CRP ≥ 75 mg/L.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Bronchial breathing + increased vocal resonance:** consolidation, cavity / abscess, mass, collapse with patent bronchus, bronchiectasis.
- **Effusion vs consolidation:** both dull; effusion stony dull with absent vocal resonance and breath sounds; consolidation increased resonance and bronchial breathing.
- **PH workup sequence:** echo (PASP) → ECG / CXR → PFT with DLCO → V/Q (exclude CTEPH) → CTD / HIV / liver → right heart cath (mPAP > 20, PCWP ≤ 15 = precapillary).
- **Light's criteria (exudate if any):** fluid / serum protein > 0.5; fluid / serum LDH > 0.6; fluid LDH > two-thirds of the upper limit of normal serum LDH.
- **Smoking-related, high-yield:** LDCT screening criteria; COPD mortality measures; squamous cancer → hypercalcemia; small cell → SIADH / Cushing / LEMS.
- **Vaccines in chronic lung disease:** influenza, pneumococcal, COVID, RSV (≥ 60), pertussis.
