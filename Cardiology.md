# Cardiology

## How to use this

All 20 cardiology stations in the Qassim 2021–2025 set fall into five presentations: chest pain, palpitations, shortness of breath / HF, syncope, and pre-op assessment. Learn the core history and exam once, then add the block for the presentation in front of you.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; check stability first (ABCD, vitals, SpO2, GCS); note what the patient is connected to (O2, monitor, IV line).
- **Stations covered:** chest pain (inferior MI + RV infarct, anterior MI + AV block, type A and type B dissection, post-PCI pericarditis); palpitations (AF ×4, VT post-MI, NSVT in cardiac sarcoid, HOCM ×2); SOB (peripartum CMP, MS ×2, ADHF from NSAIDs, severe AS); pre-op risk after DES.

## 1. Core cardiac history (every case)

- **Presenting complaint:** onset, duration, course, frequency, severity, triggers, relieving factors, previous similar episodes.
- **Cardiac symptoms:** chest pain, exertional dyspnea (NYHA class), orthopnea (pillows), PND, palpitations, presyncope / syncope, leg swelling, weight gain, fatigue.
- **Risk factors:** DM, HTN, dyslipidemia, smoking (pack-years, water pipe), obesity, family history of premature CAD / sudden death.
- **Known heart disease:** IHD (MI, PCI — stent number, site, type DES vs BMS, CABG), valve disease / rheumatic fever, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, pacemaker / ICD, valve replacement, last echo.
- **Other systems:** thyroid symptoms; anemia / bleeding; chest (cough, hemoptysis, fever); stroke / TIA; CKD; OSA; CTD (SLE, RA); constitutional symptoms.
- **Drugs:** antiplatelets (ASA, clopidogrel, ticagrelor), anticoagulants, beta blockers, ACEi / ARB / ARNI, diuretics, nitrates, antiarrhythmics, insulin / hypoglycemics, salbutamol, thyroxine, OCP, NSAIDs, steroids, compliance.
- **Social:** smoking, alcohol, caffeine, cocaine / amphetamines / illicit drugs, functional status (stairs, ADLs), occupation.
- **Family:** IHD, sudden cardiac death, cardiomyopathy, Marfan, bicuspid aortic valve, congenital heart disease.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Chest pain

- **SOCRATES:** site, onset (sudden vs gradual), character (crushing vs tearing vs pleuritic), radiation (jaw / arm vs back), associations, timing, exacerbating / relieving factors, severity /10.
- **ACS:** crushing retrosternal pain radiating to jaw / left arm; sweating, nausea; exertional; DM patients present atypically (epigastric pain); prior IHD.
- **Aortic dissection:** sudden severe tearing pain to the back; uncontrolled HTN / non-compliance; cocaine; Marfan or bicuspid valve in family; CNS symptoms (stroke, syncope); limb ischemia; recent aortic manipulation.
- **Pericarditis:** sharp, pleuritic, worse lying flat, relieved sitting forward; recent MI (days = early pericarditis, weeks = Dressler); viral prodrome; CTD, uremia, TB.
- **PE / other:** long travel, immobilization, leg swelling; chest trauma (pneumothorax); GI (PUD, esophagitis, cholecystitis); costochondritis.

### B. Palpitations

- **Character:** onset / offset (sudden = SVT / VT), fast or slow, regular or irregular, duration, frequency, at rest or exertion, how it terminates.
- **Red flags:** syncope / presyncope, chest pain, HF symptoms, palpitations on exertion, family history of sudden death.
- **Cardiac causes:** prior MI (VT), valve disease / rheumatic fever (AF with MS), HOCM, cardiomyopathy, cardiac sarcoid.
- **Non-cardiac causes:** hyperthyroidism (tremor, heat intolerance, weight loss, diarrhea); anemia / bleeding; hypoglycemia (insulin, sulfonylurea); pheochromocytoma; anxiety / panic; caffeine, alcohol, nicotine; menopause.
- **Drugs:** beta agonists, pseudoephedrine, thyroxine, beta-blocker withdrawal, diuretics (low K / Mg), QT-prolonging drugs.
- **AF complications:** stroke / TIA, bleeding on anticoagulation.

### C. Shortness of breath / heart failure

- **Dyspnea:** onset, progression, NYHA I–IV, orthopnea, PND.
- **Congestion:** ankle / leg swelling, abdominal distension, RUQ discomfort, weight gain, reduced urine output.
- **Precipitants:** NSAIDs, TZDs, steroids, diuretic non-adherence, salt / fluid excess, new AF, ACS, infection, uncontrolled HTN.
- **Cause of cardiomyopathy:** ischemic (MI, PCI, CABG); hypertensive; valvular (rheumatic fever); peripartum (delivery within last months, preeclampsia); viral; alcohol; thyroid; CTD; family history.
- **Pregnancy:** gestation, previous pregnancies, preeclampsia.
- **Valve symptoms:** exertional syncope and angina (AS); hemoptysis, palpitations (MS).

### D. Syncope / LOC

- **Event:** what he was doing (exertion = HOCM / AS), prodrome, duration, abnormal movements, incontinence, tongue bite, post-ictal confusion, injury, full recovery.
- **Cardiac clues:** palpitations before, exertional, chest pain, family history of SCD.
- **Other:** hypoglycemia, seizure, stroke / TIA, orthostasis, vasovagal triggers.

### E. Pre-operative assessment (after recent MI / stent)

- **Cardiac event:** timing of MI, type of PCI, stent number, site, type (DES vs BMS).
- **Current status:** chest pain, dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, palpitations, syncope.
- **RCRI items:** type of surgery, IHD, HF, stroke, DM on insulin, creatinine > 2 mg/dL.
- **Functional capacity:** stairs climbed, exercise (≥ 4 METs).
- **Medications:** ASA, clopidogrel, beta blocker, ACEi, statin, insulin.

## 3. Core cardiac exam (every case)

- **General:** ill / well, distress, position (orthopneic), sweating, pallor, cyanosis, jaundice, connections (O2, monitor).
- **Vitals:** BP (both arms in chest pain), HR, rhythm, RR, temperature, SpO2, glucose.
- **Pulse:** rate, rhythm (irregularly irregular), volume, character (slow-rising, bounding), radio-radial and radio-femoral delay, pulse deficit.
- **Hands / face / neck:** IE stigmata (splinters, Osler, Janeway), clubbing, mitral facies, exophthalmos, thyroid, JVP (height, cannon a waves, absent a wave in AF).
- **Precordium inspection:** sternotomy / thoracotomy scar, device (pacemaker / ICD), visible pulsation, deformity (pectus).
- **Palpation:** apex position and character (tapping, sustained, displaced, trifid), thrill, parasternal heave, palpable S4.
- **Auscultation:** S1 (loud in MS, variable in AF), S2 (soft in AS, loud P2 in pulmonary HTN), S3 / S4, murmurs (timing, site, radiation, maneuvers), opening snap, pericardial rub.
- **Chest:** basal crackles, effusion.
- **Abdomen:** hepatomegaly, ascites, pulsatile mass.
- **Legs:** pitting edema (level), DVT signs, peripheral pulses.
- **Neuro:** focal deficit (dissection, AF embolism).

## 4. Exam add-ons by diagnosis

- **ACS / inferior MI:** bradycardia and heart block; hypotension; raised JVP with clear chest = RV infarct; new murmur (MR, VSR); crackles (Killip class).
- **Aortic dissection:** BP differential between arms; pulse deficit; early diastolic murmur of AR; Marfan habitus (tall, pectus, arachnodactyly, high palate); focal neurology.
- **Pericarditis / tamponade:** friction rub; Beck triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds); pulsus paradoxus; tachycardia.
- **AF:** irregularly irregular pulse, pulse deficit, variable S1, no a wave; look for cause (MS murmur, thyroid signs, HF).
- **Mitral stenosis:** malar flush, tapping apex, loud S1, opening snap, mid-diastolic rumble at apex (left lateral position, bell), loud P2, AF.
- **Aortic stenosis:** slow-rising low-volume pulse, narrow pulse pressure, sustained apex, soft S2, ejection systolic murmur right 2nd ICS radiating to carotids.
- **HOCM:** jerky pulse, double / trifid apex, S4, ejection murmur at LLSB louder with Valsalva / standing and softer with squatting; apical MR murmur (SAM).
- **Heart failure:** orthopneic, raised JVP, displaced apex, S3 gallop, basal crackles, effusions, hepatomegaly, ascites, pitting edema; warm vs cold, wet vs dry.
- **Cardiac sarcoid:** erythema nodosum, lupus pernio, facial palsy, lymphadenopathy, lung crackles.
- **Endocarditis screen (any murmur):** fever, splinter hemorrhages, Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, Roth spots.

## 5. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### Chest pain

#### 1. Inferior-posterior MI + RV infarct + complete heart block (75M, DM, epigastric pain)

- **Clues:** epigastric pain > 3 h radiating to chest, neck, left arm; sweating, nausea, dizziness; heavy smoker; HR 40, BP 90/40, raised JVP, clear chest.
- **DDx:** MI, pericarditis, PUD, cholecystitis, costochondritis, esophageal spasm, esophagitis.
- **Workup:** ECG (12-lead + right-sided V4R, posterior V7–V9); cardiac markers (troponin, CK-MB); CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); imaging (CXR, echo)
- **Results:** ECG: ST elevation II, III, aVF + ST depression V1–V3 (posterior) + complete heart block (HR 40) + ST elevation V4R; BP 90/40; echo: EF ≈ 40%, inferior and posterior hypokinesia, no valve disease
- **Diagnosis:** acute inferior-posterior STEMI complicated by RV infarction and complete heart block.
- **Management:** aspirin load; clopidogrel / ticagrelor load; heparin; IV fluids (RV infarct); cardiology; coronary angiography for PCI; temporary pacemaker; CCU.
- **Examiner Qs:** nitrates contraindicated in RV infarct (preload dependent); long term = aspirin, clopidogrel, beta blocker, ACEi / ARB, statin, cardiac rehab, education, follow-up.

#### 2. Anterior STEMI + AV block (65M, DM, HTN, smoker)

- **Clues:** crushing pain radiating to neck, jaw, left arm; sweating; HR 50, BP 95/60; few crackles.
- **DDx:** ACS, aortic dissection, PE, aortic stenosis, pericarditis, costochondritis.
- **Workup:** ECG; cardiac markers (troponin, CK-MB); CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); coagulation (PT / INR, aPTT); imaging (CXR, echo); coronary angiography
- **Results:** troponin positive; ECG: ST elevation I, aVL, V1–V6 with RBBB and AV block (HR 45–50, QRS 162 ms); BP 95/60; echo: EF 35% with LAD-territory hypokinesia
- **Management:** aspirin load; clopidogrel / ticagrelor load; heparin; O2; cardiology; activate cath lab for primary PCI; temporary pacemaker.
- **After PCI:** CCU; DAPT; beta blocker; ACEi / ARB / ARNI; statin; permanent pacemaker if AV block persists > 24 h.
- **MI complications:** post-MI angina, HF, cardiogenic shock, pulmonary edema, acute MR (papillary muscle), VSR, free wall rupture, LV thrombus, pericarditis, Dressler syndrome, arrhythmias.
- **Discharge advice:** smoking cessation, medication compliance, clinic follow-up, cardiac rehab, BP and glucose control.

#### 3. Type B aortic dissection (65M, HTN non-compliant, smoker)

- **Clues:** sudden severe tearing pain radiating to back; BP 170/80; no deficit, no murmur.
- **DDx:** aortic dissection, ACS, PE, pericarditis, myocarditis, AS, costochondritis.
- **Workup:** ECG; cardiac markers (troponin); CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN — before contrast); coagulation + group and crossmatch; imaging (CXR, CT angiography of the aorta; MRA or TEE if CT not possible)
- **Results:** ECG and troponin normal; CXR: wide mediastinum; CTA: dissection flap in the descending thoracic aorta (Stanford B)
- **Management:** IV beta blocker first (esmolol, labetalol); then nitroprusside; analgesia (morphine); cardiology and vascular surgery referral.
- **Long term:** oral beta blocker, ACEi / ARB or CCB; surveillance CTA / MRA.

#### 4. Type A aortic dissection + Marfan (40–45M)

- **Clues:** crushing retrosternal pain to back; BP right arm 180/68 vs left 150/55; pectus excavatum; early diastolic murmur (AR).
- **DDx:** aortic dissection, ACS, PE, pneumothorax.
- **Workup:** ECG; cardiac markers (troponin); CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); liver profile; coagulation + crossmatch; toxicology (cocaine); imaging (CXR, CT angiography; echo / TEE if unstable)
- **Results:** BP right arm 180/68 vs left arm 150/55, HR 101; ECG: inferior ST elevation (flap into the right coronary); echo: dilated aortic root with AR, hyperdynamic EF > 60%; CXR: wide mediastinum / double aortic contour; CTA: flap in the ascending aorta
- **Diagnosis:** Stanford type A (ascending) dissection.
- **Management:** ICU; analgesia; urgent cardiac surgery; HR ≈ 60 and SBP < 110 with IV beta blocker (esmolol, labetalol) then vasodilator (nitroprusside, nicardipine); screen first-degree relatives.
- **Marfan major features:** skeletal (pectus, scoliosis, arachnodactyly); ocular (ectopia lentis); cardiovascular (aortic root dilatation, dissection).
- **Classification:** Stanford A = ascending ± arch (surgery); Stanford B = distal to left subclavian (medical unless complicated); DeBakey I (ascending + descending), II (ascending only), III (descending).

#### 5. Post-MI acute pericarditis (58M, day 1 after anterior MI + PCI)

- **Clues:** sharp retrosternal pain to trapezius, worse lying and inspiration, relieved sitting forward; friction rub; no JVP rise.
- **DDx:** early pericarditis, stent thrombosis, free wall rupture, tamponade.
- **Workup:** ECG; cardiac markers (troponin, NT-proBNP); CBC (with differential); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile; metabolic (HbA1c, TFT, iron); imaging (CXR, echo)
- **Results:** ECG: diffuse concave ST elevation + PR depression (PR elevation in aVR); WBC 12,000/µL (12 × 10⁹/L); Hb 13 g/dL (130 g/L); platelets 400,000/µL (400 × 10⁹/L); HbA1c 5.3% (34 mmol/mol); TFT normal; CXR: bottle-shaped heart; echo: EF 55%, mild–moderate pericardial effusion
- **Diagnosis (2 of 4):** typical pain; rub; ECG changes; new / worsening effusion.
- **ECG stages:** diffuse STE + PR depression → normalization → diffuse T inversion → T normalization.
- **Management:** aspirin 600–1200 mg q8h tapered over 2 weeks; colchicine 0.5 mg BID (once daily if < 60 kg) for 3 months; CCU and pericardiocentesis if tamponade.
- **Complications:** tamponade, constrictive pericarditis, myopericarditis.
- **Tamponade signs:** Beck triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds); pulsus paradoxus; tachycardia.

### Palpitations and arrhythmia

#### 6. Fast AF in IHD (75M)

- **Clues:** exertional palpitations with syncope and sweating; HR 140 irregularly irregular, BP 100/60; no a wave; variable S1; crackles.
- **DDx:** IHD, VHD, HF, hyperthyroidism, HTN, PE, OSA, COPD, alcohol, cardiomyopathy, constrictive pericarditis.
- **Workup:** ECG + Holter; CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); coagulation (PT / INR); metabolic / endocrine (TSH, free T4, glucose); imaging (CXR, echo)
- **Results:** ECG: AF, HR 140, irregularly irregular, no P waves; BP 100/60; TSH 3 mIU/L, T3 / T4 normal; echo: EF 40%, anterior wall hypokinesia, no valve disease
- **Management:** treat reversible causes; rate vs rhythm control; thromboprophylaxis (warfarin vs NOAC).
- **AF types:** valvular / non-valvular; paroxysmal (< 7 days); persistent (> 7 days); long-standing persistent (> 1 year); permanent (no further rhythm control).
- **Scores:** stroke = CHADS2 / CHA2DS2-VASc; bleeding = HAS-BLED (or HEMORR2HAGES, ATRIA).
- **AF complications:** HF, tachycardia-induced CMP, ischemia, sudden death, stroke / TIA, mesenteric ischemia, bleeding on anticoagulation.

#### 7. AF with CKD (67F, DM, HTN)

- **Clues:** recurrent sudden onset / offset palpitations; HR 120; BMI 31; on insulin, lisinopril, thiazide.
- **Workup:** ECG; CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN — for DOAC dosing); electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); liver profile; metabolic / endocrine (glucose, HbA1c, TSH); imaging (echo)
- **Results:** ECG: AF, HR 120; CBC normal; creatinine high with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²; random glucose high; CHA2DS2-VASc 4 (HTN, age, DM, female)
- **Management:** admit; rate or rhythm control (TEE before cardioversion); beta blocker or CCB (no CCB if EF < 40%); target HR < 110 at rest; anticoagulate (apixaban preferred over warfarin); control HTN and DM; weight loss.
- **CHA2DS2-VASc:** CHF 1, HTN 1, Age ≥ 75 = 2, DM 1, Stroke / TIA = 2, Vascular disease 1, Age 65–74 = 1, Sex female 1; this patient = 4 (HTN, age, DM, female).
- **Anticoagulate regardless of score:** HOCM, hyperthyroidism, rheumatic MS, mechanical valve.

#### 8. AF + rheumatic MS — warfarin counseling (50F and 62F)

- **Clues:** irregular rapid palpitations, LOC 3 s; tapping apex, mid-diastolic rumble at apex; crackles.
- **DDx:** AF, valvular disease, hyperthyroidism, hypoglycemia, TIA, anxiety.
- **Workup:** ECG; CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); coagulation (INR — baseline for warfarin); metabolic / endocrine (TSH, HbA1c, iron); imaging (CXR, echo, CT brain if neuro symptoms)
- **Results:** ECG: AF, HR 106; WBC 7000/µL (7 × 10⁹/L); Hb 13 g/dL (130 g/L); platelets 400,000/µL (400 × 10⁹/L); HbA1c 7% (53 mmol/mol); TFT and iron normal; echo: dilated LA, moderate–severe rheumatic MS; CT brain normal
- **Management:** ABCD; rate control; anti-failure treatment; cardiology; anticoagulation (warfarin, as MS = valvular AF).
- **Counseling steps:** introduce, private setting, ask what she knows; ideas, concerns, expectations; explain why (stroke risk cut ≈ 70%); how to take it with heparin bridging at start; INR target 2–3 with monitoring; side effects (bleeding, GI upset, rash, alopecia); alternatives (NOAC not for MS).
- **Precautions:** consistent vitamin K intake (green vegetables, green tea); drug and herb interactions (antibiotics, Bactrim); avoid NSAIDs / aspirin / multivitamins with K; acetaminophen is safe; tell dentist; electric razor; avoid pregnancy (teratogenic).
- **Safety net:** report bleeding (bruises, melena, epistaxis, severe headache) or overdose; carry warfarin card; contact numbers, leaflets; check understanding; thank her.

#### 9. Mitral stenosis + AF + pulmonary HTN (45F, DM, HTN)

- **Clues:** intermittent palpitations 2 years; progressive exertional dyspnea and orthopnea; loud S1, diastolic murmur at apex, loud P2, basal crackles.
- **DDx:** MS, AF, pulmonary HTN, HF, hyperthyroidism, anemia.
- **Workup:** ECG; CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); coagulation (INR); metabolic / endocrine (TSH, free T4, HbA1c); imaging (CXR, echo)
- **Results:** ECG: AF; Hb 12.5 g/dL (125 g/L); WBC 6 × 10⁹/L; platelets 254 × 10⁹/L; INR 1.1; creatinine 92 µmol/L (1.04 mg/dL); urea 6 mmol/L (BUN ≈ 17 mg/dL); Na 145 mmol/L; K 4.0 mmol/L; Mg 0.8 mmol/L (1.9 mg/dL); TSH 3.1 mIU/L; free T4 20 pmol/L (1.55 ng/dL); CXR: enlarged pulmonary artery; echo: rheumatic MS (moderate), RVSP 45 mmHg
- **Management:** rate control; anticoagulation; diuretic; rhythm control (cardioversion, ablation) if still symptomatic.
- **Examiner Qs:** CHA2DS2-VASc not used in valvular AF (anticoagulate anyway); AF types = paroxysmal, persistent, permanent.

#### 10. Sustained monomorphic VT after old MI (48M)

- **Clues:** episodes 15–30 s for 4 weeks, today 5 min, on exertion; thrombolysed MI 3 years ago; water-pipe smoker; cannon a waves, variable S1.
- **Workup:** ECG (regular wide-complex tachycardia); CBC (Hb); electrolytes (K, Mg, Ca); cardiac markers (troponin); later — ambulatory ECG, exercise ECG, imaging (echo, coronary angiography), electrophysiology study
- **Acute (stable VT):** DC cardioversion (class I); IV procainamide (IIa); IV amiodarone (IIb).
- **Long term:** amiodarone; coronary angiography ± revascularization; EP study and ablation; ICD; cardiology.
- **VT vs SVT with aberrancy:** AV dissociation, fusion / capture beats, QRS > 140 ms, extreme axis, precordial concordance favor VT.

#### 11. NSVT from cardiac sarcoidosis (39M)

- **Clues:** lung and skin sarcoid on prednisolone; palpitations with presyncope; irregular pulse.
- **DDx:** cardiac sarcoid NSVT, ischemic / dilated CMP, ARVC, idiopathic NSVT, low K / Mg, drug-induced.
- **Workup:** ECG + Holter; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); ACE level; cardiac markers (troponin, NT-proBNP); renal profile + electrolytes (Na, K, Mg, Ca); metabolic / endocrine (TSH); imaging (echo, cardiac MRI with late gadolinium, FDG-PET, CT chest); electrophysiology study
- **Results:** Holter: NSVT; echo: mildly reduced EF with basal septal thinning; MRI: patchy mid-wall / subepicardial LGE; PET: active myocardial FDG uptake
- **Management:** admit, monitor, correct electrolytes; optimize steroids ± methotrexate / azathioprine; beta blocker; HF therapy if EF < 50%; ICD; repeat CMR / PET; MDT; driving restrictions.
- **Poor prognosis:** syncope, VT / NSVT, low EF, AV block, extensive LGE, persistent PET uptake.
- **ICD in sarcoid:** secondary prevention (VT / VF, arrest); primary if NSVT with LV dysfunction or extensive LGE / PET uptake.

#### 12. HOCM (20M, syncope while playing football; two stations)

- **Clues:** exertional syncope; palpitations; sustained / trifid apex, S4; ejection murmur at LLSB louder with Valsalva / standing; apical MR murmur.
- **DDx:** HOCM, AS, arrhythmia (AF, VT), myocarditis, seizure, anemia, hyperthyroidism.
- **Workup:** ECG + Holter; CBC; electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); cardiac markers (troponin, NT-proBNP); metabolic / endocrine (TSH, HbA1c); imaging (echo, cardiac MRI, CT brain if syncope with head injury); exercise test; genetic testing
- **Results:** HR 106 irregular (AF); WBC 7000/µL (7 × 10⁹/L); Hb 13 g/dL (130 g/L); platelets 400,000/µL (400 × 10⁹/L); HbA1c 5.3% (34 mmol/mol); TFT normal; ECG: LVH, inferior pseudo-Q waves, deep / giant T inversion; Holter: paroxysmal AF; echo: asymmetric septal hypertrophy, LVOT gradient 40 mmHg, SAM with moderate MR, dilated LA, EF 55%
- **Management:** cardiology; avoid dehydration and competitive sport; beta blocker; alcohol septal ablation or surgical myectomy; ICD if indicated; anticoagulate AF regardless of CHA2DS2-VASc; transplant if end-stage.
- **ICD indications:** survived SCD / VF / sustained VT; family history of SCD; unexplained syncope; septum ≥ 30 mm; apical aneurysm; EF ≤ 50%; extensive LGE on CMR.

### Shortness of breath and valve disease

#### 13. Peripartum cardiomyopathy (32F, 2 weeks after delivery)

- **Clues:** SOB NYHA 2–3, leg swelling; HR 102; raised JVP, S3, basal crackles, edema.
- **DDx:** dilated, ischemic, hypertrophic, hypertensive, valvular, viral, metabolic (thyroid), systemic (SLE, toxins) CMP.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, Mg); liver profile; cardiac markers (troponin, CK-MB, BNP); metabolic / endocrine (TSH, free T4); ECG; imaging (CXR, echo; cardiac MRI / CT coronary / right heart cath / biopsy if unclear)
- **Results:** Hb 9 g/dL (90 g/L); electrolytes, renal, LFT, TSH normal; troponin 0.02 ng/mL (negative); BNP 900 pg/mL; CXR: cardiomegaly with congestion; echo: EF 30%, global hypokinesia, dilated LV, normal RV
- **Management:** low salt, fluid restriction, exercise training; ACEi / ARB (after delivery), ARNI, beta blocker, spironolactone, diuretics, SGLT2i, digoxin, hydralazine + nitrate; ICD / CRT-D; cardiology.
- **Prognosis:** about 50% recover fully; persistent LV dysfunction or EF < 25% at presentation = high risk in the next pregnancy.

#### 14. Acute decompensated HF from NSAIDs (55M, IHD)

- **Clues:** 3 days SOB, orthopnea, PND, 2–3 kg weight gain; ibuprofen TID for knee OA; raised JVP, S3, crackles, hepatomegaly, edema.
- **DDx:** ADHF, ACS, new AF, PE, decompensated liver disease, nephrotic / renal failure, ARDS, venous insufficiency.
- **Workup:** ECG; urine dipstick; cardiac markers (troponin, NT-proBNP); CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile; metabolic / endocrine (TSH); imaging (CXR, echo)
- **Results:** BP 150/90, HR 95, RR 22; ECG: sinus tachycardia; NT-proBNP high; renal: mild AKI, K 4.2 mmol/L; CXR: cardiomegaly, upper-lobe diversion, Kerley B lines, bilateral effusions; echo: EF 35%, global hypokinesia
- **Management:** stop NSAIDs; O2 to 94–98%; sit upright; strict fluid balance and daily weights; IV furosemide; GDMT (ACEi / ARNI, beta blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) once euvolemic; HF nurse; cardiology.
- **Examiner Qs:** drugs precipitating ADHF = NSAIDs, TZDs, steroids, diuretic non-adherence; NSAIDs cause Na / water retention and reduce renal perfusion; ICU if cardiogenic shock, severe hypoxia, inotropes, acute renal failure; monitor urine output, weight, BP, electrolytes, renal function.

#### 15. Severe mitral stenosis in pregnancy (35F, 5 months)

- **Clues:** 4 weeks SOB NYHA 2–3, orthopnea, palpitations; tapping apex, loud S1, opening snap, mid-diastolic rumble; basal crackles.
- **DDx:** MS, tachycardia CMP, PE, hyperthyroidism, anemia, pulmonary HTN, HF, asthma, pneumonia.
- **Workup:** ECG; CBC; renal profile + electrolytes; cardiac markers (troponin, NT-proBNP); metabolic / endocrine (TSH); imaging (echo — avoid CXR / CT in pregnancy unless essential)
- **Results:** paroxysmal AF; NT-proBNP 600 pg/mL; troponin negative; echo: rheumatic mitral valve, mean gradient 11 mmHg, valve area 0.9 cm² (severe MS)
- **Management:** beta blocker and diuretics; anticoagulation; balloon mitral commissurotomy if medical therapy fails.
- **PBMC contraindications:** LA thrombus, moderate–severe MR, Wilkins score > 8.
- **Anticoagulate in MS if:** AF, prior embolism, LA thrombus, marked LA enlargement.
- **Rheumatic prophylaxis:** 10 years or until age 40, whichever is longer.

#### 16. Severe aortic stenosis (70M)

- **Clues:** 3 months exertional SOB, angina-like pain, exertional syncope; slow-rising low-volume pulse; sustained apex; soft S2; ejection murmur right 2nd ICS.
- **DDx:** AS, ACS, HOCM, aortic sclerosis, myocarditis, pericarditis, PE.
- **Workup:** ECG; CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes; cardiac markers (NT-proBNP); metabolic (HbA1c, lipids); imaging (CXR, echo; CT / coronary angiography before valve replacement)
- **Results:** BP 130/75, HR 80; ECG: LVH; echo: calcified aortic valve, area 0.8 cm², mean gradient 45 mmHg, peak velocity 5 m/s, EF 45%
- **Causes of AS:** senile calcific, bicuspid valve, congenital (subaortic membrane), rheumatic, endocarditis, drugs.
- **Management:** treat risk factors; low-dose diuretic; avoid strenuous exercise; aortic valve replacement (surgical vs TAVI).
- **Intervene if:** severe symptomatic AS; severe asymptomatic with EF < 50%; severe AS having other cardiac surgery.
- **Examiner Qs:** mechanical AVR INR target 2–3; TAVI complications = death, stroke, vascular injury, valve embolization, paravalvular leak, AV block, tamponade, arrhythmia.

### Pre-operative assessment

#### 17. Cholecystectomy after recent DES (64M, DM, HTN, MI 5 months ago)

- **Clues:** STEMI 5 months ago with one DES in LAD; asymptomatic, climbs 2 flights, jogs daily; on ASA, clopidogrel, bisoprolol, lisinopril, atorvastatin, insulin.
- **Risk tool:** Revised Cardiac Risk Index (Lee).
- **RCRI items:** high-risk surgery, IHD, HF, cerebrovascular disease, DM on insulin, creatinine > 2 mg/dL.
- **Workup:** ECG; imaging (echo if none within a year, CXR); CBC; coagulation (PT / INR, aPTT); renal profile (creatinine — RCRI cut-off > 2 mg/dL = 177 µmol/L)
- **Results:** HR 65, BP 110/70; CBC, coagulation, CXR normal; ECG: sinus rhythm with Q waves V1–V2; echo: anteroseptal hypokinesia, EF 55%, normal pulmonary pressure
- **Anticipated complications:** stent thrombosis, ACS, death, stroke, pulmonary edema, arrhythmias.
- **Recommendation:** elective surgery, so postpone until 12 months after DES.
- **Then:** monitored bed after surgery; cardiology; stop clopidogrel ≥ 5 days before; continue aspirin, beta blocker, ACEi, statin; avoid volume overload; daily ECG and troponin ± BNP after surgery.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **AF scores:** CHA2DS2-VASc for stroke; HAS-BLED for bleeding; not used in valvular AF.
- **AF rate control target:** HR < 110 at rest; beta blocker or diltiazem; avoid CCB if EF < 40%.
- **Anticoagulation choice:** NOAC (apixaban) preferred for non-valvular AF; warfarin for MS or mechanical valve.
- **Warfarin INR targets:** 2–3 for AF and mechanical AVR.
- **MI drugs at discharge:** DAPT, beta blocker, ACEi / ARB / ARNI, statin; rehab; risk factor control.
- **Tamponade:** Beck triad and pulsus paradoxus.
- **ICD (HOCM, sarcoid, post-MI VT):** secondary prevention after VT / VF; primary prevention by disease-specific risk markers.
