# Endocrinology

## How to use this

The 19 endocrinology stations fall into five groups: diabetes and its emergencies, thyroid, adrenal, pituitary, and endocrine hypertension. Most stations score a structured history, a system-specific exam (the thyroid exam is examined on its own twice), then interpretation of a hormone panel.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; in ER cases secure ABC and ask for glucose and vitals first.
- **Stations covered:** diabetes (new T2DM with nephropathy, DKA ×2 incl. DKA + STEMI, HHS); thyroid (Graves ×3, thyroid exam ×2, PTC ×2, MTC breaking bad news, post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia); adrenal (Addison / APS, Cushing disease, Conn's, pheochromocytoma); pituitary (prolactinoma, Sheehan syndrome).

## 1. Core endocrine history (every case)

- **Presenting complaint:** onset, course, duration, severity, impact on daily life, previous similar episodes.
- **Weight and appetite:** amount and duration of weight change, intentional or not, appetite up or down.
- **Thyroid screen:** heat or cold intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitations, bowel habit, hair / skin change, neck swelling, eye symptoms.
- **Adrenal / pituitary screen:** postural dizziness, salt craving, pigmentation, bruising, striae, headache, visual field loss, galactorrhea.
- **Glucose:** polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, blurred vision, hypoglycemia episodes.
- **Reproductive:** menstrual cycle, amenorrhea, fertility, pregnancy / lactation, hirsutism, acne, libido.
- **Bone and muscle:** proximal weakness, bone pain, fractures, cramps, tetany.
- **Past history:** autoimmune disease (T1DM, thyroid, vitiligo, pernicious anemia), neck / pituitary surgery or radiation, TB, malignancy, CKD, liver disease, PCOS, gestational DM.
- **Drugs:** steroids (oral, inhaled, topical, injection) and recent withdrawal, amiodarone, lithium, iodine contrast, thyroxine, antipsychotics, antidepressants, OCP, SGLT2i, checkpoint inhibitors, licorice.
- **Family:** DM, thyroid disease, autoimmune disease, MEN1 / MEN2, VHL, NF1, thyroid cancer, early HTN.
- **Social:** smoking (worsens Graves eye disease), alcohol, diet, exercise, occupation, marital status.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Diabetes (new or uncontrolled)

- **Symptoms:** polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight change, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds.
- **Risk factors:** obesity, HTN, dyslipidemia, CVD, gestational DM, PCOS, family history, inactivity.
- **Complications:** neuropathy (numbness, paresthesia), nephropathy (frothy urine), retinopathy (vision), foot ulcers, CAD / stroke / PVD.
- **Drugs:** steroids, antipsychotics.

### B. DKA / HHS (vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion)

- **Symptoms:** onset < 24 h (DKA) vs days (HHS), nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, thirst, polyuria, drowsiness, seizures, focal signs.
- **Precipitants:** missed insulin, new-onset DM, infection (UTI, chest, skin), MI (chest pain), stroke / TIA, pancreatitis, surgery, SGLT2i, steroids, antipsychotics, cocaine.
- **DM control:** type, duration, insulin doses, compliance, home readings, last HbA1c, previous DKA / HHS admissions.
- **Psychosocial:** family support, eating disorder, socioeconomic barriers.

### C. Hyperthyroidism / neck swelling

- **Thyrotoxic symptoms:** weight loss with increased appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, palpitations, tremor, anxiety, insomnia, diarrhea, proximal weakness, oligomenorrhea.
- **Graves clues:** eye protrusion, redness, pain on movement, diplopia; family history; smoking.
- **Thyroiditis clues:** neck pain, recent viral illness, recent delivery (postpartum), amiodarone, iodine contrast, radiation.
- **Mass:** size, growth, pain, moves with swallowing, other lumps, lymph nodes.
- **Compressive symptoms:** dysphagia, hoarseness, stridor, SOB.
- **Cancer red flags:** neck radiation, family history of thyroid cancer / MEN, rapid growth, hoarseness, bone pain, hemoptysis, young HTN + hypercalcemia (MEN2).

### D. Hypocalcemia (tingling, carpopedal spasm)

- **Neuromuscular:** circumoral and acral paresthesia, cramps, carpopedal spasm, laryngospasm, bronchospasm.
- **CNS / cardiac:** seizures, confusion, syncope, arrhythmia.
- **Causes:** recent thyroid / parathyroid / neck surgery; pancreatitis; malabsorption or bowel surgery; renal or liver failure; autoimmune disease; family history.
- **Drugs:** diuretics, bisphosphonates, denosumab, antiepileptics, PPIs.

### E. Adrenal insufficiency (fatigue, postural dizziness, salt craving)

- **Symptoms:** fatigue, weakness, anorexia, weight loss, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, myalgia, arthralgia, salt craving, pigmentation, vitiligo, amenorrhea, low mood.
- **Causes:** autoimmune (T1DM, Hashimoto — APS); TB; HIV; metastases; steroid withdrawal; ketoconazole, mitotane, metyrapone; pituitary surgery / radiation.

### F. Cushing syndrome

- **Exogenous steroids first:** oral, inhaled, topical, injected.
- **Features:** central weight gain (face, neck, abdomen), easy bruising, wide purple striae, proximal weakness, fractures, acne, hirsutism, menstrual change, depression / psychosis, thrombosis.
- **Metabolic:** new DM, HTN, OSA.
- **Pseudo-Cushing:** pregnancy, severe obesity, alcoholism, depression, uncontrolled DM, severe OSA.

### G. Pituitary (high prolactin, amenorrhea, hypopituitarism)

- **Prolactin:** galactorrhea, amenorrhea / oligomenorrhea, infertility, low libido, breast pain; in men impotence, gynecomastia.
- **Mass effect:** headache, visual field loss, diplopia.
- **Other causes of high prolactin:** pregnancy, hypothyroidism, CKD, cirrhosis, PCOS, seizures, chest wall trauma, antipsychotics, antidepressants, verapamil, metoclopramide.
- **Hypopituitarism:** postpartum hemorrhage and failure to lactate (Sheehan), fatigue, cold intolerance, postural dizziness, loss of libido, loss of body hair.
- **Past history:** cranial surgery, radiation, head trauma, MEN1.

### H. Endocrine hypertension (young, resistant, or with low K)

- **Pheochromocytoma:** paroxysmal headache, palpitations, sweating, pallor, anxiety, tremor, flushing.
- **Primary aldosteronism:** muscle weakness, cramps, polyuria, polydipsia, low K on diuretics.
- **Cushing / thyroid / acromegaly:** weight gain, bruising; heat intolerance; coarse features, ring / shoe size.
- **Other:** OSA (snoring), renal disease, coarctation, licorice, NSAIDs, steroids.
- **Family:** MEN2, VHL, NF1, early-onset HTN.

## 3. Core endocrine exam (every case)

- **General:** BMI, body habitus (central obesity, cachexia), hydration, pigmentation, acanthosis nigricans, hirsutism, mood.
- **Vitals:** BP (both arms; lying and standing), HR and rhythm (AF), RR, temperature, glucose.
- **Hands:** tremor, sweating vs dry, palmar erythema, onycholysis, thyroid acropachy, palmar crease pigmentation, large hands.
- **Face and eyes:** moon face, exophthalmos, lid retraction, lid lag, chemosis, eye movements, outer eyebrow loss, coarse features, visual fields, fundoscopy.
- **Neck:** goiter, nodules, scars, lymph nodes, movement with swallowing and tongue protrusion.
- **Skin:** striae (wide purple), bruising, thin skin, pigmentation (buccal, creases), vitiligo, pretibial myxedema, candidiasis.
- **Musculoskeletal and neuro:** proximal myopathy (stand from squat, arms crossed), reflexes (brisk vs slow-relaxing), peripheral neuropathy, Chvostek / Trousseau.
- **Feet (DM):** ulcers, pulses, monofilament.

## 4. Thyroid examination (two stations)

- **Preparation:** wash hands; introduce; confirm name and DOB; explain; consent; expose neck to upper sternum; ask about pain.
- **Hands:** pulse rate and rhythm; tremor; sweating or dryness; acropachy; palmar erythema; onycholysis.
- **Face:** dry skin, sweating, loss of outer third of eyebrows (Hertoghe sign).
- **Eyes:** lid retraction, lid lag, chemosis, exophthalmos (look from above and side), eye movements, RAPD.
- **Neck inspection:** midline swelling, scars; repeat while swallowing water; repeat with tongue protrusion (thyroglossal cyst moves).
- **Palpation from behind:** size, symmetry, consistency, nodules, tenderness; swallow while palpating; tongue protrusion; cervical lymph nodes.
- **Percussion and auscultation:** retrosternal dullness from sternal notch; bruit over each lobe; Pemberton sign.
- **Status:** biceps reflex; pretibial myxedema; proximal myopathy (stand with arms crossed).
- **Close:** thank patient; dispose PPE and wash hands; summarize; suggest TFT, ECG, thyroid US and screening for associated autoimmune disease (DM, Addison, pernicious anemia, RA, SLE).
- **Signs specific to Graves:** exophthalmos, chemosis, ophthalmoplegia, thyroid acropachy, pretibial myxedema.
- **Painful thyroid:** de Quervain (subacute, post-viral), infectious (suppurative) thyroiditis, radiation thyroiditis, hemorrhage into a nodule.

## 5. Exam add-ons by diagnosis

- **New T2DM:** BMI, acanthosis nigricans, BP, fundoscopy (retinopathy), neuropathy, foot ulcers, urine dipstick.
- **DKA / HHS:** dehydration (dry mucosa, turgor, postural drop), Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, GCS, focal neurology, injection sites, source of infection, DVT signs.
- **Graves:** anxious, stare, fine tremor, warm moist skin, sinus tachycardia / AF, diffuse goiter ± bruit, eye signs, brisk reflexes, pretibial myxedema.
- **Hypocalcemia:** Chvostek sign (less specific), Trousseau sign (more specific), stridor, neck scar; chronic = cataract, dry skin, brittle nails, papilledema.
- **Addison:** postural hypotension, buccal / palmar crease / scar pigmentation, vitiligo, oral candidiasis (APS type 1), dehydration.
- **Cushing:** moon face, buffalo hump, supraclavicular fat pads, wide purple striae > 1 cm, thin skin, bruising, proximal myopathy, HTN, hirsutism, kyphosis / vertebral tenderness, edema.
- **Prolactinoma:** visual fields, galactorrhea (breast exam), hypogonadism, thyroid, chest wall scar, cirrhosis signs.
- **Hypopituitarism:** postural hypotension, pale hairless skin, puffy face, outer eyebrow loss, slow-relaxing reflexes, visual fields.
- **Endocrine HTN:** BP both arms, radio-femoral delay, fundoscopy, renal bruit, Cushingoid or acromegalic features, thyroid signs, polycystic kidneys, proximal weakness.

## 6. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### Diabetes and its emergencies

#### 1. New type 2 DM with nephropathy (42F, polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia)

- **Clues:** BMI 31, acanthosis nigricans, BP 156/98; dipstick 2+ glucose, 2+ protein, no ketones.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile (LFT, albumin); metabolic (fasting glucose, random glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile); urine (dipstick, urine ACR or 24-h protein).
- **Results:** WBC 6 ×10⁹/L (6,000/µL); Hb 14 g/dL (140 g/L); Na 139 mmol/L (139 mEq/L); K 4 mmol/L (4 mEq/L); HbA1c 9.8% (84 mmol/mol); BUN 14 mg/dL (urea 5 mmol/L); creatinine 1.2 mg/dL (106 µmol/L); urine glucose 2+, protein 2+, no ketones; albuminuria 330 mg/day (macroalbuminuria, > 300).
- **Diagnostic criteria:** HbA1c ≥ 6.5%; FPG ≥ 126 mg/dL; 2-h OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dL; random ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms.
- **Management:** endocrinology; diet, exercise, weight loss, education; metformin then add-ons (SU, others) or insulin; nephrology and ACEi / ARB for nephropathy.
- **Complications:** macrovascular (CAD, stroke, PVD); microvascular (neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy); foot ulcers.
- **Retinopathy:** non-proliferative = microaneurysms, dot-blot hemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots; proliferative = neovascularization, vitreous hemorrhage, retinal detachment; maculopathy = macular edema; refer ophthalmology for laser / anti-VEGF.

#### 2. DKA (18F, T1DM, abdominal pain and vomiting)

- **Clues:** missed insulin because of nausea; thirst, polyuria; dry mucosa, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath.
- **DDx:** DKA, gastroenteritis, acute pancreatitis, other acute abdomen.
- **Workup:** bedside (capillary glucose, capillary ketones, urine dipstick); blood gas (VBG / ABG — pH, PCO2, HCO3, anion gap); serum ketones (β-hydroxybutyrate); electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, Mg, PO4); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); CBC (with differential); pancreatic (amylase, lipase); cardiac (ECG, troponin); HbA1c; septic screen if febrile (cultures, CXR).
- **Results:** glucose 450 mg/dL (25 mmol/L); pH 7.10; PCO2 25 mmHg (3.3 kPa); HCO3 8 mmol/L (8 mEq/L); Na 132 mmol/L; K 5.5 mmol/L with peaked T; Cl 95 mmol/L; anion gap ≈ 29 (Na − Cl − HCO3); β-hydroxybutyrate 5 mmol/L; urine glucose 4+, ketones 4+; WBC 12 ×10⁹/L (12,000/µL); Hb 14 g/dL (140 g/L); platelets 250 ×10⁹/L; BUN 30 mg/dL (urea 10.7 mmol/L); creatinine 1.5 mg/dL (133 µmol/L); amylase 80 U/L (normal).
- **Management:** ABC, IV access, ICU; IV fluids by hydration; replace K (give insulin only once K ≥ 3.3); fixed-rate insulin infusion 0.1 U/kg/h; hourly glucose, electrolytes / VBG every 2–4 h; cardiac monitor; DVT and GI prophylaxis; treat trigger; switch to SC insulin with overlap; education.
- **Stop insulin infusion when:** glucose < 250 mg/dL plus 2 of pH > 7.3, HCO3 > 18, normal anion gap; and eating.
- **Precipitants:** missed insulin, new-onset DM (20–25%), infection (30–40%), MI, stroke, pancreatitis, SGLT2i, clozapine / olanzapine, cocaine, steroids.
- **Complication of treatment:** cerebral edema, hypokalemia, hypoglycemia.

#### 3. DKA precipitated by anterior STEMI (49M, T1DM)

- **Clues:** missed insulin; 2 h chest pressure; Kussmaul, HR 120, BP 95/60.
- **DDx:** DKA from MI; DKA from infection / non-adherence; HHS; lactic acidosis, toxins.
- **Workup:** bedside (capillary glucose, ketones); blood gas (VBG — pH, HCO3, anion gap); electrolytes (Na, K, Cl); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); cardiac (12-lead ECG, serial troponin); CBC; imaging (CXR, echo).
- **Management:** DKA protocol (0.9% saline, insulin 0.1 U/kg/h, K replacement) together with STEMI protocol (DAPT, morphine, urgent PCI); CCU / ICU; statin, beta blocker, ACEi once stable; sick-day rules; MDT.
- **Results:** glucose 480 mg/dL (26.6 mmol/L); serum ketones 5 mmol/L; pH 7.15; HCO3 10 mmol/L (10 mEq/L); anion gap 24; ECG anterior ST elevation; troponin raised; CXR mild congestion.
- **Examiner Qs:** MI raises catecholamines and cortisol → insulin resistance and ketosis; treat both at the same time (better survival than sequential); STEMI ECG = ST elevation, reciprocal depression, new LBBB.

#### 4. HHS (70M, T2DM, bedridden, fever, reduced LOC)

- **Clues:** poor compliance; drowsy and confused 1 day; low intake; fever, dysuria; on insulin, metformin, indapamide.
- **Workup:** bedside (capillary glucose, ketones); blood gas (VBG — pH, HCO3, anion gap); electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, Mg, PO4, corrected Na); serum osmolality (measured + calculated); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); CBC; lactate; septic screen (blood / urine cultures, urinalysis, CXR); ECG; CT brain if focal signs.
- **Results:** glucose 117 mmol/L (≈ 2,100 mg/dL); ketones 0.7 mmol/L (< 3); Na 122 mmol/L (corrected for glucose ≈ 170); K 5.3 mmol/L; HCO3 22 mmol/L; pH 7.3; PCO2 18 mmHg (2.4 kPa); creatinine 226 µmol/L (2.6 mg/dL); urea 16 mmol/L (BUN ≈ 45 mg/dL); osmolality 361 mOsm/kg (> 320); lactate 3.4 mmol/L (31 mg/dL); urine glucose ++++, no ketones.
- **Osmolality formula:** 2 × Na + glucose / 18 + BUN / 2.8 (mg/dL units).
- **Management:** ABC, two large-bore lines, ICU; 0.9% saline (+3–6 L in 12 h) watching osmolality; correct K before insulin; insulin 0.1 U/kg/h once fluids under way; LMWH prophylaxis (heparin 5000 U BID if renal impairment); antibiotics for UTI; monitor neuro status, glucose, electrolytes, osmolality; switch to SC insulin when eating; stop metformin.
- **Examiner Qs:** LMWH because dehydration + immobility = high VTE risk; treatment complications = cerebral edema (too-fast correction), arrhythmia, hypoglycemia, seizures; other causes of low GCS in DM = stroke, sepsis, hypoglycemia, uremia, drugs.

### Thyroid

#### 5. Graves disease with ophthalmopathy (36F, 30F, 35F — three stations)

- **Clues:** weight loss with increased appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, palpitations, diarrhea, anxiety, oligomenorrhea; smoker; family history; fine tremor, warm skin, diffuse goiter, lid retraction, lid lag, proptosis, diplopia, hyperreflexia.
- **DDx:** Graves, toxic MNG, toxic adenoma, thyroiditis (painless, subacute, postpartum, drug / iodine / radiation), exogenous thyroxine, TSH-secreting adenoma.
- **Workup:** thyroid function (TSH, FT4, FT3); thyroid antibodies (TSH-receptor antibody / TSI, anti-TPO, anti-thyroglobulin); CBC (baseline before antithyroid drugs); liver profile (baseline); renal profile; ECG (AF); imaging (thyroid US with Doppler, radioiodine / technetium uptake scan if TRAb negative).
- **Results:** station 1: TSH 0.002 mIU/L (0.5–5); FT4 3 ng/dL (≈ 39 pmol/L; normal 0.7–1.8 ng/dL); FT3 6 pg/mL (≈ 9.2 pmol/L; normal 2.3–4.1 pg/mL); CBC, renal, LFT normal. Station 2: TSH < 0.01 mIU/L; FT4 41 pmol/L (≈ 3.2 ng/dL; normal 7.5–14.5 pmol/L); FT3 13.5 pmol/L (≈ 8.8 pg/mL; normal 3.8–6); TRAb > 40 IU/L (< 1.5); scan: diffuse homogeneous increased uptake.
- **Management:** endocrinology; beta blocker for symptoms; methimazole / carbimazole; radioactive iodine; total thyroidectomy.
- **Ophthalmopathy:** stop smoking; lubricant drops, raise head of bed; IV steroids or teprotumumab for active moderate–severe disease; ophthalmology; RAI can worsen eye disease.
- **Side effects:** antithyroid drugs = rash, arthralgia, agranulocytosis, hepatitis, ANCA vasculitis, teratogenicity; RAI = worse eye disease, radiation thyroiditis, hypothyroidism; surgery = hypothyroidism, hypoparathyroidism, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, anesthesia risk.
- **Pregnancy:** PTU in 1st trimester then methimazole; RAI contraindicated; surgery in 2nd trimester; if planning pregnancy, consider RAI or surgery 6–12 months before.
- **RAI precautions:** avoid close contact with young children and pregnant women for a period after treatment.

#### 6. Papillary thyroid cancer from a thyroid nodule (47M; 22M — two stations)

- **Clues:** painless nodule moving with swallowing, euthyroid; ask radiation exposure and family history (mother had thyroidectomy).
- **DDx:** benign (follicular adenoma, colloid nodule, cyst); malignant (papillary, follicular, medullary, anaplastic, lymphoma, metastasis); parathyroid lesion; thyroglossal or branchial cyst.
- **Workup:** thyroid function (TSH, FT4 — uptake scan only if TSH low); imaging (neck US with TI-RADS, cervical nodes); FNA (Bethesda); bone panel (Ca, PTH) if MEN suspected; calcitonin if family history of MTC.
- **Results:** TSH 0.9 mIU/L (0.5–5); FT4 1.2 ng/dL (≈ 15.4 pmol/L; normal 0.8–1.8); US solid hypoechoic nodule with microcalcifications (TI-RADS 4–5) → FNA papillary carcinoma.
- **Worrisome US features:** solid, hypoechoic, irregular / infiltrative margin, taller than wide, microcalcifications, extrathyroidal extension, central vascularity, abnormal lymph nodes.
- **Next step:** FNA.
- **Management:** active surveillance acceptable for low-risk microcarcinoma < 1 cm; otherwise total thyroidectomy or lobectomy ± neck dissection; radioactive iodine; levothyroxine (TSH suppression); follow-up TFT, thyroglobulin, anti-TG, neck US.

#### 7. Medullary thyroid cancer — breaking bad news (28F, MEN2A)

- **Clues:** neck lump, weight loss, hypercalcemia, hypertension (pheo + hyperparathyroidism).
- **SPIKES:** private setting; ask who she wants present; explore what she knows; warning shot; give result simply; silence and empathy; plan with MDT (surgery, oncology); support; follow-up; check understanding; questions; thank her.
- **Workup:** tumor markers (calcitonin — > 100 pg/mL ≈ diagnostic; CEA); MEN screen (bone panel — Ca, PTH; plasma metanephrines); genetics (RET proto-oncogene); imaging (neck and mediastinal US; CT chest / abdomen or PET if calcitonin > 400 pg/mL).
- **Diagnosis:** MTC with likely MEN2A (MTC + pheochromocytoma + hyperparathyroidism).
- **Key point:** exclude pheochromocytoma before thyroid surgery.

#### 8. Post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia (28F, 2 weeks after total thyroidectomy)

- **Clues:** muscle twitching, carpopedal spasm, dehydrated.
- **Workup:** bone panel (albumin-corrected Ca or ionized Ca, PO4, Mg, PTH, ALP, 25-OH vitamin D); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR); liver profile; ECG (long QT); malabsorption tests / amylase only if suspected.
- **Results:** corrected Ca 1.43 then 1.41 mmol/L (≈ 5.7 mg/dL; normal 2.1–2.6 mmol/L = 8.5–10.5 mg/dL); PO4 1.77 mmol/L (≈ 5.5 mg/dL; normal 0.81–1.45); albumin 43 g/L (4.3 g/dL); ALP 57 U/L; Mg 0.56 mmol/L (≈ 1.4 mg/dL; low); intact PTH 11 ng/L = pg/mL (≈ 1.2 pmol/L; normal 15–65); vitamin D low.
- **Diagnosis:** post-surgical hypoparathyroidism + hypomagnesemia + vitamin D deficiency.
- **DDx:** hypoparathyroidism (autoimmune, DiGeorge, infiltrative, radiation); PTH resistance (pseudohypoparathyroidism, low Mg); low vitamin D (diet, malabsorption, CKD, cirrhosis); IV bisphosphonate; pancreatitis.
- **Management:** symptomatic = 10–20 mL 10% calcium gluconate in 50–100 mL over 10 min with ECG monitoring, repeat until symptom-free; IV magnesium; then infusion 100 mL 10% calcium gluconate in 1 L at 50–100 mL/h; oral calcium + calcitriol / alfacalcidol early.

### Adrenal

#### 9. Primary adrenal insufficiency / APS (27F, T1DM + Hashimoto)

- **Clues:** 3 months fatigue, postural dizziness, cramps, salt craving, darker skin, irregular cycles; BP 90/60 with orthostatic drop.
- **Workup:** CBC; electrolytes (Na, K, Ca); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); liver profile; metabolic / endocrine (glucose, HbA1c, TSH, FT4, 8 AM cortisol + ACTH, renin / aldosterone, 21-hydroxylase antibodies, pregnancy test); urinalysis; short Synacthen test if cortisol equivocal.
- **Results:** WBC 6 ×10⁹/L; Hb 13 g/dL (130 g/L); platelets 240 ×10⁹/L; Na 127 mmol/L (127 mEq/L); K 5.2 mmol/L; Ca 9.2 mg/dL (2.3 mmol/L); HbA1c 6.5% (48 mmol/mol); fasting glucose 95 mg/dL (5.3 mmol/L); TSH 0.8 mIU/L; creatinine 0.7 mg/dL (62 µmol/L); 8 AM cortisol 3.2 µg/dL (≈ 88 nmol/L; normal 5–25 µg/dL); ACTH 97 pg/mL (≈ 21 pmol/L; normal 10–60) → primary adrenal insufficiency.
- **Confirm:** ACTH (cosyntropin) stimulation test; peak cortisol < 18 mcg/dL = adrenal insufficiency (here 8.1).
- **Cause:** high ACTH + pigmentation + other autoimmune disease = Addison / autoimmune polyglandular syndrome; confirm with 21-hydroxylase antibodies.
- **Management:** hydrocortisone replacement (± fludrocortisone); IV fluids; sick-day rules (double dose when ill, IM injection kit); medical alert bracelet; endocrine follow-up.

#### 10. Cushing disease (28F, "PCOS", amenorrhea, vertebral fracture)

- **Clues:** central weight gain, hirsutism, acne, easy bruising, polyuria, low mood; no exogenous steroids; moon face, buffalo hump, supraclavicular fat, wide red striae, proximal weakness; BP 145/85, BMI 36.
- **First-line tests (2 of 3):** 24-h urinary free cortisol; late-night salivary cortisol; 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test.
- **Results:** 24-h UFC 265 and 246 µg/24 h (≈ 731 and 679 nmol/24 h; high); 1 mg DST morning cortisol 4 µg/dL (≈ 110 nmol/L; > 1.8 µg/dL = 50 nmol/L means not suppressed).
- **Next:** ACTH on 2 days → 67 pg/mL (≈ 14.7 pmol/L) = ACTH-dependent (pituitary vs ectopic).
- **DDx:** pseudo-Cushing, metabolic syndrome, PCOS, factitious.
- **Localize:** pituitary MRI; high-dose DST / CRH / desmopressin test; MRI normal → inferior petrosal sinus sampling confirms Cushing disease.
- **Management:** MDT; transsphenoidal surgery; then repeat surgery, radiotherapy, medical therapy, or bilateral adrenalectomy.
- **Drugs:** pituitary-directed (cabergoline, pasireotide); steroidogenesis inhibitors (ketoconazole, metyrapone, osilodrostat, etomidate, mitotane); receptor blocker (mifepristone).

#### 11. Primary aldosteronism / Conn's (45M, HTN on HCTZ, weakness)

- **Clues:** proximal weakness, polyuria, polydipsia; BP 160/95; power 4/5.
- **DDx:** essential HTN on diuretic, primary aldosteronism, Cushing, pheochromocytoma, hypothyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, renal artery stenosis, licorice, renin-secreting tumor.
- **Workup:** electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, HCO3, Mg); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); urine (ACR); endocrine screen (aldosterone / renin ratio, TFT, 1 mg DST, plasma metanephrines); cardiac (ECG, echo); imaging (renal artery Doppler; adrenal CT / MRI after biochemical confirmation; adrenal vein sampling before surgery).
- **Results:** Na 142 mmol/L; K 2.8 mmol/L (2.8 mEq/L; low); Cl 102 mmol/L; HCO3 30 mmol/L (high — metabolic alkalosis); BUN 18 mg/dL (urea 6.4 mmol/L); creatinine 1.0 mg/dL (88 µmol/L); aldosterone 25 ng/dL (≈ 690 pmol/L; normal 2–9); plasma renin activity 0.2 ng/mL/h (suppressed); ARR 125 (> 20–30 positive); ECG flat T waves and U waves; CT left adrenal adenoma.
- **Management:** replace K; stop HCTZ; spironolactone; endocrinology; adrenal vein sampling then adrenalectomy for unilateral adenoma; monitor K, renal function, BP.

#### 12. Pheochromocytoma (25M, headache and high BP 9 months)

- **History:** headache, palpitations, sweating, flushing, tremor, anxiety, hypertensive crises; screen Cushing, thyroid, acromegaly, renal disease; family history of MEN2, VHL, NF1.
- **Exam:** BP both arms and postural; radio-femoral delay; fundi; renal bruit; endocrine features; polycystic kidneys.
- **DDx:** renovascular disease, pheochromocytoma, primary aldosteronism, Cushing, coarctation.
- **Workup:** catecholamine screen (plasma free metanephrines, 24-h urine fractionated metanephrines, chromogranin A); other secondary-HTN screen (aldosterone / renin, 1 mg DST or 24-h UFC, TFT); electrolytes (Na, K); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR) + urinalysis; imaging (renal Doppler; CT / MRI adrenals after biochemistry; MIBG / PET if extra-adrenal or metastatic); genetics if young / bilateral.
- **Results:** plasma free metanephrine 1,200 pg/mL (≈ 6.1 nmol/L; normal < 65 pg/mL); urine metanephrines 1,000 (≈ 3× upper limit); FT4 1.2 ng/dL (15.4 pmol/L); aldosterone 12 ng/dL (333 pmol/L); renin activity 1.1 ng/mL/h (normal); CT 2 cm right adrenal mass.
- **Management:** endocrinology; alpha-blockade first (phenoxybenzamine / doxazosin) for 10–14 days; add beta blocker only after alpha-blockade; high-salt diet and fluids; laparoscopic adrenalectomy; genetic testing; metanephrines 2–6 weeks after surgery then yearly.
- **Prognosis:** sporadic tumors usually cured; malignant, extra-adrenal or SDHB tumors recur in 10–20% → lifelong surveillance.

### Pituitary

#### 13. Prolactinoma (29F, prolactin 200 ng/mL)

- **Clues:** irregular cycles and breast pain 6 months; no headache or visual symptoms; no drugs; not pregnant; no hypothyroid, CKD, CLD, PCOS.
- **Workup:** prolactin (repeat, macroprolactin if asymptomatic, dilute if large mass — hook effect); exclude secondary causes (pregnancy test β-hCG, TSH / FT4, renal profile, liver profile, drug review); other pituitary hormones (IGF-1, LH / FSH, estradiol, cortisol); imaging (pituitary MRI); visual fields if macroadenoma.
- **Results:** prolactin 200 ng/mL (≈ 4,240 mIU/L; > 200 suggests prolactinoma); TSH 2.3 mIU/L, FT4 17 pmol/L (1.3 ng/dL) normal; pregnancy test negative; MRI 9 mm pituitary microadenoma.
- **Causes of high prolactin:** prolactinoma, physiological (pregnancy, nursing, stress, sleep, seizure), hypothyroidism, CKD, liver disease, PCOS, chest wall trauma, drugs, stalk compression, idiopathic.
- **Treat if:** symptomatic microprolactinoma or any macroprolactinoma; no treatment for asymptomatic micro.
- **Goals:** normalize prolactin, shrink tumor, restore gonadal function / fertility.
- **Drugs:** cabergoline first-line (normalizes prolactin and shrinks tumor in ≈ 90%); bromocriptine (preferred if pregnancy planned); side effects = nausea, postural hypotension, nasal stuffiness, depression, Raynaud, constipation, valvulopathy at high cabergoline doses (echo if > 2 mg/week).
- **Follow-up:** prolactin from 1 month after starting; MRI at 1 year (3 months if macroadenoma, rising prolactin or new symptoms); visual fields for macroadenoma.

#### 14. Sheehan syndrome / hypopituitarism (40F, G3P3, fatigue and amenorrhea)

- **Clues:** PPH needing transfusion 5 years ago; amenorrhea and failure to lactate since; fatigue, postural dizziness, cold intolerance, weight gain, low libido; BP 95/60 with postural drop, puffy face, loss of body hair, slow-relaxing reflexes.
- **Workup:** CBC; electrolytes (Na, K); glucose (fasting); pituitary axes (TSH + FT4; 8 AM cortisol + ACTH; prolactin; IGF-1; LH, FSH, estradiol; β-hCG); bone (vitamin D, DXA); imaging (pituitary MRI).
- **Results:** Hb 11 g/dL (110 g/L; normocytic); fasting glucose 75 mg/dL (4.2 mmol/L); Na 130 mmol/L (130 mEq/L); K 4.5 mmol/L; vitamin D 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L; low); TSH 0.3 mIU/L with FT4 6 pmol/L (≈ 0.47 ng/dL) = central hypothyroidism; cortisol 2 µg/dL (≈ 55 nmol/L) with ACTH 15 pg/mL (3.3 pmol/L) inappropriately normal; IGF-1 60 ng/mL (low); prolactin 20 ng/mL (normal); LH 3 IU/L, FSH 4 IU/L, estradiol 25 pg/mL (≈ 92 pmol/L) all low; MRI partially empty sella; DXA low bone mass.
- **Diagnosis:** panhypopituitarism from Sheehan syndrome (PPH history + hormone deficiencies + MRI).
- **Other causes:** tumors (adenoma, craniopharyngioma, meningioma, metastases), infiltrative / inflammatory (hypophysitis, sarcoid), surgery, radiation, trauma, apoplexy, opiates, checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Management:** hydrocortisone first, then levothyroxine (1.6 mcg/kg) to avoid adrenal crisis; combined estrogen / progesterone; calcium and vitamin D; endocrinology, gynecology, fertility.
- **Examiner Qs:** axes lost first = GH > LH / FSH > TSH > ACTH; long-term = osteoporosis, infertility, CVD risk, adrenal crisis.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Hydrocortisone before thyroxine:** in any combined adrenal + thyroid deficiency, to prevent adrenal crisis.
- **Sick-day rules:** double glucocorticoid dose with illness, IM hydrocortisone if vomiting, medical alert bracelet.
- **Pregnancy and Graves:** PTU in 1st trimester, methimazole later, no RAI.
- **Alpha before beta:** in pheochromocytoma never start a beta blocker first.
- **Thyroid nodule pathway:** TSH → US → FNA by US risk features (scan only if TSH low).
- **DKA vs HHS:** DKA = ketones > 3, pH < 7.3, HCO3 < 18, high AG; HHS = glucose > 600 mg/dL, osmolality > 320, little ketosis, older patient, days of onset.
