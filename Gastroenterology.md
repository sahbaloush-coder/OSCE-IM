# Gastroenterology & Hepatology

## How to use this

The 20 GI stations cluster around five presentations: jaundice / abnormal LFTs, chronic diarrhea (IBD vs celiac), GI bleeding, upper abdominal pain, and dysphagia. The abdominal exam is examined on its own three times, and the ascites exam once.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; in bleeding or confused patients secure ABC, two large-bore lines and vitals with postural BP first.
- **Stations covered:** jaundice (cholangitis, acetaminophen ALF, PBC, chronic HBV, hemochromatosis, cirrhotic ascites); diarrhea (Crohn's ×3, UC ×2, celiac ×4 incl. abdominal exam); bleeding (massive UGIB from PUD ×2); pain (hypertriglyceridemic pancreatitis); cancer (esophageal cancer with breaking bad news, right colon cancer).

## 1. Core GI history (every case)

- **Presenting complaint:** onset, duration, course (intermittent, continuous, progressive), severity, previous similar episodes.
- **Pain:** SOCRATES; relation to food; radiation to back (pancreas) or shoulder (biliary).
- **Upper GI:** nausea, vomiting (content, blood, coffee-ground), heartburn, dyspepsia, dysphagia, odynophagia, early satiety.
- **Lower GI:** bowel habit change, diarrhea, constipation, blood / mucus, melena, tenesmus, incontinence.
- **Liver:** jaundice, dark urine, pale stool, pruritus, abdominal distension, leg swelling, confusion, bleeding / bruising.
- **Constitutional:** fever, weight loss (how much, how fast), anorexia, night sweats, fatigue.
- **Extraintestinal:** mouth ulcers, red eyes, joint / back pain, skin rash (erythema nodosum, pyoderma, dermatitis herpetiformis).
- **Past history:** GERD, PUD, gallstones, IBD, celiac, CLD, DM, autoimmune disease, radiation, previous abdominal surgery / ERCP / cholecystectomy, blood transfusion.
- **Drugs:** NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants, steroids, bisphosphonates, PPI, antibiotics, metformin, iron, laxatives, hepatotoxins (acetaminophen, anti-TB, antiepileptics, methotrexate, statins, amiodarone, herbals).
- **Social:** alcohol (units, CAGE), smoking, IV drugs, tattoos, sexual history, travel, sick contacts, eating outside, diet, occupation.
- **Family:** colon cancer, polyps, IBD, celiac, viral hepatitis, Wilson, hemochromatosis, GI malignancy.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Jaundice / abnormal LFTs

- **Pattern:** prehepatic (hemolysis — anemia, dark urine, SCD, thalassemia, Gilbert) vs hepatocellular vs cholestatic (pruritus, pale stool, dark urine).
- **Biliary / cholangitis:** RUQ pain, fever with rigors (Charcot triad), vomiting, gallstones, previous ERCP.
- **Viral hepatitis risk:** transfusion, IV drugs, tattoos, dialysis, unprotected sex, needle-stick, family member with HBV, travel, vaccination status.
- **Drug / toxin:** acetaminophen overdose (time, amount, number of tablets), depression or suicidal intent, herbal remedies, mushrooms, ecstasy.
- **Autoimmune:** arthralgia, rash, vitiligo, other autoimmune disease (RA, thyroid, Sjögren, celiac); IBD (PSC).
- **Metabolic / genetic:** obesity, DM, dyslipidemia (NAFLD); family history of cirrhosis (hemochromatosis — skin darkening, DM, arthropathy of 2nd–3rd MCPs, impotence); Wilson (young, neuro / psychiatric).
- **Decompensation:** distension, edema, GI bleed, confusion, sleep reversal.
- **Other:** pregnancy, HF (congestive hepatopathy), immunosuppression / HIV.

### B. Chronic diarrhea

- **Type:** watery (large volume), fatty (bulky, greasy, hard to flush), inflammatory (blood, mucus, small volume, fever, tenesmus).
- **Red flags:** nocturnal diarrhea, blood, weight loss, anemia, age > 50, family history of CRC / IBD.
- **Triggers:** gluten, dairy, fasting response (osmotic stops with fasting), stress.
- **IBD clues:** RLQ pain, perianal pain / discharge / fistula, mouth ulcers, back pain, arthritis, red eyes, skin rash, smoking (Crohn).
- **Celiac clues:** bloating, weight loss, iron deficiency, itchy blistering rash on knees / elbows / scalp, other autoimmune disease.
- **Infection:** travel, sick contacts, recent antibiotics (C. diff), amoebiasis, TB contact, HIV risk.
- **Other causes:** thyroid, DM, radiation, bowel surgery, chronic pancreatitis, microscopic colitis, bile acid diarrhea after cholecystectomy, drugs.

### C. GI bleeding

- **Upper vs lower:** hematemesis / coffee-ground / melena vs fresh blood / maroon stool (maroon + shock can still be upper).
- **Amount and effect:** number of episodes, volume, dizziness, syncope, confusion.
- **Causes:** NSAIDs, aspirin, steroids, H. pylori, alcohol / CLD (varices), retching (Mallory-Weiss), weight loss (malignancy), anticoagulants, bleeding disorder.
- **Exclude:** ENT or mouth bleeding swallowed.

### D. Upper abdominal pain / pancreatitis

- **Pain:** sudden epigastric pain radiating to back, relieved leaning forward; vomiting.
- **Causes:** gallstones, alcohol (binge, CAGE), hypertriglyceridemia (DM, obesity, family history), recent ERCP, drugs (azathioprine, valproate, thiazides, furosemide, 5-ASA, metronidazole, tetracycline), trauma, hypercalcemia.
- **Mimics:** inferior MI, perforated ulcer, dissection, DKA, cholangitis.

### E. Dysphagia

- **Level:** oropharyngeal (initiating, choking, nasal regurgitation) vs esophageal (food sticks after swallowing).
- **Pattern:** solids then liquids = mechanical (cancer, stricture); solids and liquids from start = motility (achalasia, scleroderma).
- **Progression:** progressive vs intermittent (ring, EoE); food impaction.
- **Associated:** weight loss, heartburn, odynophagia, hematemesis, regurgitation, chest pain.
- **Risk factors:** smoking, alcohol, long GERD (Barrett), corrosive ingestion, atopy (EoE), stroke, scleroderma, pill esophagitis (NSAIDs, bisphosphonates, KCl, doxycycline).

## 3. Abdominal examination (examined as its own station ×3)

- **Preparation:** wash hands; introduce; explain; consent; privacy; expose nipples to symphysis; lie flat with one pillow; ask about pain.
- **General:** look, mental state, BMI, vitals.
- **Hands:** clubbing, leukonychia, koilonychia, palmar erythema, Dupuytren, arthropathy, flapping tremor, bruising, scratch marks, wasting.
- **Face:** jaundice, pallor, Kayser-Fleischer rings, parotid enlargement; mouth for fetor, angular stomatitis, glossitis, ulcers, thrush, leukoplakia.
- **Neck and chest:** cervical nodes (Virchow node); spider nevi, gynecomastia, loss of body hair.
- **Inspection:** shape, distension, scars, fistulae, stoma, striae, pigmentation, dilated veins (caput medusae), visible peristalsis, umbilicus, pulsation, hernia on coughing, divarication.
- **Palpation:** light then deep in 9 regions; liver and spleen from right iliac fossa (roll to right for spleen); kidneys bimanually and ballotted.
- **Percussion:** liver span; Traube space; shifting dullness; fluid thrill.
- **Auscultation:** bowel sounds; rubs over organs; venous hum; renal bruits.
- **Complete by mentioning:** hernial orifices, genitalia, rectal exam, leg edema, neuro exam for alcohol, thyroid; thank patient, cover, wash hands, present findings.

## 4. Exam add-ons by diagnosis

- **Chronic liver disease:** jaundice, clubbing, leukonychia, palmar erythema, Dupuytren, spider nevi, gynecomastia, testicular atrophy, parotids, caput medusae, splenomegaly, ascites, edema, asterixis, fetor.
- **Acute liver failure:** GCS and encephalopathy grade, asterixis, rigidity, clonus / hyporeflexia, papilledema, tender hepatomegaly, bruising, petechiae, drug rash, herpetic lesions.
- **Cholangitis / cholecystitis:** fever, jaundice, RUQ tenderness, Murphy sign, peritonism, hypotension.
- **IBD:** low BMI, pallor, mouth ulcers, uveitis / episcleritis, clubbing, erythema nodosum, pyoderma, arthritis, spinal stiffness (AS), RLQ mass, perianal fistula / skin tags / abscess on PR.
- **Celiac:** low BMI, pallor, dermatitis herpetiformis (scalp, knees, elbows), angular stomatitis, glossitis.
- **GI bleeding:** postural drop, tachycardia, capillary refill, pallor, epigastric tenderness, PR for melena / fresh blood, CLD stigmata.
- **Pancreatitis:** epigastric tenderness, Cullen / Grey-Turner bruising, jaundice, eruptive xanthomas (high TG), dehydration.
- **Dysphagia / cancer:** cachexia, Virchow node, hepatomegaly, mouth, neuro exam.
- **Hemochromatosis:** bronze pigmentation (face, forearms), 2nd–3rd MCP arthropathy, hepatosplenomegaly, testicular atrophy, cardiomyopathy signs.
- **Ascites (own station):** inspect distension, flanks, umbilicus, veins; fluid thrill; shifting dullness; liver and spleen; JVP and heart (cardiac cause); lung bases (effusion); leg and sacral edema; abdominal girth; peritonism if SBP suspected.

## 5. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### Jaundice and liver

#### 1. Ascending cholangitis (48F, RUQ pain and jaundice)

- **Clues:** 1 week jaundice, fever, rigors, recurrent RUQ pain; T 38.5, HR 110, BP 102/58; Murphy positive; BMI 32.
- **DDx:** choledocholithiasis, cholangitis, cholecystitis, viral hepatitis, PUD / perforation, PBC, PSC, IgG4 cholangitis, liver abscess, pyelonephritis.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential); renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile (ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, total / direct bilirubin, albumin); coagulation (PT / INR, PTT); pancreatic (amylase, lipase); lactate; hemolysis screen; infection (blood and urine cultures, viral hepatitis serology, HIV); imaging (US abdomen, then MRCP or ERCP).
- **Results:** WBC 19 ×10⁹/L (19,000/µL); Hb 12 g/dL (120 g/L); platelets 460 ×10⁹/L; creatinine 96 µmol/L (1.1 mg/dL); urea 5 mmol/L (BUN 14 mg/dL); Na 139, K 3.8 mmol/L; ALT 250, AST 170, ALP 420 IU/L (cholestatic > hepatocellular); total bilirubin 80 µmol/L (4.7 mg/dL), direct 70 µmol/L (4.1 mg/dL); INR 2.3; PTT 30 s; US distended gallbladder with multiple stones and dilated duct.
- **Diagnosis:** ascending cholangitis (Charcot triad: fever, jaundice, RUQ pain).
- **Management:** ICU / monitored bed; IV fluids; IV piperacillin-tazobactam; correct coagulopathy (vitamin K, FFP); GI for urgent ERCP; surgical consult (cholecystectomy later).

#### 2. Acetaminophen-induced acute liver failure (24F, depression)

- **Clues:** found jaundiced, confused, vomiting; 4 empty strips of acetaminophen; GCS 13, grade III encephalopathy, rigidity, hyporeflexia; tender hepatomegaly; BP 98/52.
- **DDx:** DILI, viral (HAV, HBV, HSV), autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson, Budd-Chiari / ischemic, mushroom, acute fatty liver of pregnancy, cholangitis.
- **Workup:** toxicology (acetaminophen level at ≥ 4 h, salicylate, alcohol, urine drug screen); CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, PO4, glucose); liver profile (ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin, albumin); coagulation (PT / INR); blood gas + lactate; acute liver failure screen (viral serology and PCR — HAV, HBV, HEV, HSV, CMV, EBV; autoimmune — ANA, ASMA, LKM1, IgG; ceruloplasmin; pregnancy test); cultures; imaging (Doppler US liver, CT head if encephalopathic).
- **Results:** WBC 8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 14 g/dL (140 g/L); platelets 225 ×10⁹/L; creatinine 240 µmol/L (2.7 mg/dL); urea 12 mmol/L (BUN 34 mg/dL); Na 139, K 3.8 mmol/L; pH 7.25; lactate 3.2 mmol/L (29 mg/dL); ALT 2,100, AST 1,700, ALP 1,400 IU/L; total bilirubin 70 µmol/L (4.1 mg/dL), direct 50 µmol/L (2.9 mg/dL); INR 3.2; PTT 35 s; acetaminophen 250 mcg/mL (250 mg/L ≈ 1,650 µmol/L); HAV IgM negative, HBsAg negative, anti-HBs 86 mIU/mL (immune).
- **Tool:** Rumack-Matthew nomogram.
- **Management:** ICU; intubate for grade III–IV encephalopathy; IV N-acetylcysteine (150 mg/kg over 1 h, 50 mg/kg over 4 h, 100 mg/kg over 16 h); fluids and vasopressors to MAP ≥ 75; cerebral edema measures (head up, mannitol, hypertonic saline, ICP monitor); transplant center referral; psychiatry.

#### 3. Primary biliary cholangitis with Child A cirrhosis (45F, RA on methotrexate)

- **Clues:** 3 months jaundice, itch, fatigue, dark urine; RA, hypothyroid, dyslipidemia; xanthelasma, scratch marks, ulnar deviation.
- **DDx:** DILI (methotrexate, statin), PBC, PSC, choledocholithiasis, cholangiocarcinoma, pancreatic cancer, viral or autoimmune hepatitis, hemolysis.
- **Workup:** liver profile (ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin); coagulation (INR); CBC (platelets); renal profile; lipid profile; hemolysis screen; viral serology (HBV, HCV); autoimmune liver panel (AMA ± sp100 / gp210, ANA, ASMA, LKM1, IgG / IgM); other (TTG-IgA, ceruloplasmin, ferritin, TSH); imaging (US → MRCP; FibroScan; biopsy only if AMA negative or overlap suspected).
- **Results:** WBC 8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 14 g/dL; platelets 190 ×10⁹/L; creatinine 90 µmol/L (1.0 mg/dL); urea 6 mmol/L (BUN 17 mg/dL); ALT 80, AST 95, ALP 780 U/L (> 1.5–2× ULN); total bilirubin 50 µmol/L (2.9 mg/dL), direct 30 µmol/L (1.8 mg/dL); INR 1.2; albumin 40 g/L (4.0 g/dL); cholesterol 300 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L); LDL 130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L); triglycerides 100 mg/dL (1.1 mmol/L); AMA positive.
- **Management:** hepatology; ursodeoxycholic acid 13–15 mg/kg/day; add obeticholic acid or fibrates if poor response; pruritus = cholestyramine, antihistamine, then naltrexone, rifampicin; Ca / vitamin D and bone density; EGD for varices; US ± AFP every 6 months for HCC; HAV / HBV vaccination; transplant for decompensation, HCC or refractory itch.

#### 4. Chronic hepatitis B (30M, raised ALT)

- **Clues:** HBsAg positive for > 6 months, anti-HBs < 10, raised ALT / AST, no risk factors.
- **Workup:** HBV serology (HBsAg — repeat at 6 months, anti-HBs, anti-HBc IgM / IgG, HBeAg / anti-HBe); HBV DNA (PCR); liver profile (ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin); coagulation (INR); CBC (platelets); co-infection screen (anti-HCV, anti-HDV, HIV, HAV IgG for vaccination); HCC surveillance (AFP + US); fibrosis (FibroScan, APRI / FIB-4).
- **Results:** HBsAg positive; anti-HBs < 10 IU/L; ALT and AST elevated → chronic hepatitis B; treat if HBV DNA > 2,000 IU/mL with ALT > ULN, or significant fibrosis (FibroScan > 7 kPa, APRI > 0.5), or cirrhosis.
- **Treat if:** HBV DNA > 2000 IU/mL with ALT above normal; fibrosis ≥ F2 (FibroScan > 7 kPa, APRI > 0.5); cirrhosis; or regardless with HIV / HDV / HCV coinfection, family history of HCC, immunosuppression, extrahepatic disease, pregnancy with high viral load.
- **Treatment:** entecavir or tenofovir; GI clinic; screen and vaccinate family.
- **Goals:** undetectable DNA, HBsAg loss, normal ALT.
- **HCC surveillance:** cirrhosis, advanced fibrosis, family history of HCC, men > 40 / women > 50 from endemic areas; US every 6 months ± AFP.

#### 5. Hereditary hemochromatosis (43M, right hand pain)

- **Clues:** 2nd–3rd MCP pain and stiffness, fatigue, weight loss, new DM; father and uncles died of liver disease; bronze pigmentation; hepatosplenomegaly.
- **Workup:** CBC; liver profile (ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin, albumin); renal profile; coagulation (INR); iron studies (serum iron, TIBC, transferrin saturation, ferritin); HFE genotype (C282Y / H63D); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); arthritis screen (RF, anti-CCP, ANA); viral hepatitis serology; ceruloplasmin; metabolic (glucose / HbA1c); imaging (US liver ± MRI iron quantification; echo; hand X-ray).
- **Results:** WBC 6.2 ×10⁹/L; Hb 15 g/dL (150 g/L); platelets 210 ×10⁹/L; creatinine 88 µmol/L (1.0 mg/dL); urea 5 mmol/L (BUN 14 mg/dL); ALT 92, AST 78, ALP 140 IU/L; total bilirubin 28 µmol/L (1.6 mg/dL); HBsAg and HCV negative; serum iron 210 µg/dL (≈ 37.6 µmol/L; normal 60–170); TIBC 240 µg/dL (≈ 43 µmol/L; low); transferrin saturation 88% (> 45%); ferritin 1,200 ng/mL (= µg/L); ceruloplasmin 25 mg/dL (normal).
- **Liver biopsy:** if ferritin > 1000 µg/L or raised enzymes (fibrosis risk).
- **Management:** hepatology; genetic counseling (HFE), screen first-degree relatives and spouse; phlebotomy 1 unit weekly until ferritin 20–50, Hct < 35%, saturation < 50%; then every 2–3 months.
- **Phlebotomy effects:** improves fatigue, pigmentation, cardiac function, enzymes and survival (if before cirrhosis); does not reverse arthropathy, cirrhosis, DM or testicular atrophy.

#### 6. Ascites from decompensated cirrhosis — ascites exam (55M, CLD)

- **Clues:** 2 months distension, early satiety, ankle swelling; fluid thrill, shifting dullness, mild hepatomegaly, palmar erythema.
- **DDx:** cirrhosis, cardiac failure, nephrotic syndrome, peritoneal carcinomatosis.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, eGFR, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile (ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin, total protein); coagulation (INR); diagnostic paracentesis (cell count with neutrophils, albumin for SAAG, total protein, Gram stain and culture in blood-culture bottles, cytology, ± amylase / ADA); cause of cirrhosis (viral serology, ferritin, autoimmune, ceruloplasmin); imaging (US with Doppler, echo if cardiac).
- **Results:** AST / ALT elevated, albumin low; ascitic fluid SAAG > 1.1 g/dL (11 g/L) with protein < 2.5 g/dL (25 g/L), negative cytology and culture → portal hypertension from cirrhosis; shrunken nodular liver on US.
- **Management:** salt restriction; spironolactone ± furosemide; daily weight and girth; large-volume paracentesis (with albumin) if tense; treat cause, stop alcohol; vaccinate; transplant evaluation; watch for SBP and encephalopathy.

### Chronic diarrhea

#### 7. Crohn's disease, ileocecal (36M, RLQ mass)

- **Clues:** 2 months watery diarrhea, nocturnal, occasional blood; RLQ pain after eating; low back pain; 5 kg weight loss; smoker; BMI 19, pallor, RLQ 5 cm mass.
- **DDx:** Crohn's, infection (TB, amoeba), celiac, bile acid malabsorption, chronic pancreatitis, microscopic colitis, drugs, CRC / lymphoma.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; electrolytes; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, fecal calprotectin); stool (O&P, C. diff toxin, culture, occult blood, osmotic gap); infection screen (QuantiFERON / PPD, HIV); celiac serology (TTG-IgA + total IgA); TSH; nutrition (iron studies, B12, folate, vitamin D, albumin); imaging (CT / MR enterography); colonoscopy with ileal intubation and biopsy.
- **Results:** WBC 12 ×10⁹/L; Hb 10 g/dL (100 g/L); MCV 65 fL (microcytic); platelets 400 ×10⁹/L; renal, LFT, electrolytes normal; CRP 9 mg/L (high); stool O&P and C. diff negative.
- **Management:** admit; IV fluids; DVT prophylaxis; antibiotics if abscess; GI and surgical consult; steroids; azathioprine; biologics (infliximab, adalimumab); surgery.

#### 8. Crohn's with ileopsoas abscess (26M)

- **Clues:** 6 months intermittent bloody diarrhea, 10 kg loss, arthralgia; RLQ pain, fever 38, BP 90/50, RIF mass.
- **Workup:** CBC (with differential); renal profile; electrolytes; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, fecal calprotectin); stool (O&P, C. diff toxin, culture); infection screen (QuantiFERON before biologics, HIV, hepatitis B); iron studies; imaging (CT abdomen and pelvis); colonoscopy with biopsy.
- **Results:** WBC 14 ×10⁹/L with 85% neutrophils; Hb 9 g/dL (90 g/L); MCV 65 fL (microcytic — iron deficiency); platelets 400 ×10⁹/L; LFT, renal, electrolytes normal; CRP 20 mg/L; ESR 75 mm/h; stool O&P and C. diff negative; fecal calprotectin > 1,000 µg/g (< 100); QuantiFERON pending; CT terminal ileum fistula to the right psoas with abscess; colonoscopy skip lesions, terminal ileal ulcers; histology cryptitis, crypt abscesses.
- **DDx:** Crohn's, ileocecal TB, diverticulitis, appendicular abscess, lymphoma.
- **Acute management:** admit; GI and surgery; IV fluids and bowel rest; ciprofloxacin + metronidazole; drain the abscess.
- **Long term:** steroids for flares; azathioprine / 6-MP; biologics (infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab, ustekinumab); surgery.
- **Resection indications:** obstruction, perforation, abscess, hemorrhage, severe perianal disease, dysplasia / cancer, refractory disease, growth failure in children.
- **Associations:** AS, uveitis / episcleritis, PSC, arthritis, erythema nodosum, pyoderma.

#### 9. Fistulizing perianal Crohn's (20M)

- **Clues:** 2 years nocturnal watery diarrhea not settling with fasting, 10 kg loss; 6 months perianal pain with pus soiling; red patches on shins; smoker; BMI 17, pale, fistula opening on PR, erythema nodosum.
- **Workup:** CBC; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP, fecal calprotectin); renal profile + electrolytes; liver profile; nutrition (iron studies, B12, folate, vitamin D, albumin); stool culture; infection screen before biologics (QuantiFERON / TB PCR on biopsy, hepatitis B, HIV); endoscopy (ileocolonoscopy with biopsy); imaging (CT / MR enterography; MRI pelvis for fistula; EUA).
- **Results:** WBC 8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 9.1 g/dL (91 g/L; microcytic hypochromic) with low ferritin; vitamin D low; B12 normal; ileal biopsy chronic inflammation, TB PCR negative; CT ileal thickening with comb sign; MRI pelvis complex perianal fistula without abscess.
- **Before anti-TNF:** hepatitis B serology, QuantiFERON / PPD, CXR.
- **Management:** education; stop smoking; infliximab + azathioprine; colorectal surgery for fistula; vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, HPV); IV iron; vitamin D.
- **Anti-TNF contraindications:** active infection, latent TB, demyelinating disease, NYHA III–IV heart failure.
- **EIMs independent of activity:** ankylosing spondylitis, uveitis, PSC; activity-related = erythema nodosum, peripheral arthritis, aphthous ulcers, episcleritis.

#### 10. Acute severe ulcerative colitis (24M)

- **Clues:** 3 months diarrhea now 8 bloody motions / day; LLQ cramps; back pain; quit smoking a year ago; pallor, LLQ tenderness, anal tenderness, fresh blood PR.
- **DDx:** IBD, C. diff, invasive bacteria (TB, Yersinia), amoeba, CMV / HSV, STD / HIV, radiation colitis, CRC / lymphoma.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile (creatinine, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K, Mg); liver profile + albumin; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, fecal calprotectin); stool (C. diff toxin, O&P, culture); infection screen (TB, HIV, hepatitis B, CMV on biopsy); imaging (abdominal X-ray for toxic megacolon ≥ 6 cm); flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsy.
- **Results:** WBC 12 ×10⁹/L; Hb 8 g/dL (80 g/L); MCV 70 fL; platelets 480 ×10⁹/L; LFT, renal, electrolytes normal; CRP 42 mg/L; ESR 54 mm/h; stool O&P and C. diff negative; fecal calprotectin 1,800 µg/g (< 100) → Truelove-Witts severe (≥ 6 bloody stools + Hb < 10.5 g/dL, ESR > 30).
- **Management:** admit; GI; IV fluids; DVT prophylaxis; exclude C. diff and toxic megacolon (AXR); IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day.
- **No response by day 3:** surgical consult; repeat sigmoidoscopy; rescue IV infliximab or cyclosporine, or colectomy.
- **Still failing 5 days after infliximab (CRP 48, guarding):** urgent colectomy.

#### 11. Mild–moderate UC after treated amoebiasis (24M)

- **Clues:** 6 weeks small-volume bloody stools 4–5 / day, tenesmus; improved after tinidazole then recurred; LLQ tenderness, painful DRE with blood.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile + electrolytes; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, fecal calprotectin); stool (O&P, C. diff toxin, culture, amoebic antigen / serology); colonoscopy with biopsy.
- **Results:** WBC 12 ×10⁹/L; Hb 14 g/dL; MCV 83 fL; platelets 480 ×10⁹/L; LFT, renal, electrolytes normal; CRP 12 mg/L; ESR 14 mm/h; colonoscopy left-sided colitis; histology cryptitis, crypt abscesses, architectural distortion.
- **Diagnosis:** active IBD (mild–moderate UC) on colonoscopy and histology.
- **Management:** GI referral; oral + rectal 5-ASA; budesonide MMX 9 mg or prednisolone 40–60 mg if 5-ASA fails; escalate to infliximab + immunomodulator, adalimumab, vedolizumab, ustekinumab or tofacitinib if needed; stop smoking; dietitian; low-fiber diet while active.

#### 12. Celiac disease with dermatitis herpetiformis (21F)

- **Clues:** 2 months watery diarrhea, epigastric pain, heartburn, 7 kg loss; rash on scalp and knees; labeled IBS-D; BMI 18.
- **DDx:** Crohn's, infection (TB, Giardia), celiac, bile acid diarrhea, Whipple, eosinophilic gastroenteritis, HIV enteropathy, gastrinoma, carcinoid, chronic pancreatitis, microscopic colitis, lymphoma.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile + electrolytes; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, fecal calprotectin); TSH; celiac serology (TTG-IgA with total IgA ± EMA); nutrition (iron studies, folate, B12, vitamin D, Ca, albumin); stool (O&P, osmotic gap); infection screen (TB, HIV); endoscopy (EGD with ≥ 4 duodenal biopsies; colonoscopy if needed).
- **Results:** WBC 8 ×10⁹/L; Hb 12.3 g/dL (123 g/L); MCV 82 fL; platelets 225 ×10⁹/L; ALT 48, AST 38, ALP 180 U/L (mildly raised); renal and electrolytes normal; CRP 4 mg/L (normal); stool O&P and C. diff negative; TTG-IgA positive.
- **Management:** EGD with duodenal biopsies; dietitian for strict gluten-free diet (wheat, barley, rye); follow-up at 6 and 12 months then yearly; check iron, folate, B12, vitamins A / D / E, zinc, copper; pneumococcal vaccine; bone density; dermatology (dapsone for DH).
- **Associations:** T1DM, thyroiditis, Addison, RA, Sjögren, autoimmune hepatitis, PBC, PSC, Down syndrome, DH, IgA deficiency.
- **Genetics:** HLA-DQ2 (90–95%) or DQ8; absence of both rules out celiac.
- **Histology:** increased intraepithelial lymphocytes (≥ 25 / 100), crypt hyperplasia, villous atrophy.

#### 13. Abdominal exam station → chronic diarrhea (25M, 6 months; three stations)

- **Task:** full abdominal exam as in section 3.
- **DDx:** IBS, IBD, celiac disease.
- **Tests:** CBC; TFT; celiac serology (TTG-IgA + total IgA); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP, fecal calprotectin); stool (occult blood, O&P); endoscopy with biopsy.
- **Examiner Qs:** celiac histology = villous atrophy, crypt hyperplasia, intraepithelial lymphocytes; skin association = dermatitis herpetiformis.

### GI bleeding

#### 14. Massive upper GI bleed from PUD with shock (43M)

- **Clues:** three hematemeses, drowsy, tachypneic; after 2 L fluid still HR 134, BP 70/45, SpO2 88%; epigastric tenderness, melena / blood on PR.
- **DDx:** PUD, varices, Mallory-Weiss, tumor, erosive gastritis / esophagitis, Dieulafoy / vascular lesions, swallowed blood.
- **Workup:** blood bank (group and crossmatch, massive transfusion protocol); CBC; coagulation (PT / INR, PTT, fibrinogen); blood gas + lactate; renal profile (creatinine, urea / BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile + albumin; glucose; cardiac (ECG, troponin); hepatitis screen; imaging (erect CXR; US / CT if perforation or varices suspected); urgent endoscopy within 24 h (within 12 h if unstable).

#### 15. UGIB from PUD presenting as maroon rectal bleeding (45M)

- **Clues:** maroon PR bleeding × 3 with dizziness; aspirin + ibuprofen; treated H. pylori; BP 90/55, HR 120; epigastric tenderness, fresh blood PR.

### Pain, dysphagia and cancer

#### 16. Acute pancreatitis from hypertriglyceridemia (42M, DM on SGLT2i)

- **Clues:** abrupt epigastric pain to back, vomiting; DM on metformin, linagliptin, empagliflozin; BMI 32; no alcohol.
- **DDx:** pancreatitis, PUD / perforation, cholangitis, cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis, inferior MI, dissection, hepatitis, DKA (euglycemic with SGLT2i), adrenal crisis.

#### 17. Esophageal cancer with breaking bad news (65M, dysphagia and weight loss)

- **Clues:** 3 months progressive dysphagia, solids then liquids, 10 kg loss, GERD.
- **DDx:** esophageal cancer, achalasia, Chagas, stricture, diverticulum.
- **Key test:** EGD with biopsy.
- **Breaking news:** introduce with role; right patient; consent and confidentiality; ask about relatives present; explore what he knows; check he wants results now; warning shot; simple language; silence; respond to emotion; invite questions; honest prognosis; referral to oncology / surgery; offer help telling family; contact details; check understanding.

#### 18. Right-sided colon cancer + family screening (46M)

- **Clues:** 6 years labeled IBS; fatigue, bloating, intermittent constipation; cachectic.
- **Workup:** CBC (Hb, MCV); iron studies; liver profile; renal profile; stool (FOBT / FIT); tumor marker (baseline CEA); colonoscopy with biopsy; staging (CT chest / abdomen / pelvis).
- **Results:** microcytic hypochromic anemia; occult blood positive → colonoscopy = exophytic fungating mass in right colon.
- **Next:** CT chest / abdomen / pelvis, baseline CEA, surgery and oncology referral.
- **Risk factors:** age, obesity, smoking, alcohol, red / processed meat, IBD, polyps, abdominal radiation, family history.
- **Hereditary syndromes:** Lynch (HNPCC), FAP, MUTYH-associated polyposis.
- **Staging:** limited to submucosa, node-negative, no metastasis = stage I, 5-year survival ≈ 90%.
- **Family screening:** wife at 45 (average risk); sons by colonoscopy at 36 (10 years before father's age at diagnosis) or 40, whichever earlier, every 5 years.
