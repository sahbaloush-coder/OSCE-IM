# Infectious Diseases

## How to use this

The 17 ID stations are dominated by four organisms that keep returning: TB (pleural, peritoneal, miliary / meningeal), Brucella (spine, sacroiliac, endocarditis), HIV (PCP, Kaposi, new diagnosis), and sepsis from a source. The marks reward an exposure history (travel, animals, raw milk, sex, sick contacts) more than in any other specialty.

- **Open every station:** introduce yourself; take permission; septic or confused patient = ABCDE, IV access, cultures before antibiotics.
- **Stations covered:** HIV (PCP ×3, Kaposi sarcoma, new HIV disclosure); TB (pleural effusion ×2 incl. pregnancy, peritonitis, miliary + meningitis); Brucella (spondylodiscitis, sacroiliitis, endocarditis with root abscess); others (brain abscess from septic prosthetic knee, Salmonella + epididymitis, dengue, gonococcal urethritis, urosepsis → septic shock).

## 1. Core ID history (every case)

- **Fever:** onset, duration, pattern (continuous, intermittent, nocturnal, relapsing), Tmax, rigors, response to antipyretics / antibiotics already given.
- **Constitutional:** night sweats, weight loss (how much, how fast), fatigue, anorexia.
- **Source hunt by system:** ENT / dental; chest (cough, sputum, hemoptysis, SOB, pleuritic pain); heart (palpitations, murmur history); GI (diarrhea, vomiting, pain, jaundice); GU (dysuria, flank / testicular pain, discharge, ulcers); CNS (headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, confusion, seizures, focal signs); MSK (back pain, joint swelling); skin (rash, ulcers, nodules).
- **Exposures:** sick contacts (TB — family, housekeeper, prison, work); travel (endemic areas, Hajj); animals (sheep, camels, cats); raw milk / undercooked meat; food eaten outside.
- **Sexual history:** partners (number, gender), protection, previous STDs; IV drugs; transfusions; tattoos.
- **Immune status:** HIV, DM, steroids / biologics / chemo, transplant, splenectomy, alcohol.
- **Past history:** prosthetic joints / valves, valve disease (rheumatic, AS), recent hospital admission or procedures, previous TB / brucellosis.
- **Drugs and allergies:** recent antibiotics (C. diff, partially treated infection), drug allergies, vaccines (BCG, HBV, pneumococcal, influenza).
- **Occupation and social:** farmer, healthcare, prison officer; smoking, alcohol; living situation.

## 2. History add-ons by presentation

### A. Fever + cough / dyspnea

- **TB:** chronic cough > 2–3 weeks, hemoptysis, sweats, weight loss, contact (prison, housekeeper from endemic country).
- **PCP (HIV):** subacute dry cough, exertional dyspnea, desaturation on walking, thrush, weight loss, high-risk sex, stopped ART.
- **Pleural effusion:** pleuritic chest pain, progressive SOB.
- **Other:** CAP (acute, productive), NTM, Kaposi (purple skin lesions), malignancy (smoker).

### B. Fever + back / joint pain

- **Brucellosis:** raw milk (camel, sheep, goat), animal contact, farmer, undulant fever, sweats, sacroiliac or lumbar pain, hepatosplenomegaly, epididymo-orchitis.
- **Spinal infection red flags:** weakness, numbness, bladder / bowel incontinence.
- **Endocarditis:** known valve disease (AS), poor dental hygiene, new murmur, emboli.
- **TB spine:** chronic back pain, gibbus, sweats.

### C. Fever + headache / confusion

- **Meningitis / encephalitis:** neck stiffness, photophobia, seizures, rash (meningococcus), Hajj travel.
- **Brain abscess:** headache, vomiting, blurred vision (papilledema), focal deficit, a source (septic joint, dental, ear, endocarditis).
- **TB meningitis:** weeks of headache with chronic cough, weight loss, cranial nerve palsies.
- **Dengue / viral hemorrhagic fever:** high fever, retro-orbital pain, bone pain ("break-bone"), rash, bleeding, travel (Najran, Jeddah, Makkah).

### D. Fever + diarrhea

- **Gastroenteritis:** food eaten outside, travel (Thailand), sick contacts, blood or mucus, vomiting.
- **C. diff:** recent antibiotics or admission.
- **Proctitis / STD:** anal sex, tenesmus, genital symptoms (epididymitis).

### E. Genital discharge / STD

- **Discharge:** onset, color, amount; dysuria; ulcers, vesicles, warts; testicular or anal pain.
- **Systemic:** fever, joint pain and red eyes (reactive arthritis), rash (secondary syphilis), oral ulcers.
- **Sexual history:** partners, protection, previous STDs, partner symptoms.

### F. HIV-related

- **HIV course:** date of diagnosis, ART regimen and adherence (reasons for stopping), last CD4 and viral load, prophylaxis (TMP-SMX).
- **Opportunistic infection screen:** thrush / odynophagia (Candida), skin lesions (Kaposi, zoster), vision loss (CMV retinitis, toxoplasma), headache and confusion (Cryptococcus, toxoplasma), diarrhea, cough (PCP, TB).

## 3. Core ID exam (every case)

- **Vitals:** temperature, HR, BP (shock), RR, SpO2 at rest and on walking, GCS.
- **General:** ill / toxic, cachexia, pallor, jaundice, hydration, lymph nodes (cervical, axillary, inguinal).
- **Mouth and ENT:** thrush, ulcers, dental hygiene / abscess, tonsils, ear discharge, sinuses.
- **Skin:** rash type (petechial, macular on palms / soles, vesicular), Kaposi lesions, IE stigmata (splinters, Osler, Janeway), injection marks, pressure sores.
- **Eyes:** conjunctival hemorrhage, Roth spots, retinitis, papilledema.
- **Heart:** new murmur, rub, heart failure.
- **Chest:** consolidation, effusion (dullness, reduced breath sounds), crackles.
- **Abdomen:** tenderness, hepatosplenomegaly, ascites, renal angle.
- **Genitals and anus:** discharge, ulcers, testicular swelling / tenderness, warts.
- **MSK:** spine tenderness, sacroiliac tenderness, joint effusions, prosthetic joint.
- **Neuro:** meningism, focal deficit, cranial nerves, mental status.

## 4. Exam add-ons by diagnosis

- **PCP / HIV:** thrush, desaturation on exertion with clear chest, lymphadenopathy, Kaposi lesions, retinitis.
- **TB effusion:** reduced expansion, stony dullness, reduced breath sounds, no tracheal shift (small–moderate).
- **Brucellosis:** fever, hepatosplenomegaly, sacroiliac / lumbar tenderness, epididymo-orchitis, new murmur (endocarditis).
- **Endocarditis:** murmur, splinters, Osler / Janeway, Roth spots, splenomegaly, heart block.
- **Brain abscess:** papilledema, focal deficit, drowsiness, source (joint, teeth, ears).
- **Dengue / hemorrhagic fever:** high fever, shock (BP 70/50), petechial rash, tourniquet test, bleeding, hepatomegaly.
- **Sepsis:** hypotension, tachycardia, tachypnea, low GCS, mottled skin, low urine output; look for source (renal angle, chest, skin, lines).
- **STD:** urethral discharge, genital ulcers, inguinal nodes, testicular tenderness, joints, eyes, rash.

## 5. Case scenarios

Each case: stem → clues that decide it → key results → diagnosis → management → examiner's extra questions.

### HIV

#### 1. PCP as the first presentation of HIV (34M; two stations)

- **Clues:** 3 weeks fever, dyspnea, dry cough, 5 kg loss; smoker; multiple male and female partners; T 38, SpO2 94%, oral thrush, clear chest; ± purple skin lesions (Kaposi).
- **DDx:** pneumonia (viral, bacterial, atypical), TB, NTM, PCP, Kaposi sarcoma.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; LDH; blood gas (PaO2, A-a gradient); infection (blood cultures; HIV 4th-generation Ag / Ab → viral load + CD4; β-D-glucan); induced sputum / BAL (bacterial culture, AFB smear + TB PCR, PCP immunofluorescence / GMS / PCR); imaging (CXR → CT chest if CXR normal); G6PD before dapsone / primaquine.
- **Results:** station 1 WBC 7 ×10⁹/L, Hb 11 g/dL (110 g/L), platelets 230 ×10⁹/L; station 2 WBC 3.4 ×10⁹/L, Hb 11 g/dL, platelets 150 ×10⁹/L; renal and LFT normal; PaO2 73 mmHg (9.7 kPa) on room air; LDH 460 U/L; blood culture no growth; HIV positive, viral load 100,000 copies/mL; sputum AFB negative, PCP immunofluorescence positive; CXR bilateral interstitial infiltrates.
- **Management:** admit; airborne isolation until TB excluded; TMP-SMX; fluconazole for thrush; CD4 and HIV genotype; start ART within 2 weeks; ID consult; counsel on diagnosis and adherence.

#### 2. PCP in HIV off ART for 3 months

- **Clues:** ART 3 years, CD4 220 last year; cough, fatigue, mild fever; desaturation on walking, clear exam.
- **Workup:** blood gas (PaO2, A-a gradient); LDH; β-D-glucan; CBC; renal and liver profile; HIV viral load + CD4 + resistance genotype; induced sputum / BAL (PCP stain / PCR, AFB, culture); imaging (CXR, CT chest).
- **Expected results:** low PaO2 (< 70 mmHg = 9.3 kPa) with wide A-a gradient (≥ 35 mmHg); high LDH; raised β-D-glucan.
- **CXR:** may be normal, bilateral interstitial infiltrates, pneumothorax.
- **Confirm:** induced sputum or BAL with silver (GMS) stain / immunofluorescence / PCR; CT chest (ground glass).
- **Organism:** Pneumocystis jirovecii (PJP).
- **Management:** airborne isolation until TB excluded; TMP-SMX first line; alternatives = TMP + dapsone, clindamycin + primaquine, atovaquone, IV pentamidine; delay ART about 2 weeks (until TB / cryptococcus excluded).
- **Add steroids if:** PaO2 < 70 mmHg on room air or A-a gradient ≥ 35 mmHg.
- **Pregnancy:** TMP-SMX is still preferred; TMP-dapsone is the alternative.

#### 3. AIDS-related Kaposi sarcoma (40M, fever and bleeding oral lesion)

- **Clues:** 6 weeks fever, 15 kg weight loss; painless bleeding oral lesion; painless purple nodules on legs for 6 months; transfusion in India 5 years ago; cat at home.
- **DDx:** Kaposi sarcoma, mucormycosis, angiosarcoma, bacillary angiomatosis (Bartonella).
- **Workup:** CBC; coagulation; HIV test + CD4 + viral load; tissue (biopsy of the oral lesion ± HHV-8 PCR / LANA stain, tissue culture); other infection (Bartonella serology — bacillary angiomatosis); extent (ENT / sinus endoscopy, upper endoscopy, CT / MRI sinuses, US of leg lesions, CXR).
- **Results:** Hb ≈ 10 g/dL (100 g/L; mild anemia); WBC, platelets and coagulation normal; CD4 ≈ 150 cells/µL; biopsy spindle-cell proliferation with slit-like vascular channels (HHV-8 positive); leg US subcutaneous hypoechoic vascular nodules; no nasal lesions.
- **Management:** symptom control (pain, bleeding, secondary infection); local therapy (cryotherapy, radiotherapy, intralesional chemo) for limited disease; systemic chemotherapy for extensive / visceral disease; start or optimize ART; ID, oncology, dermatology, ENT; follow CD4 and viral load.
- **ART classes:** NRTI (tenofovir), NNRTI (efavirenz), PI (darunavir), INSTI (dolutegravir).
- **High-risk groups for KS:** HIV with low CD4, transplant recipients, elderly Mediterranean / Eastern European men, sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Visceral sites:** lungs, GI tract, lymph nodes.
- **Mucormycosis:** Mucorales fungi; surgical debridement + liposomal amphotericin B, step down to posaconazole / isavuconazole.
- **Bacillary angiomatosis:** Bartonella; doxycycline, azithromycin or rifampicin.

#### 4. New HIV diagnosis — disclosure (34M, married)

- **Clues:** 6 kg loss over 4 months, night sweats, oral ulcers, flu-like illness 3 months ago, pneumonia 2 months ago; frequent traveler with unprotected sex; oral thrush.
- **Workup:** HIV (4th-generation Ag / Ab → confirmatory differentiation assay; CD4; viral load; genotype resistance); baseline labs (CBC, renal profile, liver profile, lipid profile, glucose / HbA1c, urinalysis); co-infections (HBV, HCV, syphilis, TB — IGRA, toxoplasma IgG, CMV); imaging (CXR); G6PD (before dapsone / primaquine); pregnancy test for partner.
- **Diagnosis:** HIV with advanced disease (AIDS) — CD4 < 200, high viral load, opportunistic infection.
- **Disclosure (SPIKES):** private setting; explore what he knows; warning shot; give result; silence; empathy; explain ART is effective; respect autonomy but stress duty to inform partner (and public health reporting); safe sex; don't share razors / toothbrushes; MDT (ID, psychiatry, social work); follow-up; contacts; check understanding.

### Tuberculosis

#### 5. Tuberculous pleural effusion (39M, prison officer)

- **Clues:** 3 weeks progressive SOB, dry cough, low-grade fever, right chest pain; sick contact at work; reduced expansion, stony dullness, absent breath sounds right base.
- **DDx:** pleural effusion (TB, parapneumonic, malignant), pneumonia, lung cancer, bronchiectasis.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); serum LDH + total protein (for Light's criteria); cardiac (troponin, pro-BNP); TB tests (PPD / IGRA, sputum AFB × 3); autoimmune profile; imaging (CXR, chest US, CT); diagnostic thoracentesis (appearance, cell count and differential, pH, glucose, protein, LDH, ADA, Gram stain / culture, AFB smear / culture, cytology); pleural biopsy if needed.
- **CXR:** right effusion with meniscus sign.
- **Pleural fluid:** cell count / differential, protein, LDH, glucose, pH, culture, cytology, ADA, AFB.
- **Results:** WBC 10.5 ×10⁹/L; ESR 95 mm/h; CRP 50 mg/L; serum LDH 185 U/L; serum protein 9 g/dL (90 g/L); sputum AFB negative; PPD / QuantiFERON positive; autoimmune negative; fluid yellow, turbid, > 70% lymphocytes, pH 7.27, glucose 65 mg/dL (3.6 mmol/L), protein 6.5 g/dL (65 g/L; ratio 0.72 > 0.5), LDH 130 U/L (ratio 0.7 > 0.6), culture negative, cytology negative, ADA ≈ 80 U/L (> 40) → lymphocytic exudate = TB pleuritis.
- **Diagnosis:** tuberculous pleural effusion.
- **Management:** baseline LFT, renal, eye exam; RIPE (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) for 2 months then rifampicin + isoniazid for 4 months; chest drain if large or complicated; pulmonary and ID referral.

#### 6. TB pleuritis in pregnancy (27F, 12 weeks)

- **Clues:** 4 weeks left pleuritic chest pain, mild SOB and dry cough; housekeeper from Uganda with chronic cough.
- **DDx:** TB, viral pleuritis, pneumonia with effusion / abscess, PE, malignancy.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR); PE screen (leg Doppler, D-dimer); TB tests (IGRA, induced sputum AFB + TB PCR); autoimmune and viral screen (ANA, RF, anticardiolipin, TSH, HBV, HCV, HIV, RPR); imaging (shielded CXR, echo); thoracentesis (cell count, protein, LDH, glucose, ADA, AFB smear / culture).
- **Results:** WBC 11 ×10⁹/L; Hb 11.2 g/dL (112 g/L); Hct 33.9%; platelets 434 ×10⁹/L; renal and LFT normal; Doppler and D-dimer negative; small left effusion; CRP 87 mg/L; ESR 62 mm/h; IGRA positive; sputum AFB and PCR negative; screen negative; echo normal; fluid 650 mL, WBC 1,000 with 80% lymphocytes, LDH 400 U/L, protein 5.4 g/dL (54 g/L), glucose 66 mg/dL (3.7 mmol/L), ADA 50 U/L; fluid AFB / PCR negative.
- **Management:** admit, airborne isolation; treat as active TB with isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol for 6 months (pyridoxine with INH); ID and obstetrics.

#### 7. Miliary TB with TB meningitis (44M)

- **Clues:** 1 month headache with neck pain, photophobia, back pain; fever; > 20 kg weight loss; 4 months blood-tinged cough; heavy smoker; bronchial breathing, crackles, cervical and axillary nodes.
- **DDx:** TB, lung cancer, lymphoma, CTD.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile (baseline before RIPE); electrolytes (Na — SIADH); HIV; blood cultures (+ mycobacterial); induced sputum / BAL (AFB smear, GeneXpert, culture); imaging (CXR, MRI brain with contrast before LP); LP (opening pressure, cell count, protein, glucose with paired serum glucose, AFB smear, GeneXpert, culture, ADA).
- **Results:** WBC 12 ×10⁹/L; Hb 11 g/dL (110 g/L); platelets 200 ×10⁹/L; renal and LFT normal; CXR miliary pattern; blood culture no growth; AFB smear positive in induced sputum and CSF.
- **Management:** admit; airborne isolation; ID; HIV testing; RIPE then RI for 9–12 months for CNS TB; add dexamethasone for TB meningitis.

#### 8. Tuberculous peritonitis (35M)

- **Clues:** 3 months abdominal distension, diffuse pain, nocturnal fever 38.5, sweats, 10 kg loss; pale; ascites without CLD.
- **Workup:** CBC; liver profile + albumin; renal profile; coagulation (INR); inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); blood cultures; HIV; imaging (CT abdomen / pelvis, CXR); paracentesis (cell count and differential, albumin for SAAG, total protein, amylase, cytology, Gram stain and culture, AFB smear / culture, ADA, TB PCR); laparoscopic peritoneal biopsy if needed.
- **Results:** Hb 9 g/dL (90 g/L); MCV 65 fL (microcytic); CRP 32 mg/L; ESR 70 mm/h; LFT and renal normal; ascites straw-colored, WBC 500 cells/mm³ mostly lymphocytes, fluid albumin 5.5 g/dL vs serum 3.5 g/dL (SAAG < 1.1 g/dL), fluid protein 4 g/dL (40 g/L; > 2.5), cytology, Gram, AFB smear and culture negative → high ADA expected (> 39 U/L).
- **DDx:** TB or bacterial peritonitis, peritoneal carcinomatosis / lymphoma, pancreatitis, nephrotic syndrome.
- **Confirm:** peritoneal biopsy (laparoscopy) → caseating granulomas, AFB, TB PCR positive.
- **Management:** ID; RIPE for 8 weeks then INH + RIF to at least 6 months.
- **Counseling on TB drugs:** adherence (resistance); orange secretions (RIF); hepatotoxicity (INH, RIF, PZA); vision changes (EMB); neuropathy (INH — give pyridoxine); gout (PZA); drug-induced lupus (INH); rash and drug interactions incl. OCP (RIF).

### Brucellosis

#### 9. Brucella spondylodiscitis (65M, Sudan, camel milk)

- **Clues:** 3 weeks intermittent nocturnal fever; worsening chronic back pain; warmth on medial thigh; DM, HTN, aortic stenosis; farmer drinking camel milk; poor dental hygiene; ejection murmur; lumbar tenderness.
- **DDx:** brucellosis, infective endocarditis, malaria, spinal infection (osteomyelitis / discitis, epidural abscess).
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); infection (≥ 2 blood cultures held 3–4 weeks, urine culture, Brucella SAT / Coombs titer, malaria film, TB tests); imaging (CXR, TTE, MRI spine with contrast); CT-guided biopsy (culture, TB PCR, histology) if cultures negative.
- **Results:** WBC 11 ×10⁹/L; Hb 11.2 g/dL (112 g/L); Hct 33.9%; platelets 400 ×10⁹/L; renal and LFT normal; ESR 45 mm/h; blood and urine cultures negative; Brucella titer 1:2560 (≥ 1:160 significant); malaria film negative; CXR clear; TTE no vegetation; MRI L4–5 discitis without abscess.
- **Management:** admit; streptomycin or gentamicin + doxycycline ± rifampicin; at least 3 months for spondylitis; ID.

#### 10. Brucella sacroiliitis (40M, raw milk)

- **Clues:** 3 weeks fever and back pain; raw milk; palpable liver; right sacroiliac tenderness; no neuro deficit.
- **DDx:** brucellosis, TB, lymphoma, ankylosing spondylitis.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); infection (blood cultures, Brucella titer, HIV, PPD / IGRA); rheumatology (HLA-B27); imaging (MRI sacroiliac joints, CXR, abdominal US, echo); bone / joint biopsy (bacterial culture, TB PCR / culture) if titer negative.
- **Results:** Brucella titer 1:1280; mildly raised LFT; echo normal; MRI right sacroiliitis.
- **Confirm:** blood or tissue culture / PCR.
- **Management:** combination of 2–3 drugs (aminoglycoside / doxycycline / rifampicin; alternatives FQ, TMP-SMX, ceftriaxone) for 12 weeks for focal disease; ID; education (boil milk, cook meat, avoid animal contact).
- **Pregnancy < 36 weeks:** non-focal = rifampicin + TMP-SMX; focal = ceftriaxone + rifampicin + TMP-SMX.
- **Pregnancy ≥ 36 weeks:** rifampicin alone (non-focal) or ceftriaxone + rifampicin (focal) until delivery (TMP-SMX risks kernicterus); then full combination; delay breastfeeding until treatment ends.

#### 11. Brucella endocarditis with aortic root abscess (30M, farm)

- **Clues:** 3 months fever, sweats, back and joint pain; exertional dyspnea and orthopnea; sheep contact and raw milk; macular rash on palms and soles; grade 3/6 diastolic murmur (AR); spleen 2 cm.
- **Workup:** CBC; inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP); renal profile + urinalysis; liver profile; infection (≥ 3 blood cultures — hold 3–4 weeks, urine / sputum cultures, Brucella titer); cardiac (troponin, ECG, CXR, TTE → TEE); imaging (CT chest / abdomen / pelvis for emboli, splenic abscess).
- **Results:** gram-negative coccobacilli after 3 weeks; first-degree AV block with LBBB; para-aortic collection.
- **Diagnosis:** Brucella endocarditis complicated by aortic root abscess (new conduction block = root abscess).
- **Management:** ID; cardiothoracic surgery (valve replacement / drainage); gentamicin or streptomycin + doxycycline 100 mg BID + rifampicin 600–900 mg daily for at least 6 months.
- **Complications:** heart block, arrhythmia, heart failure, emboli (stroke, limb), splenic infarct / abscess, death.

### Other infections

#### 12. Brain abscess from septic prosthetic knee (68M)

- **Clues:** 7 days fever and frontal headache, vomiting, blurred vision; recent septic arthritis of left knee prosthesis, refused removal; drowsy, T 38.9, BP 102/60, papilledema, swollen tender knee.
- **DDx:** brain abscess, meningitis, septic arthritis / osteomyelitis, brain tumor, TB.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR); infection (3 sets of blood cultures, urine and stool culture, AFB, joint aspiration — cell count, Gram stain, culture); cardiac (echo for endocarditis); imaging (CXR, contrast CT / MRI brain — no LP with papilledema or mass).
- **Results:** WBC 19 ×10⁹/L (19,000/µL); renal and LFT normal; urine, stool and AFB negative; S. aureus in blood and joint cultures; joint fluid WBC 100,000 cells/µL; echo and CXR normal; CT left frontal ring-enhancing abscess.
- **Management:** stabilize, IV fluids; neurosurgery (aspiration / drainage); orthopedics (washout / prosthesis removal); IV vancomycin + ceftriaxone + metronidazole, narrowed to culture; 6–8 weeks.
- **Complications:** meningitis, ventriculitis / rupture into ventricle, herniation, venous sinus thrombosis, seizures.

#### 13. Salmonella gastroenteritis with epididymitis (20M, back from Thailand)

- **Clues:** 5 days non-bloody diarrhea, fever 38.6, vomiting, cramps, headache; ate out; left testicular pain; unprotected sex 4 months ago; testicular swelling and tenderness.
- **DDx:** bacterial or viral gastroenteritis, C. diff, STD-related proctocolitis, liver abscess, GU infection / STD, IBD.
- **Workup:** CBC; renal profile; liver profile; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR); stool (culture, O&P, C. diff toxin); blood and urine cultures; sexual health (NAAT for gonorrhea / chlamydia, syphilis, HIV); imaging (abdominal US, testicular US with Doppler).
- **Results:** WBC 12 ×10⁹/L; Hb 11.2 g/dL; Hct 33.9%; platelets 400 ×10⁹/L; renal and LFT normal; CRP 35 mg/L; HIV and STD screen (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis) negative; no ova / parasites; stool culture Salmonella; C. diff negative; blood and urine cultures negative; abdominal US normal; testicular US increased epididymal vascularity = epididymitis, no abscess.
- **Management:** admit; fluids (IV or ORS); ceftriaxone (alternatives ciprofloxacin, TMP-SMX); urology; ID / GI if no improvement.

#### 14. Dengue with shock (38F, from Najran, back from Hajj)

- **Clues:** 2 days fever 40, severe headache with retro-orbital pain, bone pain; BP 70/50, HR 120, RR 26, SpO2 92%; petechial rash; no meningism.
- **DDx:** viral hemorrhagic fever (dengue, Alkhurma), meningococcal disease, viral meningitis, measles.
- **Workup:** CBC with serial hematocrit and platelets; inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR); infection (≥ 2 blood cultures, malaria film, dengue NS1 antigen / RT-PCR + IgM / IgG, other viral serology); renal profile + electrolytes; liver profile; coagulation; blood gas + lactate; imaging (CXR, US for plasma leakage — effusions, ascites); ECG.
- **Results:** WBC 14 ×10⁹/L; Hb 9 g/dL (90 g/L); hematocrit rise > 20% (plasma leakage); platelets 25 ×10⁹/L; AKI; acute liver injury (AST > ALT); ESR 300 (as given); blood culture negative; CXR clear; dengue RT-PCR positive.
- **Management:** critical care; ABC; careful fluid resuscitation with hematocrit monitoring; correct electrolytes; blood products for bleeding; avoid NSAIDs / aspirin; acetaminophen only; supportive care.

#### 15. Gonococcal urethritis (32M)

- **Clues:** urethral discharge and itch; check ulcers, joints, eyes, rash, partners.
- **Workup:** urethral swab Gram stain (point of care); NAAT for gonorrhea and chlamydia (first-catch urine); urinalysis + urine culture; sexual health screen (HIV, syphilis, HBV, HCV); CBC.
- **Results:** gram-negative intracellular diplococci; chlamydia NAAT unavailable; HIV negative.
- **Management:** ceftriaxone (gonorrhea) + doxycycline or azithromycin (chlamydia); abstain / condoms; treat partners; notify public health; repeat HIV test in 6–12 weeks.
- **If ceftriaxone allergy:** gentamicin + azithromycin (alternatives high-dose azithromycin, FQ if susceptible).
- **Causes of urethritis:** N. gonorrhoeae, C. trachomatis, M. genitalium; also Trichomonas and HSV.

#### 16. Urosepsis → septic shock (70M, DM, IHD, Alzheimer, HR 44)

- **Clues:** 2 days fatigue and dizziness, bradycardia; then dysuria, right flank pain, fever, GCS drops to 11.
- **DDx:** BPPV / stroke; MI, arrhythmia, orthostasis; PE; anemia; infection (UTI, pneumonia, bed sore, meningitis); hypo / hyperglycemia; drugs.
- **ER approach:** ABCDE, IV access, ECG monitor; IV fluids; empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics after cultures.
- **Workup:** blood gas + lactate; CBC; coagulation; inflammatory markers (CRP, procalcitonin); renal profile (creatinine, BUN); electrolytes (Na, K); liver profile; glucose; urine (urinalysis, culture); blood cultures × 2 before antibiotics; cardiac (ECG, troponin — HR 44); imaging (CXR, CT brain if GCS drop, renal US / CT for obstruction).
- **Results:** leukocytosis, high procalcitonin and lactate; perinephric fat stranding → acute pyelonephritis with sepsis (SOFA ≥ 2).
- **Then:** BP 80/50, anuric, raised LFT / RFT = septic shock → ICU; fluids; vasopressors (norepinephrine) to MAP ≥ 65; escalate antibiotics; ID.
- **Culture:** ESBL Klebsiella → continue meropenem 10–14 days; stop vancomycin.
- **Response markers:** MAP ≥ 65, falling lactate, urine output.

### Questions asked across several stations

- **Airborne isolation:** any suspected pulmonary TB until 3 negative sputa; also before starting PCP treatment in HIV.
- **RIPE regimen:** 2 months RIPE + 4 months RI (pleural, peritoneal); 9–12 months + steroids for CNS TB; pyridoxine with INH.
- **Pleural / ascitic TB clue:** lymphocytic exudate with high ADA (> 40).
- **Brucella regimen:** doxycycline + rifampicin ± aminoglycoside; 6 weeks uncomplicated, ≥ 12 weeks spine, ≥ 6 months endocarditis.
- **HIV and ART timing:** start within 2 weeks for most OIs; delay for cryptococcal and TB meningitis.
- **Sepsis bundle:** cultures, lactate, broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 h, 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension, vasopressors for MAP < 65.
